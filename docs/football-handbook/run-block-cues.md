# Run-block cues: combinations, departures and edge reads

This is a compact companion to [Trenches](trenches.md) and
[Moving run-game footwork](moving-run-footwork.md), researched on 2026-10-28.
**Teaching** below means inspected public coaching prose. **Translation**
means our proposed observation or measurement, not the author's exact rule,
an implemented feature, or an NFL-wide standard. No clinic video was audited.

## Name the installation before grading it

The name alone does not establish the target, leaving member or release time.

| Term | Documented example | What must be specified for a snap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ace** | Campbell: center–guard working a DL to a linebacker [1]. Wilmesherr reserves Ace for a backside-LB destination and calls his playside-LB center–guard combination **Jack** [2]. | Which guard, which DL, which backer/area, and the response to movement. |
| **Deuce** | Campbell: guard–tackle [1]. Wilmesherr's Deuce works toward a backside LB; **Queen** is his playside-LB equivalent [2]. | The pair and destination; neither member is the permanent climber just because he was initially uncovered. |
| **TAG** | Glazier's Christensen summary: tackle down-blocks the DT; guard folds around him to the playside LB [3]. | A fold installation, not necessarily two hands-on blockers before a climb. Do not silently substitute **Trey** (tackle–TE in [1]) or assume every staff uses TAG this way. |
| **Overtake** | Penree's outside-zone takeover: the uncovered lineman runs laterally to the defender's playside shoulder/number and executes the reach [4]. | A changing body/contact relationship, not another universal pair name or a guaranteed completed seal. |

For annotation, record the pair, first-level threat, backer responsibility,
run direction and technique family. These are descriptions of the existing
call, not a proposal for another assignment system. A formation named Ace or
a coverage named Deuce says nothing about these blocking calls.

## Cue entries

### Deuce: guard–tackle double to a backer

**Teaching [1, 2].** Establish the first-level fit together while watching
the assigned second-level defender. Wilmesherr distinguishes a linebacker
running through, scraping over the top, or hanging; the response changes with
that movement. If he hangs, his installation continues vertical work rather
than releasing by elapsed time.

**Translation.** At the local double, capture the blocker's acquired backer
track and the DL's near surface before the departure request. Then measure
who actually retains contact, whether the departing player clears the bodies,
and whether he intercepts the backer before the runner needs that path. A
release event is not a delivered second-level block. Keep a hanging-backer or
DL-splitting-the-pair case as the opposing check; do not force a climb to make
the count larger.

### Ace: center–guard, with a movement-dependent leaving member

**Teaching [6].** In Janak's odd-front Duo example, the playside guard and
center work the nose to the Will. If the nose slants weak, the center takes
him and the guard climbs. If the nose works or shades strong, the guard keeps
him and the center climbs. This belongs to that installation; an Ace label
alone does not assign the Will in every offense.

**Translation.** Near the nose, record his observed path relative to both
blockers and which member can retain a useful fit. The departing member's
identity may change without changing the backer obligation. Compare opposite
nose movement with the same pair. Do not use the defender's private slant
assignment, a fixed center/guard priority, or knowledge of his future escape.

### TAG fold: a planned route around a down block

**Teaching [3].** The tackle goes down on the DT while the guard pulls around
to the playside linebacker. The published summary distinguishes a skip pull
for a tight, vertical insertion from an open pull for a wider perimeter path.
It does not prescribe waiting for a double-team release.

**Translation.** The guard can start his installed pull at the snap; actual
clearance around his moving tackle constrains execution. Measure his route
behind that body, turn-up location, acquired LB approach and useful arrival.
The tackle's down block must account for the DT throughout the fold. A DT
penetrating that wall is an opposing case, not permission to award a seal or
send the guard through his teammate. A fold and an overtake should not share
an assumed requirement for a sustained two-player contact interval.

### Outside-zone overtake: acquire the surface before calling it secured

**Teaching [4].** Penree's takeover player runs laterally toward the DL's
playside shoulder/number. In the complementary reach–stab–climb technique,
the covered player briefly checks the defender so his partner can reach him,
then works to the second level. These are coordinated techniques, not an
instruction to keep the original covered blocker on the DL forever.

**Translation.** Around the moving DL, distinguish attempted takeover from
achieved partner position/contact and the original blocker's departure.
Useful evidence includes the partner's acquired chest/shoulder relationship;
actual contact and load can separately audit the physical result. Being
nearby or appearing in a combo list does not establish either. Check a
successful takeover against a widening DL the helper cannot reach. Neither
case earns an automatic hook, prescribed release second or guaranteed gain.

### Climb timing: three teaching families, not one stopwatch

| Teaching | Visible/local trigger and action | Translation and opposing check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sustained vertical combo [2]** | After fitting the DL, read the assigned LB's run-through, scrape or hang; continue the double when he hangs. | Time the acquired commitment before the release request. A scrape is not interchangeable with a downhill fill. |
| **Brief inside-zone help [5, 9]** | Grabowski teaches a quick uncovered-player punch and departure when the LB threatens; he permits more Level-1 work if the LB is not a threat. Kuchar's open-gap example climbs when the near DL hip moves away/upfield, but helps if it slants into that gap. | Record the near-gap threat and backer cue independently. Keep the slant/run-through control; do not require a long double before every climb. |
| **Duo fit/scrape/slant [6]** | Janak's tackle leaves for a Mike filling B gap, stays on the double when Mike scrapes, and takes an inside-slanting DT while the TE climbs. | Record DL movement as well as LB movement. The same LB motion need not produce the same leaving member after the DL changes the available fit. |

These triggers need the player's own acquired view. Local contact can inform
him that a partner has left—Janak explicitly teaches a change to drive/finish
when that is felt [6]. It does not disclose an unseen linebacker's exact
position or the runner's true route. Our requirement to inspect surviving
keeper contact/support is a physical and information constraint, not a
published coaching force threshold or a claim that a player can sense his
teammate's complete balance state.

### Reach steps: respond to the defender's movement

**Teaching [4].** Penree starts the reach with playside depth and width toward
the playside shoulder. His second step differs if the DL stays even versus
widens: recover the base without crossing over in the former case, or cross
and run with the widening defender. His uncovered reach–check–climb player
keys the near knee: movement toward him calls for reach work; movement away
permits the climb. This is one college teaching table, not a universal ban or
requirement for crossover steps.

**Translation.** From initial approach through contact, measure width/depth
gained, relative defender travel, requested versus achieved turn, and supported
arrival at the intended surface. A defender who cannot be hooked can still
be stretched. Center-only tracking cannot validate a literal knee read or
foot sequence; film is needed, or a clearly labeled center-motion proxy.
Keep the even and widening defenders distinct rather than tuning one first
step until both end at the desired spot.

### Kick-out versus log: the read and the resulting block are different

**Teaching [7].** The published Texas/Gatewood account uses a kick-out intent
through squeeze and wrong-arm. After clearing the center, the puller reads
the edge's movement: closing down the tackle's hip, squeezing along the line,
or charging upfield at the mesh. With a tight pull and tight wrong-arm, the
reported technique lets contact turn into a log. A mesh charge instead calls
for an adjusted angle and near-shoulder hand contact.

**Teaching [8].** Kirby's account of Sam Parker's Ferris State Counter uses
an initial trap intention; if the guard cannot get sufficiently inside for
the kick, he runs his feet to take the defender vertically or wash him away
from the back and second puller. That is a different teaching description,
not evidence that every wrong-arm must trigger a pre-contact mode switch.

**Translation.** Once the edge is actually acquired, record his depth and
motion relative to the down-blocking tackle, the puller's attainable inside
angle, first contact and achieved displacement. Then inspect the wrapper/back
route against the resulting space. A kick label does not prove outside
displacement, and a log label does not prove an outside lane. Preserve a
legitimate squeeze/spill win and the alternative mesh charge. Teaching intent
cannot guarantee that contact produces either block.

## Make a cue measurable without making it omniscient

For one selected clip, annotate **cue available → request → achieved contact
or departure → useful next contact**. Keep analyst-visible truth separate
from the player's delayed picture; do not call a correct ground-truth read
an acquired one. Event-relative timing is more useful here than a universal
“climb by 1.0 seconds” target. Occluded cues can remain uncertain.

NFL tracking can constrain pair separation, relative travel, departure and
arrival times. It cannot establish gaze, calls, exact knees/hands, transmitted
load or why a coach assigned that backer. Torso orientation is not gaze; a
proximity-based “double” or “climb” is an operator's classification, not proof
of the installed obligation. In simulation, measure actual load separately
from proximity and from protection of the runner's path.

Pick only the relevant cue and one opposing case under
[the testing-cost policy](../TESTING_COSTS.md). This page adds no mandatory
test matrix. It does not certify the legality of old clinic contact advice;
the applicable NFL rules still govern the simulated technique.
