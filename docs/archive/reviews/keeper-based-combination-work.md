# OL combinations: who can stay, who can find the next job

## Status and underlying rule

This increment follows [zone surface acquisition](zone-reach-and-displacement.md)
and the [run-technique teaching review](offensive-line-run-technique.md).
It repairs a shared combination rule rather than adding a stronger blocker or
a separate force law for each run concept.

A combination is a shared responsibility, not a requirement that both players
always provide equally useful contact. Before someone climbs, ask whether the
player remaining can carry the first-level job. A good double can develop into
a good solo block and a useful second-level approach even if its departing
member never had a strong fit. The remaining blocker still has to execute the
solo work through his own support, hands and physical capability.

This rule applies to the existing combinations installed by zone, Duo and gap
calls. Their targets, surfaces, displacement goals and reserved puller work
remain distinct. It does not make every run use the same assignments.

## Reproduced defect

The saved IZ Over/right/seed3 C/LG combination targets DT_W. LG is the actual
primary even though C was installed as the covered owner. LG carries a real
loaded hand joint, sound leverage and upright support. C's helper geometry is
poor enough to transmit little or no useful force. The old reader rejects the
whole combination before looking at LB_W because every helper must have
`helperSupport >= .25`.

At 1.05 s LG's paired hand normal is about 895.4 kg·yd/s², its player-wide arm
impulse is 44.9 kg·yd/s, control .455 and leverage -.022 quarter-turns. C's
maximum geometric support is zero. The near backer is not read at all. Duo
Odd/right has the same veto with a different primary/helper arrangement.

Across the 12 prior fixed films, 61 of 115 attached-combination reads have a
conservatively secure primary yet fail that helper-geometry test; 60 consume
no backer image. These repeated diagnostic reads do not measure an NFL error
frequency, nor do they automatically certify that leaving was safe.

## Small shared repair

The existing secure-primary control/base/leverage checks and short stability
dwell remain. The backer and runner still enter through the player's consumed,
delayed picture. The reader then considers who stays and who climbs:

- The keeper must have his own upright support, above the same existing .60
  availability threshold, and an attainable current contact angle.
- His real hand joint must exist in World and connect his body to the actual
  down defender. A relationship label cannot supply that contact.
- If a helper becomes keeper, he must have his own established useful helper
  contact. A departing helper's poor fit no longer vetoes a sound primary.
- The existing responsibility/backer cost selects between legal transfers.
  It can select the installed owner to climb when his partner actually owns
  the block. Installation labels are not physical ownership.

The helper eligibility check still uses the existing `helperSupport` function,
including its functional-reach check. The retained primary is not asked to
reacquire hands simply because an extended existing grip exceeds fresh-strike
range. Extended helper promotion remains a separate boundary case; this patch
does not broaden the helper force law.

When an actual helper becomes primary, his existing helper hand joint becomes
the primary joint. Ordinary synchronization removes the departing member's
hands and updates technique commands; it does not invent new contact or reset
hand history. The acquired surface, control, start and contact-report state
persist. A cloth grab made by a departing primary is released; the new keeper
does not inherit that illegal hold. Previously recorded penalties remain.

Bodies, velocities, support, physical profiles, force/work budgets and World
resolution are unchanged by the transfer decision. The next ordinary solve
determines whether the keeper can actually sustain the block and whether the
climber can navigate traffic and meet the linebacker.

## What universal OL logic should mean

The common decision process is responsibility-first:

1. Account for the installed first-level threat and protected surface.
2. Use achievable movement to establish the appropriate actual contact.
3. Read defender movement and the designated next-level threat while working.
4. Keep, assist or transfer according to the remaining blocker's real fit.
5. Recover from a lost fit or classify genuinely new work before approaching it.

Scheme detail supplies the intended surface, direction and responsibility.
The shared physical model supplies movement, support, contact and transmitted
load. This repair improves step four; it does not claim all five are finished.

Existing runBlock/handTechnique ratings affect precision and sustain;
awareness/playRecognition affect delayed vision and decision cadence;
consistency/discipline/composure affect coherent execution error and attention.
Good players should apply the same valid rules more promptly and precisely,
while their bodies retain finite capabilities. `Player.familiarity` currently
means position comfort, not knowledge of this call. Per-call training has not
been invented as a strength or success scalar.

## Validation and next work

Compare unchanged inputs across eight concepts and five fronts. Keep actual
hand identity/load, keeper support and achieved climb movement separate from
release labels and final yards. Retain weak-primary, unsupported-helper,
missing-contact and delayed-backer controls. Preserve all adverse plays.

The next distinct improvements are brief check/post/climb before an uncovered
player establishes sustained contact, independent member attention, lane-aware
work pickup after stretch movement, reserved kick/trap responsibility, and run
footwork's current dependence on passBlock. Each needs its own diagnosed cue
and executed football test. They should not be hidden by stronger traction or
more detailed anatomy.

## Executed evidence and limits

The [saved measurements and commands](../../../data/reviews/physics-world/combo-transfer/README.md)
retain 160 ordinary matched inputs: eight concepts, five fronts, both directions
and seeds 3/11, with one generated 31/32 roster pair. Two older 41/42-roster
witnesses are separately labelled. All 324 observed/plain executions match;
all 162 inputs end naturally. These are diagnostics, not an NFL distribution.

| Ordinary panel | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Combination climbs | 68 | 118 |
| Keeper's same hand object after controller and solve | 65/68 | 118/118 |
| Keeper's live positively loaded paired hands after solve | 68/68 | 118/118 |
| Mean run gain, yd | 1.307 | 1.389 |
| Stuffs, gain ≤ 0 yd | 31 | 37 |
| Runs of 10+ yd | 1 | 1 |
| OL falls | 12 | 13 |

The new handovers all retain genuine supported contact. Of the 118 keepers,
102 have recorded OL delivered loads and 16 are tight ends with positively
loaded native joints; the OL-only recording does not supply those TEs' arm
impulses. Joint force, player-wide arm impulse and assignment labels remain
separate observations. The ordinary IZ witness releases C at 1.05 s with LG
staying; Duo releases RG at 1.25 s with C staying. Existing control/frame and
the keeper's physical hands survive both transitions. In the two real helper
promotions, support availability is 1 and helperSupport exceeds .96.

Seven ordinary pairs gain at least two yards; one loses at least two. The
largest ordinary loss is Duo Odd/right/seed3, 7.39→2.70 yd. Nine previously
positive runs become stuffs, while three old stuffs clear. The separate OZ
Over/left/seed11 witness changes from a 70-yd touchdown to a 28.49-yd tackle.
The evidence therefore supports a coherent shared transition, not an overall
rushing-efficiency improvement or a guaranteed successful climb.

Two of the 118 transfer events change the climber's target again during the
same controller update: Power Odd/left/S3 LT and Trap Bear/right/S3 RT. Their
keeper Y retains genuine hands, but the old work rules immediately replace the
event's linebacker target. Count these as contact-preserving transfers, not
completed second-level blocks. Independent member attention, target ownership
and reclassification still need work. Stored engagement control is also an
approximation during promotion; preserving real hands does not validate every
mental/technique scalar attached to the old engagement.

The Duo loss exposes the next concrete shared job defect. At 1.25 s C keeps
sound NT contact while nearly ineffective RG leaves. At 2.20 s RT's
`readRules` exchanges DT_S for RG's LB_M. The exchange swaps targets without
reclassifying jobs: RG still uses `climb` against DT_S, and RT keeps his old
first-level technique against LB_M. DT_S contacts the runner at 2.55 s,
earlier than the baseline SS contact at 3.15 s. This is an adjacent
`block_exchange`, not a `block_collect`. Ordinary `findWork` already calls
`classifyWork`; the exchange path bypasses it. Prioritize coherent job
classification at every actual target transition, with this adverse input as
a fixed executed test. Do not restore redundant help to hide the later defect.

The separate 144-run, three-roster guard changes mean gain 1.696→1.789 yd,
stuffs 33→36 and 10+ runs 2→3. All 64 isolated tackle rows are exact. The two
full-game guards have no physical timeouts or budget excess, but football
paths diverge and scoring rises substantially (45–27/23–11 becomes
53–50/52–28). Those are retained warnings about football behavior, not
evidence of better league realism or a measured isolated passing effect.

Native momentum/work residuals stay at numerical roundoff and traction,
arm-work and support-motor limits remain intact. No shared physics module, capability
mapping or solver resolution changes. The 17 new tests verify both keeper
roles, missing/unsupported/insecure contact, delayed pictures, actual climb
movement, holding ownership and recognition sensitivity. The pinned baseline
fails ten new-feature cases while passing seven controls. Higher recognition
consumes the same changing linebacker cue sooner; it does not guarantee yards.
