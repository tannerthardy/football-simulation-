# Rushing film review

This pack shows **current engine simulation film**, with 24 selected plays and a separate library of all **302 clean back runs** from the eight-game benchmark. It is not NFL video. The selected clips help us compare useful contrasts; the full library helps check whether an issue happens regularly or only in the highlighted plays.

## Open the film room

1. Download and unzip the film-room archive.
2. Open **`rushing-review.html`** in a browser for the 24 selected clips. Start with the six below.
3. Open **`all-runs.html`** to browse all 302 clean runs. Both pages contain their replay data, so they work locally after extraction.

Expand **Formation, game, team & search**, then search an exact play ID, such as **`G5-P053`**. The viewer can group plays by offensive concept, defensive front, coverage, formation or game. Use those groups to watch several ordinary examples of the same call before judging a technique from one unusually good or bad result.

Pause around the timestamps below, then play at **0.5× or 0.25×**. Times are seconds after the snap. Use the viewer's **Copy reference** control to send the play and timestamp with your observation. **Export clip** saves a short **WebM** video you can share.

These IDs use the native game-log index. Some older generic exports added one to their displayed play numbers; use the IDs in this pack when reporting feedback.

## Start with these six

**1. G1-P059 — Inside Zone, +8.18 yards.** A useful ordinary successful run against 4-3 Over / 2-Man. Pause at **1.40s** for the handoff, **2.05s** for the recorded RG climb, and **3.15s** for the linebacker wrap. Watch the guards' first steps and the RB's path. Does the runner enter space created by the blockers? Does the RG leave his initial contact with a plausible route to useful work? This gives us a baseline before watching the failures.

**2. G5-P053 — Counter, −4.13 yards.** Against 4-3 Over / 2-Man. The handoff is at **1.30s**, the weak-side end wraps the runner at **1.60s**, and the runner falls at **1.85s**. Rewind before the wrap and follow the pullers instead of the ball. What is the first visible obstruction? Is the path blocked, the defender penetrating, the runner arriving early, or something else? The goal is to identify where it goes wrong, rather than infer the cause from the final loss.

**3. G2-P021 — Counter, +21.33 yards.** Against 4-3 Under / Cover 1. This is the sample's only clean Counter reaching our success threshold. Watch **1.30s** for the handoff and compare the early development with G5-P053. The first recorded carrier contact is much later, at **4.85s**; that hold slips at **5.15s**, and another defender wraps at **5.55s**. What opens the initial lane? Separately, does the tackle sequence after the successful blocking look believable?

**4. G1-P077 — Power, +87.20-yard touchdown.** Against 4-3 Under / Cover 3 Match. The handoff is at **1.35s** and the first carrier contact at **3.05s**. A linebacker wraps at **3.25s**, the safety assists at **3.50s**, and the recorded holds slip at **3.60s** and **3.80s**. Concentrate on that short sequence before watching the long touchdown run. Do the approach, contact and continued motion fit together? Where does a plausible ordinary gain become a breakaway?

**5. G2-P136 — Outside Zone, +84.60-yard touchdown.** Against 4-3 Over / Cover 1 Robber. Pause at the **1.35s** handoff, then the recorded missed tackles at **2.85s** and **3.35s**. This play has **no recorded carrier-contact event**. Follow the approaching defenders before each miss: could they reach the runner from those speeds and angles? Do they brake appropriately, and does the runner's change of direction look attainable? Compare with the ordinary Outside Zone runs below.

**6. G3-P099 — Duo, +2.07-yard goal-line touchdown.** Against 4-3 Under / Cover 0 with a blitz. Watch **1.40s** for the handoff, **1.75s** for the recorded center climb, and **2.75–2.80s** for the wrap and touchdown. Focus on the initial block geometry and the last small amount of forward movement. Does the runner cross the line in a way consistent with the visible bodies and contact?

## What to send back

A useful note identifies **play ID, timestamp, player and visible behavior**. For example: `G5-P053 @ 1.60s — DE_W #59 / RB #25 — [what looks wrong]`. Distinguish “this path looks impossible” from “I think his assignment should be different”; both are useful, but they point to different fixes.

These light replays show **paths, facing and recorded events**, rather than recorded eyes, jobs or forces. Facing alone does not establish where a player was looking. Event notes help locate interactions but do not prove that the football technique was correct. After finding a concern, search the same concept in **`all-runs.html`** and check several ordinary clips before deciding how common it is.
