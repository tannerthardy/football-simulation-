COVER 4 GLOSSARY

U,

JARTERS (CB TECHNIQUE) = A match technique in which the Corner is responsible for #1 unless a “China” or “In” call is made. Can

be played from a “Clue” or “MOD” alignment.

QUARTER FLAT (APEX TECHNIQUE) = An undercoverage technique in which the defender is responsible for any route to the flat area

or relating to the final #2 underneath. Can be played from a “Wall” or “Heads” alignment. If #3 is away, there is no delivery help, so

they must take #2 inside across the formation.

QUARTER HOOK (MIDPOINT TECHNIQUE) = A match technique in which the Midpoint is responsible for relating to the final #3 in the

distribution. Played from a “Wall” alignment vs. 3x1. Must carry #3 vs. 3x1 in “Quads.”

INSIDE QUARTERS (SAFETY TECHNIQUE) = A match technique in which the Safety is responsible for any upfield route by #2 or they

will zone the 1/4 and find work. Played from a Quarters alignment.

KEY & MATCH (ZONE PRINCIPLE) = A form of zone coverage with M/M principles that utilizes their key reads to streamline pattern

recognition and leverage routes properly. Defenders are matching routes that break into their leverage side and delivering routes

that break away from them into their help.

VISION & BREAK (ZONE PRINCIPLE) = A form of zone coverage in which defenders drop to landmarks, play with vision on the QB

while feeling routes & receivers, and break to the catch point using the QB’s body language as a trigger.

MAN MATCH = A form of coverage that plays like man-to-man unless their receiver releases into their help, then they zone off and

pattern read based on the concept played. It’s man-to-man with zone principles built in when necessary.

2-3-2 PRINCIPLE = An undercoverage principles in which the Apexes match the final #2 to their side and the Midpoint matches the

final #3 in the distribution. Also applies to Fire Zone.

#3 AWAY = Denotes the backside of the coverage in which #3 occupies the Midpoint. As a result, the “1/4 Flat” defender is also

responsible for taking #2 back across the formation (no delivery).

¥ QUADS = Quarters coverage with “key & match” zone principles. Corner is in “Clue” alignment.

¥ MOD = Quarters coverage with “key & match” zone principles. Corner is in “MOD” (press) alignment.

J AREA = A Quarters concept played to 3x formations in which the Corner plays normal Quarters technique while Apex, Safety, and

Midpoint play “Triangle” on #2 & #3 inside.

v TRIX =A potential Quarters concept played to 3x formations that plays “Quads” to the Nickel/Sam side while the Free Safety

keys #3-to-X to overtake the Midpoint’s vertical responsibility. If #3 is up or over, it plays as 5-over-3 strong. Backside plays like

“Freeze” or Cover 1 (by game plan).

v TRIP = A potential Quarters concept played to 3x formations that plays “Quads” to the Nickel/Sam side while the Free Safety

keys #3-to-Post to overtake the Midpoint’s vertical responsibility or play “vision & squeeze” technique in the post. If #3 is up or

over, it plays as 5-over-3 strong. Backside plays like “Freeze” or Cover 1 (by game plan).

v ZORRO =A True Quarters concept played with “vision & break” principles in which the Corner & Safety handle upfield threats

while Apex & Midpoint handle underneath threats.

v READ = A 3-over-2 “key & match” Quarters concept that plays as “Quads” or “Wild” (#2 Flat) based on #2's release.

v¥ BOOK = A 4-over-3 “key & match” Quarters concept played to a 3x formation in which the Corner locks #1 while the Apex,

Midpoint, and Safety play “Read” over #2 & #3. No “China” or “In” calls available.

Vv STUMP = A 4-over-3 “key & match” Quarters concept played to specific 3x formations that we will understand to play like

“Book,” with “China” and “In” calls available. Typically played to 3x exchanges where #1 is not a vertical threat.

v ERASE = A 4-over-3 “key & match” Quarters concept played to a 3x formation in which the Corner locks #1 while the Apex,

Midpoint, and Safety play “Quads” (#3 Away version) over #2 & #3.

v BRACKET = An aggressive form of Man-Match Quarters in which the Safety reads #2-to-1 looking to double the upfield threat.

Plays as “Cone” unless #3 is fast to the flat, then it plays like “MEG” would.

®. “CHINA” CALL = A live coverage adjustment made by the outer defender when the receiver they align over runs a route that

doesn’t clear the No Cover Zone. They will zone it off in order to combat Smash concepts.

@. “IN” CALL = A live coverage adjustment made by the outer defender when the receiver they align over runs shallow across the

No Cover Zone. They will zone it off to combat Hi/Low concepts with Shallow routes.

FINAL #1-2-3-4 = Denotes receiving threats from outermost to innermost within the pattern after distribution.

WALL ALIGNMENT = A pre-snap coverage alignment taken by an Apex or Midpoint defender in which they align inside of the

relative receiver in order to prevent free access inside. Depth can vary, but typically is about 5 yards deep.

CLUE ALIGNMENT = A pre-snap coverage alignment taken by a Corner in which they align 7+ yards deep and outside of #1 in order

to be able to properly read their keys and maintain advantageous positioning on routes.

HEADS ALIGNMENT =A pre-snap coverage alignment taken by any defender in which they align head-up over the relative receiver.

This allows them to disguise intent and prevent us from using the Apex’s alignment as a context clue.

MOD (PRESS-BAIL) ALIGNMENT = A pre-snap coverage alignment taken by a Corner in which they align pressed up to #1 before

bailing for depth (usually late in the cadence -- sometimes post-snap).

ZONE ALIGNMENT (PRESENTATION) = Denotes a pre-snap coverage alignment in which defenders align relative to their zone

responsibility rather than a specific matchup against like talent. Corners align relative to #1, Apexes align relative to #2, and

Midpoint aligns relative to #3. Corners do not follow X/Z across vs. slot. All motions are bumped across.
