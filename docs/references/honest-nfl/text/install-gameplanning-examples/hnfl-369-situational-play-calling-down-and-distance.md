P & 10 — Open down. Looking to kickstart the drive with a completion play or some

form of run if the front structure is advantageous.

1st & 10 — Open down. Generally lean more into the run game after moving the chains,

but quick game and screens are very successful for us in this context.

2nd & Short — Open down. We want to be looking to attack downfield with high reward

potential since we still have two downs to convert 1-2 yards if we fail.

2nd & Medium — Open down. Generally lean more into 3- & 5-step pass game and

between-the-tackles run game. Get to 3rd & Short at the least.

2nd & Long — Pass down. Especially if it’s as a result of an ineffective run snap. Screen

game has been great getting us back on track as well.

2nd & XL — Obvious pass. Get to 3rd & Medium at the least so we can open the

playbook back up.

3rd & Short — Run down. Possible to treat it the same as 2nd & Short based on game

context. Just know that we will never fear a 4th & 1 scenario.

3rd & Medium — Pass down with the potential to be an open down if we like the front

structure. We will almost always be willing to go on 4th & Short, so we can treat it the

Same as 2nd & Medium in those scenarios.

3rd & Long — Pass down. We can treat this like 2nd & Long in most situations unless we

are inside our own 40.

3rd & XL — Field position. Can be treated like 2nd & XL under some circumstances, but

generally the play for positioning is the way. Screen game, Draw series, etc.

e

4th & Short — Just get a fucking yard. We welcome SY situations with open arms.

FLAWS DON’T BECOME ANCHORS — The flip side to weaponizing talent is being able

to mask weaknesses through effective game planning and creative play calling. If the

defense is able to exploit the weakest link, the offense is operating at a disadvantage

and loses their ability to dictate the terms of play. This is most commonly applied to

the Quarterback (“game managers”) and offensive line, but it can include any

position if what they’re lacking affects anyone else on offense. If your ‘Z’ struggles to

get off the line against press, don’t design and call plays that put them in that

position.

THEY ARE “BALANCED” IN THE BEST WAYS — The most misleading phrase that

results in the greatest demises of offensive coaches is being “balanced” by measuring

their ratio of runs to passes. The only thing about an offense that needs to be

“balanced” is the way you attack a defense. That can be accomplished with any ratio

of runs to passes as long as the defense is being attacked in multiple ways that limit

their ability to key in on any one thing.

LONG, SUSTAINED DRIVES CONSISTENTLY THROUGHOUT — A long, time-consuming

drive that ends in a touchdown is hard to achieve consistently. To be able to achieve

them consistently throughout a game is a sign of a play caller who is staying ahead of

the defense and putting their players in posi

ons to execute in ways that the defense

hasn’t been able to catch up to. It’s likely that they’re staying in “open downs”

(downs in which the entire playbook is open to be called, like 2nd & 4-7 or 3rd & 2-4)
