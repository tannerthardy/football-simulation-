# QB_V2 design note: the judgment (D) and the cycle (E) (2026-11-24)

QB lane, the first deliverable under [the plan of record](https://github.com/tannerthardy/football-simulation-/blob/703fc628c/docs/reviews/2026-11-24-qb-decision-plan.md)
(develop 703fc628c). For review by Integration, and by Coverage for information honesty from the defense's side,
before any code. Module C (the look) is summarized where D and E depend on it. Section 5 lists where I think the plan
should change.

## 1. Vocabulary

- **Picture** of an agent: `QBMind.estimate(d)` (position, velocity, facing), `pictureAge(d)`, `pictureSpread(d)`.
  Everything below reads only these, his receiver forecasts, his felt rush and the authored read plan. No `Agent`
  field is read for a defender except through them.
- **Look**: where his eyes are and how resolved it is. One look at a time, on a receiver or on a key defender.
  `res = 1 − e^(−dwell/τ)` while the target is inside his clear cone. τ is his processing (playRecognition,
  decisionMaking, experience), slowed by felt pressure and by disagreement with his pre-snap expectation. Resolution is
  kept when his eyes leave (what he saw stays his) and ages with the picture.
- **Scouting report** for a defender: believed speed scale, reaction, ball skills and technique. It is the position's
  typical values plus a per-player adjustment that film would show, set before the game. It is never the defender's
  true rating scaled by the passer's rating (§1.6's `believe()` is rebuilt as this). The passer's playRecognition
  decides how far he moves from the position typical toward the film adjustment.

## 2. Module D: the judgment

**Input:** one candidate throw: target, catch point, ball flight (time and arc from the throw mechanics, as now),
release time (now + windup).
**Output:** `{ P, risk, spreadP, spreadRisk, roles }`. P is his belief the ball is caught; risk is his belief it is
intercepted; the spreads come from the look's resolution and the pictures' spread; roles lists who he accounted for
and how (for the trace and the calibration operator).

Each defender in his picture gets at most one role per throw, in this order: coverer, then driver, then lane man.
A defender not in his picture well enough (below) gets no role. Each role has one physical model.

### 2.1 The coverer

**Who:** the defender his picture puts on the receiver, meaning within the coverage radius of the receiver's
forecast path and on it in leverage (inside, outside, over or under). One per throw. With two in that radius (a
bracket), the nearer is the coverer and the other is a driver.

**What he reads, from his picture at the decision:**
- leverage: the coverer's offset across the route's line;
- cushion: depth to the receiver;
- hips: his facing against the route's stem (turned to run, square, or open);
- speed: closing or bailing along the stem.

**Model:**
1. Before the break, the coverer holds his seen offset to the receiver along the receiver's forecast path (the
   forecast gives the receiver at each time). He changes it only as his seen velocity already says he is changing it.
2. At the receiver's break, as the forecast places it, the coverer is still moving on his pre-break relation. After
   his believed reaction, from the scouting report, he redirects to the catch point.
3. The time to the catch point comes from the body law, `bodyArrive`: his pre-break state from step 1 projected to
   the break plus reaction, his facing (a bail with hips turned must turn before it can come back), braking and
   re-acceleration, with the scouting report's speed.
4. With his hips already turned toward the break direction (a squat on a stop, a trail on a slant), the reaction is
   only the time to see the break; nothing is lost turning.

**Arrival margin:** `m = tBall − tArrive`, with spread from the receiver's forecast spread and the coverer's picture
spread, both inflated by `1 − res` of the look. Its contributions: to P, the share where he arrives and plays the ball
(contest and break-up); to risk, the share where he arrives in front of the receiver's hands on the ball's line (his
leverage on the throw side).

This is the anticipation that lets a window clear before the break: a bailing corner on a stop shows a positive
margin at the plant, a squatting corner a negative one, before the receiver has turned.

**What it does not read:** the defender's assignment, his true key, or whether he will in fact break on the ball.
The leak control (`docs/reviews/2026-11-24-window-arrival.md`) found no improper cue in our defense's closing during
the windup under FLIGHT_BREAK, so the coverer's closing there is the route, which this model reproduces from the
forecast.

### 2.2 The driver

**Who:** a defender off the receiver, in his picture, with his eyes toward the passer (seen facing within the
defender's eye cone of the passer, as the passer sees him) and close enough to reach the catch point or the ball's line
in the flight time at the scouting report's top speed.

**Model:** he starts on the cue he can see:
- the passer's load (windup start) for a driver whose eyes are on him, plus his reaction;
- the release, plus reaction, for a driver turned away whose eyes come back to the passer.
Both cues exist in our engine (`release-belief.ts`: `loadingAt`, `releasedAt`); Coverage is asked to confirm which
drivers use which (section 5, item 2). His arrival is the body law from his seen state. The margin enters P and risk
as for the coverer. A driver arriving in front of the catch point on the ball's line is the interception the
judgment must see coming.

### 2.3 The lane man

**Who:** anyone in his picture under the ball's path: an underneath defender, or a rusher whose hands are up near the
line.

**Model:** geometry. The ball's height at his position as it passes, against his reach (standing or jumping by his
seen state, from the scouting report), with his move toward the line from the same start cue as a driver. A rusher's
hands near the line are a lane man for low throws, which is the batted-ball case. The lane man contributes to risk (a
tip or a catch) and to 1 − P.

### 2.4 Who he accounts for

- The coverer always, if he is in the picture at all.
- Drivers and lane men in his clear cone during the look, or seen within the picture's memory horizon. Each enters
  with the error his picture carries (PERIPH_ACUITY, age).
- A defender he has never seen, or whose picture has aged out, is not in the judgment. The robber the passer never
  looked at, and the hook defender behind him, are where the unpriced interceptions come from.

Ratings act through τ and the cones (how many he has and how true), the scouting report's accuracy, and the bar and
need in E. No rating changes a role's physics or awards a margin.

### 2.5 Stated choices and the calibration contract

**Stated choices** (labeled in code, no fitted constants):
- the coverage radius for "on the receiver" (the coverer);
- the eye-cone width for "eyes on the passer" (the driver);
- the reach margins (standing, jump), from the body law where it has them;
- the secure-catch interval after arrival.

**The contract:** a standing test and report.
- For a mid-rated passer with a resolved look, on league games of the current engine, compare per role the believed
  arrival margin against the actual one (was the role's defender at the catch point by the ball, and where).
- Compare the throw's believed P and risk against the actual completion and interception, binned.
- Tolerances are stated per role. When the defense changes and a role drifts, the test goes red and names that role.
  The repair is to that role's model.

The operator extends `scripts/diag/eyes/arrival-check.ts` and `int-price.ts`, which already measure believed against
actual arrival by branch. Tools owns the standing test.

## 3. Module E: the cycle

One state machine on one clock, his feet. Each tick: perception (QBMind, unchanged) → look (C) → state step (E),
which may call the judgment (D) → one command.

### 3.1 State fields

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `state` | `presnap`, `drop`, `read`, `throw`, `structureOut`, `escape` (out of the pocket) |
| `plan` | the authored read plan (A) with B's pre-snap choices applied: ordered reads, each with its step, look target (receiver or key defender), candidate throws, on-break or seen-open, checkdown, hot answer |
| `step` | footwork step counter: 0 at the plant (the drop's count from the snap), then hitches at his stride |
| `stepAt[n]` | when step n lands (stride from the drop class; the quick drop's hitch is one stride) |
| `readIdx` | the live read; read n is live from `stepAt[n]` |
| `look` | `{ target, since, res }` per look target; kept after the eyes leave |
| `need` | confidence need (composure, discipline), lowered by felt pressure |
| `bar` | P threshold and risk limit from temperament (aggression, decisionMaking), lowered by urgency |
| `urgency` | from felt rush and structure used (steps run out); no timer from the set |
| `pressureState` | `clean`, `felt`, `imminent`, `contact`, each with enter and exit margins on the felt rush |
| `passed` | reads left (a read is left once) |
| `outPass` | how many out-of-structure passes he has made (at most 1) |
| `decision` | the committed throw or action, with the tick it was taken |

### 3.2 Transitions

**presnap → drop** at the snap. Before it, B has written the starting read, the alert and the hot threat into `plan`.

**drop.**
- Eyes on the plan's confirm defender: one look; its result can flip the plan's side once, with a margin.
- On a hot trigger (the hot threat seen unblocked and coming, pressure ≥ `felt`): `read` on the hot answer with
  `step = 0`. Same gates as any read; the hot answer's look may be peripheral, and its spread is the cost.
- At the plant: `read` with `readIdx = 0`.

**read (read n live).** On each tick:
1. The look goes to read n's target.
2. If res ≥ `need`, D judges the read's candidate throws. If one clears `bar`, go to `throw` (commit).
3. If res ≥ `need` and none clears, the read is answered no. At `stepAt[n+1]` the eyes move: `readIdx = n+1`, read n
   goes to `passed`.
4. If res < `need`:
   - a look that is resolving holds (the stare-down), up to `stepAt[n+1]` plus the time it would take to resolve;
   - a look that cannot resolve (target outside his clear cone after the head's turn) does not hold, and the eyes move
     at `stepAt[n+1]`. This is the step clock's measured repair.
5. The pressure ladder (3.3) can pre-empt at any tick.
6. When the last read is passed: `structureOut`.

**throw.** `startThrow` with the committed decision. THROW_HOLD and QB_STICK govern a pump on a perceived change, as
now.

**structureOut.**
- One pass (`outPass < 1`) through the reads not passed (and the checkdown), at the lowered bar, on the same look
  rules.
- Then the checkdown if live; then extend (pocket movement or `escape`) or throw it away by THROWAWAY_FELT.
- No restart at read 1.

**escape.** Out of the pocket by his own position (THROWAWAY_FELT's margins). Reads become scramble-drill reads (the
nearest receiver working his way), the judgment on the same roles, throw it away on THROWAWAY_FELT. Ported as needed
by phase.

### 3.3 One ordered pressure response

`pressureState` moves on the felt rush, with margins (for example `felt` enters at 0.6 and exits at 0.4, as
THROWAWAY_FELT). The response is chosen by state, not by tick order:

| `pressureState` | response |
|---|---|
| `clean` | the cycle as above |
| `felt` | `need` and `bar` lower (urgency); the look shortens; the checkdown becomes a read if its step has passed |
| `imminent` | hot answer if live; else the best option clearing the lowered bar; else pocket movement toward the space his picture shows (step up or slide); else `escape` if the lane is there; else throw it away |
| `contact` | throw it away or protect the ball (Flex's under-contact work) |

Each response is a decision kept until its state exits.

### 3.4 Every throw through the same gate

Hot, rhythm, checkdown and out-of-structure throws all go through D with the look as it is. Skipping the eyes is paid
by the spread of an unresolved or peripheral look in D, and by the throw mechanics' accuracy from his body orientation,
as now. No separate penalty is added, so the cost is not counted twice.

### 3.5 Commit to a decision

- A read is left once.
- Every threshold has enter and exit margins: need, bar, pressure states and the pocket.
- A committed throw is pumped only on a perceived change clearing QB_STICK's margin.
- The dither budget (`test/dither-budget.test.ts`) gets a row for each new stage.

## 5. Where I think the plan should change

1. **A read can be on a defender, not only a receiver.** Slant-flat and stick are taught as reads of the flat or curl
   defender (sink or widen), and the throw is decided by him. In E a read's look target is the key defender, and the
   read's candidate throws are both routes; D judges each. Phase 1 needs Rules' plans to name the key per concept.

2. **The driver starts at the cue he can see, not always at the release.** Our defenders believe the load
   (`loadingAt`) before the release, and under FLIGHT_BREAK a disciplined eyes-on driver uses it. The passer, as
   taught, knows that a defender staring at him breaks on his shoulders. If D starts every driver at the release, it
   misprices those drivers by the windup (0.3-0.4 s), the same mispricing measured in the arrival check. Coverage to
   confirm which drivers use which cue, by technique.

3. **The stare-down is a hold.** The plan's E says "otherwise his eyes move at the next step". That fits the passer
   who is resolved and answered no. The passer who is not yet resolved stays: that is the stare-down, and it must be
   allowed, or ratings cannot produce it. It must be bounded too: only a resolving look holds, as the step clock's
   repair, and pressure pre-empts it. I have written E that way (3.2, item 4).

4. **Pressure by state, not by order.** "Hot, else speed up, else checkdown, else move, else leave, else throw it
   away" read as an order evaluated each tick is again "the first to return wins". I propose it keyed on
   `pressureState` with margins (3.3), so one response is chosen and kept.

5. **No urgency timer.** The plan keeps "falls with urgency". The urgency must come from the felt rush and the
   structure used up (his steps run out), not from a per-passer timer from the set. THROWAWAY_FELT just removed that
   timer's throwaway; V2 should not bring it back as urgency.

6. **Calibration needs a resolved look and a fixed passer.** The contract measures the model, not a rating effect,
   so it uses one mid-rated passer and only throws with res ≥ 0.9. Low-rated rows are the rating panel's job.

7. **Phase 1 gate on quick game needs Rules' key defender and step per read**, and the release-minus-break operator
   for slants and quick outs on the NFL side. Stops and outs exist (`release_vs_break.py`); slants need the in-family
   split.

## 6. §1.6 fixes on the current engine (separate, small landings)

- Rushers' true `profile` and `speedScale` in felt time and hold (qb.ts:2782, 2749): use the scouting report's
  believed profile and speed. His felt time then errs where the rusher is faster or slower than the position's
  typical, by the passer's recognition.
- `laneDist`'s true leap (2142): the scouting report's believed reach.
- `screenTouch` (2885): the true profile is replaced by the scouting report; the true engagement by his picture's
  "blocked" (a blocker between, from his picture).
- `believe()`: becomes the scouting report (1. above), built once per game per defender.

Each lands as its own flag, measured on the same seeds, with the decision-information scan showing its rows fall.
