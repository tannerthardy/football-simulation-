## What ordinary movement was missing

The existing compound response matches an obstructing teammate’s transverse motion while braking toward the actor’s existing responsibility. That is useful for moving traffic. A stationary teammate directly ahead supplies no transverse motion to match, however, so the available action family can remain a stop. Stopping is appropriate when geometry or momentum requires it; it is not a complete method for passing a finite stationary obstruction.

The staged actor starts at x50, yCENTER, velocity `(4,0)` and facing0, repeatedly tracking the **same** point `(58,CENTER)` with existing speed.7/gain3/facing0. Peers are initially stationary passive bodies receiving `{vel:0,free:true}` requests. They remain physical, movable, collidable bodies; they do not actively brace or hold position. Their pictures pass through the existing delayed perception buffer with their ages preserved. The controller receives neither future teammate decisions nor private intentions. This is a controlled movement sequence, not a natural football decision.

## Coherent successor and legitimate information boundary

`traffic-path.ts` represents acquired teammate **touch-disc geometry together**. Visible tangent segments and exposed circular arcs join at tangent/intersection nodes. Covered overlap seams cannot become shortcuts through another body. The helper finds the shortest path in that represented graph using caller-supplied field bounds and touch radii. It does not shrink bodies, add a universal teammate clearance bubble, promise future teammate motion or claim that every physically possible route is represented. An inside-body goal or unavailable represented route declines this optional action and retains the ordinary response.

`traffic-route.ts` preserves the existing own goal. It tries short requests along the proposed first direction at the actor’s existing raw pace, restricted pace and gather pace. Existing facing, posture, effort and coverage containment remain. Ordinary .60-second movement previews account for the body’s current momentum and support. The remaining represented joint-route distance at the achieved endpoint ranks the alternatives, using the same short constant-velocity peer estimate. A brief brake or temporary retreat can therefore make useful access possible.

Only whether the actor is already maneuvering is retained. Each evaluation uses the new ordinary acquired pictures; unknown/nonfinite peer age or motion and unknown own-goal flow do not become stationary or private live data. Existing eligible free man/zone scope is retained. This adds no route assignment, receiver read, RB gap choice, body maneuver or physical capability. Constant velocity is an estimate, not knowledge of the teammate’s next choice.

The final optimization reduces work for both the current direction proposal and scalar endpoint cost. `trafficRoute` initially solves a plane relaxation using discs violated by the direct route, then adds every omitted disc crossed by the returned continuous line or circular arc. It returns that lower-bound route only after checking **all explicit omitted discs and the field bounds**, including arc extrema. Failure falls back to the original full `trafficPath` graph, whose bytes remain unchanged; returned arc identifiers are remapped to original input discs. This is constraint generation, not ignoring distant peers. Equal-distance side ties can differ on other geometries; saved-case identity does not prove universal first-direction equivalence. Every proposed direction still passes the same achievable-motion/contact checks.

The static graph proposes a direction and measures access. The short physical forecast evaluates a command. **Neither certifies a clear route through the future play.** Ordinary movement remains available when the optional action cannot be supported. Joint geometry, endpoint ranking and achievable braking form the demonstrated correction; this experiment does not isolate geometry alone.
