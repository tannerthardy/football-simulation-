## Reading

1. **Our edge rushers win as often and as fast as the NFL's, but in front of the tackle, not around him.**
   - At the win, ours are level or in front 55–57% of the time (NFL 27%) and past outside 8–9% (NFL 26%).
   - Standing in front of a retreating tackle, the rusher is inside the OL span when he reaches the QB. That is why
     `free-rush.py` counted almost no "edge" free rushers.
   - The 3%-vs-23% gap is a difference in **how** the edge wins, not whether it does.
2. **The rusher never threatens the corner.** He asks to run inward, straight at the QB (−2.6 to −3.1 yd/s inward), so
   his path asks for the line the tackle already owns. The tackle's body takes out the lateral part: he achieves about
   0 outward. That is Integration's case (a): no arc to the QB's launch point, so no level-then-turn, no bend, and few
   inside counters (inside at 1.5 s: 1% against 8%).
3. **The tackle never stops kicking.**
   - At 2.5 s, 1.3 yd in front of the QB, he still asks to retreat at 2.6 yd/s.
   - By 3.0 s he is deeper than the QB (−8.2 against −7.8). The NFL tackle sets to about −6.8, with the QB 1.4 yd
     behind him.
   - He stays wide (+0.8 against the NFL's −0.6), never widening the rusher past the QB and riding him there.
   - So rusher and tackle arrive together in the QB's lap: a walked-back, "in front" win.
4. **Not the bend or the speed.** Speeds around the arc are NFL-like, and time to win is similar. Ours never take the
   arc, so bend is not tested.

## Proposal (not built; owner to be decided, since Run Game is parked)

Both pieces are controllers: coached rules, built as taught and from what the player sees.

1. **The edge rusher's rush plan: rush the arc to the QB's launch point.**
   - Aim at a landmark outside the tackle's outside shoulder at the QB's set depth; get level; turn to the QB when his
     hips are past the tackle's (bend, by agility and speed through a curved path; the World decides what a body
     leaning on him allows).
   - If the tackle oversets to the arc, counter inside. That is the intent; execution comes by his pass-rush and
     finesse ratings.
   - Requested against achieved shows whether the bend physics holds once the path asks for it.
2. **The tackle's set: kick to the depth that meets the arc at the QB's launch point, then stop and ride.**
   - Kick-slide to depth and width by the rusher's alignment and the QB's drop.
   - Then anchor and push the rusher past the QB's launch point, rather than keep retreating.
   - Retreat only as he is driven: requested against achieved distinguishes "driven back" from "kicking".

Gates for either:
- the state table against time, against the NFL's;
- where the win happens against the tackle;
- tackle depth and width against the QB's depth;
- the edge share of free rushers at 2 yd;
- downstream: the no-throw population (scrambles against edge rushers, the NFL's 40%).

Read the tackle and the rusher one at a time, then together. A rusher who takes the arc against a tackle who keeps
retreating may just meet him deeper.
