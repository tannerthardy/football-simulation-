# SET_SPOT: the interior pass set's reference (Flex, 2026-11-15)

This is section 23 of the hand-fight design note on the Flex branch (`claude/quirky-franklin-9lbwbp`), whose sections
20–22 hold the measurements behind it. It answers Integration's order of 2026-11-15: design the set reference first,
the pins after.

Flags, both off:
- `SET_SPOT`: the interior set.
- `SET_SPOT_TACKLE` (needs `SET_SPOT`): the tackles' half. It is unrepaired and stays off.

## What changed

### 1. The live-QB read is removed, not replaced (interior OL)

The interior set read where the QB is now, behind the blocker and unseen. That read came in three places:
- `passSetDepth`'s clearance, `sim.los - sim.qb.pos.x - 3.8`;
- the `qb` the pass set aims at;
- `protectPoint`'s fallback.

Under `SET_SPOT` an interior lineman's set no longer reads the live QB anywhere:
- **Depth:** the short set (section 3).
- **Protected point:** the launch point he knows from the call (`calledLaunchX`). Without a pass concept, it is the QB's
  alignment at the snap.

No other live read takes its place.

### 2. The tackles: `SET_SPOT_TACKLE`, rusher-paced (repaired; off)

**Built after the patch above.** Integration approved the coached kick-slide: paced by the rusher, with the call as its
floor, never set from the quarterback's measured depth. Under `SET_SPOT_TACKLE`:
- **His own set.** The three-step vertical set off his snap spot (3 × 0.45 yd), no deeper than the pocket's front.
- **Beat him to the spot.** He kicks at the pace that wins the race to the pocket's front: the tackle clearance (2.8 yd)
  in front of the launch point he knows from the call. The race uses the rusher's upfield speed as the tackle sees him:
  his own way to that depth over the rusher's time to it. A faster rusher means more pace.
- **Unchanged bounds.** He stays deeper than the man by `KICK_LEAD`, never takes ground back, and never goes past the
  launch point.

`qb-pocket.py`, `sack-room.py`):

**What the attempts show:**
- **Call-only spots** set him too deep. The call's launch depth is about a yard deeper than where quarterbacks are at
  2 s.
- **The rusher's present depth alone** is too late against speed. A rusher who wins the corner wraps under to the
  quarterback (edge-inside pressure 21%).
- **The race to the floor** had a bug in its target. It raced him to the launch point, not to the pocket's front.
- **The race to the pocket's front** reproduces the live-QB law's depth without reading the quarterback. Edge level
  and inside pressure are near the NFL's.

**Still open.** The corner: fast rushers get past outside far less than the NFL's 43%. That gap predates this change
(live-QB 15–17%) and is in the corner itself, not the read. It is recorded in KNOWN_PROBLEMS PRO-INFO #12.

### 3. The interior short set: depth, as taught

As taught, the guard sets back two steps (the tackle three). Guards hold the pocket's depth, fight at the line and give
little ground.

Under `SET_SPOT` the interior lineman sets two set steps (2 × 0.45 yd) straight back off his snap spot, no deeper than
the pocket's front, which is the call's launch point less 3.8. He meets the rusher there. Further ground is what the
rusher's push wins.

**The choice.** The set request is the short set only. There is no second phase that gives ground with the drop:
coaches teach against it, and Integration asked specifically whether the late shallowness was the drive or the
set (below).

Attempt 1 sent him to the pocket's front from the snap: 1.80 / 3.00 / 3.90 yd deep at 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 s, against the
NFL's 0.80 / 1.70 / 2.70. It was replaced.

### 4. The lateral reference, unbounded

**The target.** He aims at the inside-out point on his man: where the rusher's line to the launch point crosses his
own depth (`setSpotTarget`). It is fed forward by that point's own motion, a fraction `frac` of the rusher's lateral
velocity.

**What it replaces.** Off, his target was a step in front of the rusher on the rusher's line, moving with the man he was
carrying. That made a self-starting slide (staged: 1.28 yd/s by 1.5 s from no drift at all; section 21).

**The bounds tried.** Two bounds were tried, and each let the man go when he worked across it:
1. a fixed zone of half a split about his alignment (`SET_ZONE` 0.65 yd);
2. no drift beyond his own split from the neighbors he sees, plus half a split ("no gap to the man inside").

| interior pressers "engaged, then free", % of dropbacks (s11 / s23) | off | zone | neighbors | unbounded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | 7.0 / 8.4 | 11.0 / 10.4 | 17.7 / 18.6 | 5.4 / 6.1 |

In games the neighbors are on their own men, so a bound off them is tighter than the zone.

**The reference needs no bound.** As the pair leaves the launch line, the target pulls back toward the ball. Staged
(`mirror-staged.mts`, the center and DT_W with the protector's set staged):
- **No lateral request:** a drift of ±0.6 yd/s, or none, peaks at 0.5–0.8 yd/s and turns back by 2.5–3.0 s, with him
  about 0.9 yd off his start. The default instead grows to 1.28 yd/s and keeps going.
- **The rusher's real lateral move (2 yd/s across):** mirrored throughout at a constant 0.3–0.4 yd, carried about 2 yd,
  then the pair turns back.
- So the drift dies on a 2.5 s scale, not a 1 s one. The gate holds in that sense: no self-starting loop, and his man
  is not given up.

**What it does:**
- The interior lineman now gets off the ball. He meets the rusher at about the NFL's depth by 1.0 s (0.85 against 0.80;
  0.50 off), and the held pair no longer slides.
- Pressure and sacks are about unchanged overall. Interior pressure falls a little, and the edge is unchanged.
- **Late, the pocket is shallow:** 1.75 against 2.70 yd at 2.0 s. Is that the rusher's drive, or the set's second
  phase? Mostly the drive (`set-retreat.mts`, seed 11 × 4, LG / C / RG on dropbacks):
  - Past the short set he asks for little retreat: 0.39 / 0.43 / 0.46 yd/s at 1.0–1.5 / 1.5–2.0 / 2.0–2.5 s.
  - He is carried back at 1.19 / 0.96 / 0.79 yd/s, so half to two thirds of the late ground is the rusher's drive.
  - Off, his own request ran 1.6–1.8 yd/s late. That request was the old second phase: the live QB's drop, read through
    the clearance.
  - The NFL's held rusher still moves at 1.58 yd/s late, ours at 1.0–1.1; toward the QB, 0.85 against 0.2–0.3. The
    held rusher asks for 4.5 yd/s at the QB and is stopped.
  - So the late gap is how hard the pair drives through his anchor. It is not a request we are missing. Adding a
    "give ground" request would be the opposite of the coaching.
  - Releases are now rarer than the NFL's (never released 63% against 37%), consistent with the same stall.
- **Bull rush** (`rep-rolls.mts`, seed 11 × 4):
  - Sheds a bull opened: off 14 of 100 sheds in 379 dropbacks, giving 13 pressures. SET_SPOT: 7 of 77 sheds in 344
    dropbacks, giving 4 pressures.
  - Carried back beyond his request on reps with a bull: 0.34 → 0.30 yd/s for the stronger rushers (top third) and
    0.14 → 0.14 for the weaker.
  - Interior gain on the QB with no move in flight: 0.22 → 0.08 yd per 0.5 s.
  - The bull still moves the blocker by strength. It opens fewer clean wins because he meets it set, not standing.

## 5. POCKET_LEVEL: the interior set's second phase (off; needs SET_SPOT)

**Why.** Under SET_SPOT the held interior pair stalled: the rusher's late drive toward the QB was 0.2 yd/s against the
NFL's 0.85, and never released 61–64% against 37%. Two measurements explained it:
- **The push contest** (`anchor-staged.mts`). In the staged pair the blocker, driven back, sits at his traction limit,
  while the rusher, moving forward, is 10–15% short of his to force–velocity. Between men of similar mass the pair
  settles where neither moves the other. The rusher's strength never enters: his leg drive is itself traction-capped.
  That model-rule problem has gone to Tools through Integration.
- **The uncovered blocker** (`ol-set-depth.py --uncovered`). The NFL's interior lineman with nobody within 1.2 yd of
  him through 2.0 s goes back 0.2 / 0.9 / 1.9 / 2.9 yd at 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 s, as far as an engaged one
  (0.2 / 0.8 / 1.7 / 2.7). So the NFL's walk-back is his set, keeping level with the pocket, not the push. The
  walk-back is also nearly weight-flat in the NFL (`held-walkback.py`: 1.23–1.36 yd/s at 1.0–1.5 s across rusher-minus-
  blocker weight bins). Off, the live-QB clearance had produced this retreat; SET_SPOT removed it, which exposed the
  stall.

**The mechanism.**
- **The pocket** is the cup the line forms around the launch point he knows from the call. It is never the QB.
- **The target.** After the short set he keeps level with that cup: his distance from the launch point is the median
  of three, his two neighbors' as he sees them beside him and the tackles' mean. The honest tackles set the pocket's
  edges from the rusher and the call. His target is the point at that distance at his own width, no shallower than his
  short set and no deeper than the pocket's front.
- **The pace** is the set's own track and the rep's kick toward the target. Engaged, he gives the ground under control
  with his man in front; stepPassRep's lateral reference is unchanged.

**The median of three is a stated choice.** It makes one neighbor beaten cleanly an outlier, so he doesn't drag
anyone, while a stunt or two men driven back together move it. The alternatives considered:
- **The mean of the two neighbors.** One beaten neighbor drags him halfway.
- **The line between the tackles, interpolated at his width.** It ignores the interior men. It is also too deep for
  the interior: the cup is curved, so the interior sits shallower than the tackles. It would put the interior at about
  4 yd at 2.0 s.
- **The call's drop schedule alone** (where the call puts the QB, less a clearance). The tackle attempts showed the
  call's launch depth sits about a yard deeper than where quarterbacks are at 2 s, so it overshoots.
- **The neighbors alone.** It is self-referential: nobody leads, so the line stays at the short set.
