# PASS_OFF alone on engine-69 (Flex, 2026-11-15)

`PASS_OFF` (off): a pass rusher who beats his blocker while a helper's hands are on him is kept by the helper, who
has seen him come off (`passOff`, `beat` in `src/sim/engagement.ts`). It was designed and first read with
FITTED_HANDS, in section 10 of the hand-fight design note on the Flex branch (`claude/quirky-franklin-9lbwbp`). This
note is its own read, alone, on engine-69 (1538db2d).

**Read.** Seeds 11 and 23 x 8 games, engine-69 defaults, `PASS_OFF=1` against off. These are the same operators the
come-off diagnosis used, run on NFL and sim: `pressure-source.py`, `sack-room.py` and `interior-comeoff.py`, all in
`scripts/diag/pass-operators/`.

**What it does:**
- The interior man who comes off his blocker reaches the QB about half as often.
- The help case shows the mechanism directly.
- The no-help case falls as well. Presumably that is through the next blocker's pickup, but this read did not
  separate it.
- Overall pressure and sacks are about unchanged. Interior pressure moves to the other sources; the
  pressure-source rows show where.

## The honest hand-over (2026-11-16, engine-70 / engine-71)

**The defect.** At the hand-over `passOff` set the rep's control to 0 whatever the keeper's hands were doing. Logged in
rush-bull and rush-rip, every hand-over went to exactly 0 from −0.41..+0.75. A fresh engagement takes its control from
the bodies at first contact. `control` sets:
- the contact hands' push, hold and steer;
- whether he punches (≥ 0);
- who owns the grip under OPEN_HANDS;
- the move weights and the rolls.
So the 0 gave the keeper the grip and punch eligibility by assignment.

**Now (folded into PASS_OFF):** the keeper's control is the first-contact law.
- **The exchange:** his punch against the rusher's current move (his swipe against a finesse move, his strike against
  power), without the old pairing's rep edge.
- **Hands not loaded:** a keeper whose hands are not yet on the man (the `seen` hand-over) must land them before the man
  clears his reach. Landing after is a miss (−0.5, as a swiped punch); between, the fit falls smoothly with the share of
  that window he used.
- **Off his feet:** no fit.

Two attempts reached this:
1. The first bounded the fit by firstHands. firstHands compares both men's landing over the same distance, so it never
   penalised a late keeper.
2. The second applied the landing window to every keeper, so a fitted, slowly closing keeper was a near miss.

The correction keys the landing window on the load on his own hands joint, not on which check found him: the `seen`
check runs first each tick and finds fitted keepers too.

**Staged** (`handover-staged.mts`, 864 plays, mirrored; scripted → honest):
- **Control after**, p10 / 50 / 90: 0 / 0 / 0 → −0.50 / −0.24 / +0.31.
- **Fitted keepers:** median −0.23 (p25 −0.50). Rushers reaching the QB 13 → 20%.
- **Unloaded, late keepers:** median −0.50. Reaching the QB 52 → 36% (n 22).
- **All hand-overs:** rushers reaching the QB 14 → 20%.

**What PASS_OFF keeps under an honest hand-over:**
- Off-blocker reach: about 10% against 11.3% with it off. That is about what the scripted version gave, small and within
  seed noise.
- The sacks the scripted reset saved are gone.
- On engine-70 PASS_OFF's whole effect is far smaller than the 13–14 → 7% read on engine-69.
