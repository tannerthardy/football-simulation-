# SCREEN_QB: screens thrown as NFL quarterbacks throw them

Flag `SCREEN_QB` (off; experimental). Branch `rules/screens`. Lane: Rules + Coaching. Date 2026-11-13.

This PR opens off by default. It carries the screen lab's operator fix, so the flag can be judged on the lab's own
numbers.

## The operator fix (screen lab)

The lab's flight ran to the sim's `catch` event, which is possession: the end of the securing window, about 0.2–0.45 s
after the ball reaches the hands. NFL `pass_arrived` is the ball at the hands. The lab now writes `pass_arrived` from
the first `secure` event after the throw (CATCH_SECURE).

With that change the old "float" touch's 0.8 s flight turned out to be the operator; NFL screens fly 0.5 s over 6–10 yd.
A frame check on BDB 2025 week 1 completions (`scripts/diag/flight/nfl_arrival_check.py`) found:
- At `pass_arrived` the ball is about 1.0–1.7 yd from the receiver's tracked center (median by bin).
- It closes to 0.3–0.5 yd one or two frames later.
- No completion had a tip before the arrival.
So `pass_arrived` is the ball arriving at about arm's length, roughly 0.05 s before it reaches the hands' contact. The
two operators are the same moment to within a frame.

## What the flag does

**Slow screens (RB, TE).** He throws on the screen's timing, once the receiver is ready (settled behind his blockers),
at a low bar of min(thr, 0.3). Waiting for the ordinary completion bar had him hit before the throw on a quarter to a
third of slow screens.

**Touch.** Screens are thrown firm. He adds a little air (×1.2) only over a rusher in his lane, as he sees it.

**The bubble's numbers kill.** At the line he checks out of the bubble when he counts more defenders than receivers to
that side, in his own picture.

**A quick screen thrown to its spot** goes on its timing unless a defender in his picture is within 2 yd of the spot.

**A dead quick screen is thrown away on his read, not on a clock.** From the moment the screen should be thrown (after
his drop and the receiver's sell), he reads from his own delayed, error-laden picture (`mind.estimate`, `see`) whether
it is dead:
- **The catcher is covered:** a defender not in a block within 1.5 yd of the catcher, where he sees him, or of the
  spot it is thrown to.
- **The lane is filled:** any defender in the first min(4 yd, 45%) of the throw's line, within 1.5 yd of it.

A picture that stays dead for his patience is thrown away.

**Patience is per player:** 0.15 + 0.25·(1 − decisionMaking) + 0.3·aggression, where aggression rises for gunslingers
and falls with decision-making. It is his processing time plus how long he hangs on.

**The clock is only the outer bound:** one second past the screen's timing, if the screen still isn't throwable by his
ordinary evaluation. The QB's `screenThrownAway` records which one fired (`'dead'` or `'bound'`).

**A quick screen thrown to its spot is carried out as it was decided.** Two execution faults held the tunnel to
2.7–3.0 s even though the decision to throw it came at 1.4 s.
- **The recheck.** The recheck at the delivery (`pullDown`) re-priced the throw as a route's window. That window priced
  the defender a blocker in the call is assigned to as a 40%+ pick, so he pumped on every look.
- **The release aim.** The release re-aimed at his forecast of the receiver, which points the wrong way while the
  receiver is still selling.
- **The fix.** The recheck now uses the rule the throw was made on (a defender now sitting on the spot in his picture),
  and the release goes to the spot.

**`naturalFlight` is refit** to the BDB 2025 `pass_forward` → `pass_arrived` medians (the flight table in
`scripts/diag/flight/`): 0.27 + 0.0173·d + 0.00062·d², with the arm term kept. The old fit came from the 2026 data's
output frames, over all targeted passes and to the pass's end, so it ran 0.15–0.3 s long at 10–35 yd. Its only consumer
is the to-spot throw, so the refit is behind this flag.

What remains:
- the flight is 0.55 s against 0.8: the lead is estimated in a straight line;
- the line's release (1 released against 3, Flex);
- the yards after the catch.

- **Geometry, before the sell fix.** NFL tunnel catchers line up 15.8 yd from the ball and catch it about where they lined up. Ours line up at
  13.2 yd (the lab's gun doubles) and the `tunnel` route brings them 2.9 yd further inside, so the catch is in the
  rushers' lane. That is the route's geometry and the line's release (the screen set), not the QB.
- **A route-timed lead was tried and dropped.** Timing the receiver along the installed route instead of in a straight
  line changed nothing (35 against 36 caught), so it is not in this change.

**Dead quick screens, before the tunnel fixes.** The tunnel throwaways below happened while he was still pumping it. With
the fixes he throws it at 1.8 s, before it can go dead.

The Now-screen case at the outer bound: the catcher was set 16 yd wide, nobody was within 2.5 yd and the lane was
empty, but his evaluation priced the window under 0.2. The off corner was 3.6 yd away and closing on a 0.9 s flight.
- This is the one throwaway on an uncovered catcher.
- It comes from his ordinary window judgment at the outer bound, not from the dead read.
- It is kept here as an adverse case.

**Merely late and uncovered.**
- The read never fired on a catcher with no defender near and an empty lane.
- Every tunnel catcher is late by design (a 1.1 s sell). The 69 tunnels that were not dead were not thrown away.

**Slow screens.** The dead read is quick-screen only, so the slow-screen numbers stand as measured. Screen lab, 8 seeds
× 4 calls, off → on (NFL in brackets):

| | RB slip screen | TE screen |
|---|---|---|
| Never thrown | 25% → 6% | 34% → 22% |
| Flight | 1.0 → 0.6 s (0.5) | 0.75 → 0.5 s (0.5) |
| Throw time | 2.6 → 2.45 s (2.3) | 3.15 → 2.5 s (2.4) |

RB screens are caught 63% (NFL 83%). The misses are let-through rushers reaching the QB:
- Our line is about 1 yd deeper than the NFL's at the throw.
- It stays engaged at 1.5–2.0 s (90% against 72%).

That is the screen set, now assigned to the concepts and executed by Flex (option a); it is not in this PR.

## Not in this PR (deferred)

- **WR-screen line releases are deferred as cosmetic.** NFL linemen on WR screens release at about the throw, but they
  are 10–12 yd from the catcher and in the convoy on only 16–21% of snaps, so they rarely change the play.
- **The screen set** for RB and TE screens: the shallow set, the lineman's own count, the release. That is an
  assignment in the concepts, executed by Flex's line state.
- **The tunnel's remaining gaps:** the flight (the straight-line lead) and the line's release (Flex).
- **TE-screen yards:** 3.0 when caught (NFL 9).
