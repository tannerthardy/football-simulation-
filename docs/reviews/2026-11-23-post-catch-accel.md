# Speed after the catch: NFL vs ours, and what limits it (measurement and reading, no build)

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-23, for Integration (assignment A, "dudes too wide open": do receivers get away after the catch faster than they should, or slower?). Positions only. NFL = BDB 2025 weeks 1-9 (136 games), 2022 season. Ours = the 24-game census set (seeds 101 games 0, 2, 4, 6 and seeds 202, 303, 404, 505 x 4 games) on develop at engine-91 (0226f0d3): **default**, and **stride** = `CATCH_STRIDE=1` (patch `claude/quirky-franklin-9lbwbp`, `docs/reviews/patches/catch-stride-0226f0d3.diff`). No engine change here.

Operator (`scripts/diag/census/post_catch_accel.py`, one code path on both sides): speed = centred 0.2 s difference of position; rows are completions with the next 2.0 s of the play recorded and an open field (no defender within 1.5 yd from the catch to +2.0 s); bins by speed at the catch and by the turn between the velocity at the catch and 1.0 s later; gain = s(c + k) - s(c); peak acceleration = largest (s(f+3) - s(f-3))/0.6 s for f from c+0.3 s to c+1.7 s (so it spans c to c+2.0 s); t90 = time to 90% of the top speed reached in the 2 s. Rows: NFL 292 | default 191 (shift 3) / 151 (shift 0) | stride 280 / 231.

**Catch frame.** The sim logs a catch at the end of the securing window (the ball is in the receiver's hands for 0.12-0.45 s before the catch, `src/sim/catching.ts:302`); the NFL's `pass_arrived` is the ball arriving. `--shift 3` moves the sim's catch back 3 frames (0.3 s) to line them up; the tables below are shift 3 unless said. The shift changes the picture a lot (it is the whole finding), so both are in `data/reviews/census/accel-final-sh{0,3}.md`.

## What the NFL does

Mean speed of an open-field receiver, from 0.5 s before the catch (yd/s): 4.95, 4.98 (-0.3 s), 5.04 (-0.1 s), **5.11 (catch)**, 5.32 (+0.2 s), 5.45, 5.75 (+0.5 s), 6.47 (+1.0 s), 7.01 (+1.5 s), 7.13 (+2.0 s). **Monotonic: no dip, no pause at the catch**; he is already speeding up before it. Caught at 2-6 yd/s the same: 3.83 → 4.15 → 4.99 → 5.88 → 6.57 → 6.75.

Gain after the catch by speed at the catch (NFL, 0.5 / 1.0 / 2.0 s): 0-2 yd/s +1.55 / +3.19 / +5.11 (peak 4.9 yd/s², 90% of top by 1.50 s, top 6.5); 2-4 +1.03 / +2.16 / +3.37 (3.4; 1.27 s; 6.8); 4-6 +0.67 / +1.38 / +1.98 (2.2; 0.98 s; 7.4); 6+ +0.15 / +0.39 / +0.45 (1.1; 0.49 s; 8.5). Headroom closed by 1.0 s about 0.55-0.62 in every bin. By turn: under 30° +0.76 / +1.42 (s0 5.6), 30-90° +0.48 / +1.14 (5.0), over 90° +0.76 / +2.46 (s0 1.7; he is nearly stopped). By group at 1.0 s: back +1.46, TE +1.63, WR +1.16 (s0 4.9 / 4.9 / 5.4; peak 2.3-2.4 and t90 0.81-1.08 s).

The NFL does show a small cost of catching, against a handoff carrier in the same speed bin: at 4-6 yd/s the carrier gains +1.04 / +1.69 at 0.5 / 1.0 s (receiver +0.67 / +1.38); at 2-4 +1.42 / +2.45 (receiver +1.03 / +2.16). About 0.3-0.4 yd/s less at 0.5 s and 0.3 at 1.0 s, gone by 2 s. It is confounded (the receiver reads the defense and turns, the carrier runs to a hole he already sees), and it is a lower gain, not a loss of speed.

## What ours does

Mean speed, default (shift 3): 4.70 (-0.5 s), 4.79, 4.86, **4.74 (arrival)**, **4.13 (+0.2 s), 4.02 (+0.3 s)**, 4.53 (+0.5 s), 5.70 (+1.0 s), 6.64 (+1.5 s), 7.08 (+2.0 s). **He loses about 0.7-0.8 yd/s within 0.3 s of the ball arriving and is back to his arrival speed at +0.5 s.** Against the NFL curve he is 0.4 / 1.2 / 1.4 / 1.2 / 0.8 / 0.4 / 0.05 yd/s behind at 0 / 0.2 / 0.3 / 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 s; integrating the difference, **about 1.4 yd over the 2 s** (a trapezoid on two mean curves; about 1 yd of it is the dip, since he also arrives 0.25 yd/s slower at -0.5 s). By 2.0 s he is at the NFL's speed (7.08 vs 7.13), so the top speed is not the limit.

By bin, gain at 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 s: 0-2 yd/s +0.84 / +3.20 / +4.77 / +5.35 (NFL +1.55 / +3.19 / +4.42 / +5.11); 2-4 -0.36 / +1.36 / +2.66 / +3.39 (NFL +1.03 / +2.16 / +3.09 / +3.37); 4-6 -0.15 / +0.80 / +1.66 / +2.08 (NFL +0.67 / +1.38 / +1.87 / +1.98); 6+ -0.74 / -0.01 / +0.61 / +0.85 (NFL +0.15 / +0.39 / +0.55 / +0.45). **A receiver who is already moving (2+ yd/s) is slower at +0.5 s and +1.0 s than he was at the catch, or about even; the NFL's is faster by 0.7-1.0 yd/s at 0.5 s.** Headroom closed by 1.0 s: ours 0.36 / 0.37 / 0.13 (4-6, 2-4, 6+) vs NFL 0.62 / 0.55 / 0.53. t90: 1.29 vs 0.98 s (4-6), 0.89 vs 0.49 s (6+).

**Caught from rest or near it (0-2 yd/s) he matches the NFL by 1.0 s** (+3.20 vs +3.19) and his peak acceleration is slightly higher (5.4 vs 4.9 yd/s², all bins have ours at or above the NFL's: 4.1 vs 3.4, 2.7 vs 2.2, 2.1 vs 1.1; the peaks come after the dip, a rebound, not a standing start). So **the body's acceleration is not the limit: his rebound is as strong as the NFL's.** What is missing is not accelerating after the ball, it is not slowing at the ball.

With shift 0 (catch as logged, after the securing window) the dip is already inside the pre-catch mean (4.86 at -0.5 s, 4.20 at -0.1 s, 4.11 at the catch) and after it he gains faster than the NFL (4-6: +1.94 / +2.73 at 1.0 / 2.0 s vs +1.38 / +1.98), which is the same dip seen from the other end. A reader who aligns on the logged catch would conclude "ours accelerates more than the NFL's"; aligned on ball arrival, he loses speed first.

Turn: under 30° ours +0.86 at 1.0 s (NFL +1.42, t90 1.22 vs 0.80); 30-90° +0.89 (NFL +1.14); over 90° at 2-4 yd/s -1.95 at 0.5 s vs the NFL's +0.88 at 30-90° (n 10 vs 6, thin). By group at 1.0 s: back +1.21 (NFL +1.46), TE +0.53 (+1.63), WR +0.98 (+1.16). TE is the worst row, n 42.

**CATCH_STRIDE removes the dip and over-corrects slightly.** Mean speed: 4.82 → 4.86 (arrival) → 4.89 (+0.2 s) → 5.05 → 5.58 (+0.5 s) → 6.46 (+1.0 s) → 7.14 → 7.49: within about 0.3 yd/s of the NFL at every point to 1.0 s and equal at 1.0 s (6.46 vs 6.47); after that he keeps going to 7.49 (NFL 7.13) and his top speed is high (WR 8.0 vs 7.7, TE 7.2 vs 7.6, back 7.8 vs 7.3). Gains at 0.5 / 1.0 s: 4-6 +0.70 / +1.48 (NFL +0.67 / +1.38); 2-4 +0.76 / +2.15 (NFL +1.03 / +2.16); 0-2 +2.04 / +4.00 (NFL +1.55 / +3.19: too fast from rest); 6+ +0.34 / +0.68 (NFL +0.15 / +0.39). Over 90° it is worse than default (-1.30 at 0.5 s, n 10). Stride has 280 usable rows against 191 default (the open-field filter keeps more of them, probably because he runs away from the nearest defender); the sample changes as well as the speed, and I did not check why.

## Handoff control

The same operator on a ball carrier after a handoff (the first frame he has the ball; NFL 603 rows, ours 70, 24 games: open field at the handoff is rare in ours). NFL carrier gains at 0.5 / 1.0 s: 0-2 yd/s +1.91 / +3.63; 2-4 +1.42 / +2.45; 4-6 +1.04 / +1.69; top speed 7.2-7.7. Ours: 2-4 +1.30 / +2.31 (n 20; top speed 6.1); 4-6 +0.25 / +0.37 (n 46; top speed 6.5). **Ours has no dip at the handoff** (his gain is positive at 0.5 s in every bin) but he does not accelerate to the NFL carrier's speed: 6.5 vs 7.7 yd/s top at 4-6. This is not the receiver's problem and is not explained here: I have not diagnosed it: I did not look at the handoff's own timing (whether the handoff has a securing window) or at the carrier's speed request. Treat the control as "no dip at the handoff", not as a speed comparison.

## What limits a receiver's acceleration and turn after a catch, from the code

1. **The securing window and the ball-tracking brake (the dip).** With CATCH_SECURE (on by default since the C14 work; `catching.ts:298-305`) the ball is pinned to the receiver's hands for `secureT = clamp((0.22 + 0.3·diff)·(1.25 - 0.5·hands), 0.12, 0.45)` s, and the receiver stays in TrackBall. In TrackBall (`src/sim/behaviors/receiver.ts:981`) `if (dist < 0.5) return { vel: { x: a.vel.x * 0.5, y: a.vel.y * 0.5 }, facing }`: with the ball within half a yard the requested velocity is half his current velocity, every tick. Pinned in his hands is within half a yard for the whole window, so he is asked to halve his velocity each tick through the window; the body (the same friction and force limits as every sprint) follows with its own deceleration. That reproduces the dip (loss 0.7-0.8 yd/s in 0.2-0.3 s from about 4.8 yd/s, longer secure times for harder catches) and is released at the catch, after which he runs the normal sprint start from there (as strongly as the NFL's).
2. **The catch itself is a decision, not a body limit.** After the catch the receiver's request comes from the carrier controller; with CATCH_EYES he runs the carrier line for one reaction time, and with CATCH_STRIDE (afterCatch) the request is full vmax upfield away from the nearest seen defender from the first tick, with the catch point chosen by the speed it needs. Default has neither, so the request after the window is the normal carrier request (same thing every ball carrier follows); that is why the rebound looks normal.
3. **No encumbrance or head coupling.** The body has no cost for holding the ball, for the head being turned back to find it, or for a catching posture: acceleration, friction and turn limits are the same as for an unencumbered player. The only ball-related loss in speed is item 1.
4. **Start force and turning.** `locomotionAccel`'s start-force law is the same for all players from rest; a receiver caught at 0-2 yd/s rebounds as the NFL's does (peak acceleration 5.4 vs 4.9; the 1.0 s gains match). Turn: the controller is the carrier line, and what limits a turn through 90° is the shared body model, not catch logic; the one thin row (over 90° at 2-4, -1.95 at 0.5 s) is the dip again plus a turn, not a separate cost I can isolate at this n.
5. **DIR_START (#850, preloaded start) is not in develop** and was not tested: it concerns the first step from rest, and the receiver from rest already matches by 1.0 s, so it is not what these rows say is missing.

## Does the NFL show a real transition cost ours lacks?

No dip: the NFL receiver is faster at +0.2 s than at arrival (5.32 vs 5.11) and continues to rise. The only NFL cost is a smaller gain than a handoff carrier in the same speed bin (about 0.3-0.4 yd/s at 0.5 s), small and confounded. Ours has a cost the NFL does not have: speed drops 0.7-0.8 yd/s for 0.3 s and he is ~1.4 yd behind the NFL curve after 2 s. So the answer to "do we leave them too open after the catch" in the sense of speed away from the defender is no: by the first second the receiver is more likely to be caught by his defender in ours than in the NFL (his speed is lower for 0.5 s). The census's wide-open and flat-YAC excess is not explained by a receiver that is too quick after the catch.

## Caveats

- Aligning on ball arrival uses a 3-frame shift picked from the CATCH_SECURE window range (0.12-0.45 s, typically ~0.3 s); the shape of the dip changes with the shift (shift 0 hides it in the pre-catch mean). The result survives either way as a mismatch with the NFL (shift 0: faster after the catch but slower before it).
- Open-field rows only (no defender within 1.5 yd for 2.0 s): 292 NFL completions; ours 191 default (stride 280), so default is less often in the open and the rows are not the same plays.
- Handoff control is thin (ours 70) and not diagnosed. Nothing here proposes a change.
