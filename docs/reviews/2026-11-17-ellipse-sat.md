# ELLIPSE_SAT: saturate each locomotion demand at its own limit before the friction ellipse (Tools, 2026-11-17, develop 6f1136ae, flag off)

Attempt (Integration, from the holder-brake diagnosis): in `locomotionAccel`, clamp the along-velocity demand to its limit (drive or brake) and the across-velocity demand to the lateral limit, then let the existing ellipse arbitrate. Full lateral plus full brake each get 1/sqrt(2) of their limit; the envelope is unchanged.

Result: negative on the gates that matter, so the flag stays off and is not proposed for a card.

* Premise check. The 78% (p50) / 42% (p75) figures in the diagnosis were over all upright holder ticks, including the 30% that ask for no braking. On the ticks that do ask to brake (request more than 1 yd/s under his speed, 828 ticks), the direction-preserving scaling already keeps 0.94 of the brake limit at the median (0.83 in the worst quartile). The over-asked lateral demand starves braking much less than described.
* Because the along demand is usually the larger one (about 54 yd/s^2 against a limit of 6.4, the lateral about 25 against 5.7), scaling the pair by one factor gives the larger demand nearly all of the envelope, which is the ellipse's own answer to "head straight for the wanted velocity". Saturating first equalizes the two and takes braking down to 1/sqrt(2) = 0.71 of its limit exactly. The flag therefore weakens braking where braking is the larger demand.

If the ellipse's arbitration should change, the physically right form is not component saturation. The ellipse is the set of forces the feet can give; the wanted change in velocity has a direction, and the force that best serves it is the largest force in that direction, which is what the present scaling does. A different arbitration would have to say which of the two demands the body gives up first when both are over their limits (braking before a turn, or a turn before braking), and that is a decision about the player, not the ground.
