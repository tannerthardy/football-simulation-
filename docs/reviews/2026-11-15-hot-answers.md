# What a real install gives a free rusher where our concepts have no hot (design note, 2026-11-15)

Rules + Coaching, for the QB lane via Integration. Design only: nothing here is built.

**The finding this answers.** The QB lane found several concepts with no hot answer on the free rusher's side: post_cross, trips_outs, pa_post_dig, pa_shot, and the screens.
- Their hot rule (`BLITZ_READ`, `qb.ts`): his eyes go to the quickest-breaking route on the free man's side that breaks within 1.6 s, else the back if he is out on a route.

## What coaches teach (sources checked against the page unless marked)

1. **Throw hot when they bring more than you protect.**
   - Shanahan: "When people blitz more than you have, you've got to throw hot" (Rich, West Coast Football, 2024-11-10).
   - The same piece: choice routes give "an easy outlet versus the blitz", and "tight ends chipping in pass protection" are hot targets.
2. **The protection slides, and the unblocked man is the quarterback's.**
   - Against six at the line, "the usual response in protection is to revert back into a half slide", then either "make the quarterback responsible for beating an unblockable defender" or "throwing hot to an underneath route" (Diante Lee, PFF, 2022-02-25).
   - A full slide can "leave the weak edge unblocked for the quarterback to control with his arms and legs" (same).
3. **Replacement and sight adjustment** (Bill Hewitt, USA Football, 8 rules for sight adjustment):
   - "Teach replacement";
   - "Always make adjustments to the receiver's side";
   - against a safety blitz with no audible, "only a quick three- to five-step drop";
   - "The quarterback must release the ball by the third step whenever possible".
   - Unverified (search summary): the receiver replaces the area the blitzer came from, and that route is automatically hot.
4. **Some installs protect instead of throwing hot.**
   - A playbook on Scribd: "We will protect, rather than throw 'hot' against the blitz in most situations" (unverified: not fetched).
   - Max protection for shots keeps the back and tight ends in, with three routes (game guides and a paywalled Colts piece only: unverified).
5. **Screens are themselves the answer to pressure.** The QB's rule on a screen against a blitz (Tom Karlo, Nike 2016 Coach of the Year Clinic Notes, on Coaches Insider):
   - "Quarterback has to get depth against blitz";
   - "If there is a blitzing linebacker, the quarterback will have to sink deeper";
   - "Get the ball there any way possible (sidearm, jump, bend, etc.)";
   - when covered, the line turns up for "the most dangerous man", and "When in doubt give it to the running back".

## Design (for the QB lane; not built)

- **A protection-aware plan.** Each concept states whether it is hot-protected (a quick-game route on each side meant to be thrown by the third step) or protection-first (numbers in, no designed hot).
  - The compiler can read it from the install: backs and tight ends kept in, and the routes' break times against the drop.
  - It is the coordinator's install, not a geometric inference.
- **The hot bar by the drop.** A route is hot if he can throw it by the drop's third step (Hewitt's rule 8), so the bar depends on gun / three-step / five-step, instead of 1.6 s for every drop.
- **Protection-first concepts.** A seen free rusher sends him to his existing answers: beat the man (evade, PFF's "arms and legs"), or get rid of the ball (throwaway). That is the coached rule, not a missing hot.
- **Screens.** No hot. A blitzer in his face changes his drop depth and release (Karlo), and that is the QB lane's.
