# Are our tracks jumpier than the NFL's: real body jitter or the 10 Hz export? (measurement and reading; no change)

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-24, for Defense (speed roughness p50 2.69 against 1.76 yd/s²). `scripts/diag/body/jitter.py` (the export grid, NFL against ours, one operator) and `sim_jitter.mts` (a read-only tap on every 0.05 s tick of 2 games, 64,000 open-movement player-ticks); tables `data/reviews/census/jitter.md`, `jitter-native.md`. Defense's own operator was not available to me and did not reproduce: on mine the medians are **NFL 1.79, ours 2.12** (their 1.76 and 2.69).

## Answer

**The extra roughness is not a sampling artifact of the export, and it is not tick-level jitter either: it is larger real accelerations, a smaller effect than 2.69 against 1.76, and the export's 0.01 yd rounding hides any finer difference.**

- Operator A = |speed(k+1) - speed(k)| / 0.1 s on the forward 0.1 s speeds, players moving 2 yd/s or more with nobody within 1.5 yd. On the export grid: **p50 1.79 NFL | 2.12 ours, p90 4.12 | 5.00** (n 1.45 million | 356,000). A centred 0.2 s speed (C) gives the same: 1.70 | 2.11.
- **Our own tracks at full precision give the same A at every sampling:** 20 Hz native 2.33 / 5.15; 10 Hz, both tick phases, full precision 2.19 / 4.83; rounded to 0.01 yd (what the export is) 2.18 / 5.00 and 2.17 / 5.00; positions averaged over each tick pair and rounded 2.17 / 5.00. The two phases agree, rounding changes nothing, smoothing by pair means changes nothing: A is an acceleration the body really has, not an aliasing of a faster oscillation into the 10 Hz grid. If our 20 Hz physics had a tick-to-tick flutter, the 10 Hz even and odd phases would disagree and the pair-averaged track would be smoother; they are not.
- **The second difference, which removes a steady acceleration, is small at full precision (B p50 0.22 at 20 Hz, 0.43 at 10 Hz, p90 1.2-2.2): our speed changes are smooth.** Rounded to 0.01 yd it becomes 1.04 / 3.00, and the NFL's is 1.08 / 2.81. A position rounded to 0.01 yd is a speed rounded to 0.1 yd/s at 0.1 s, so the second difference of a perfectly smooth track measures about 1.0 yd/s² of pure quantisation: **B cannot separate the two samples** (both sit on the quantisation floor), and A's medians (1.8-2.2) are about twice the floor, which is why A can.
- **So what is real: our bodies change speed by about 0.35 yd/s² more per 0.1 s (median), 0.9 more at p90, than the NFL's tracked ones.** The heading change per 0.1 s at 3 yd/s and over: p50 1.7 NFL | 2.2 ours, **p90 5.1 | 8.0 degrees**. A larger part of the difference is the tail: ours turns and brakes in steps more often. The likely source is controller steps (a re-aimed request, the cut law, a plant, a target switch), the thing `test/dither-budget.test.ts` bounds, rather than a 20 Hz flutter of the integrator; I did not attribute it to a stage here (`scripts/diag/effective-mass/dither-core.ts` does that).
- What I cannot say: how much of the NFL's smoothness is the provider's own filter (a smoothed track has lower A and B; real athletes are not smoother than the tracker shows), so the NFL's roughness is a lower bound on the real thing and the gap to ours is, if anything, an upper bound. The medians of 1.8 against 2.1-2.2 could be partly the provider's smoothing (not measured here); the p90 tail (heading 5 against 8 degrees, A 4.1 against 5.0) is the larger and more plausible difference.

Defense's 2.69 comes from something I did not reproduce (perhaps frames in contact or a different speed filter); the ordering, ours rougher, holds on mine, the size does not.
