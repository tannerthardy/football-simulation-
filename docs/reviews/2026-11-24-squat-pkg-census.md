## Reading

The package alone does what Flex built it for on the flat (open flats per game 2.04 -> 1.17, open-flat YAC 27 -> 20, the NFL's 1.50 and 17) and at the same time sinks the corner into the flat, shallower than the default in every cell (Cover 2 corner at 3.0 s 4.1-4.8 against the NFL's 8.9-9.9), which opens the 10-15 yd window to .615 in Cover 2. Adding the sink restores the corner's depth and position at #1's break (on top .42 and .32) and cuts the window to .316 and .152, but gives the flat back (2.08 open per game, open-flat YAC 28.4). The A-B corner split is at the NFL's in Cover 2 (0.8 against 1.0). Neither arm has the NFL's combination: the package without the sink has the flat and not the window, the package with the sink the window (closer) and not the flat. The per-throw rates do not separate the arms (completion .746 | .748 | .757, PBU .097 | .095 | .089); Cover 2 interceptions fall to 1 in package+sink and Cover 3 rise to 10, cells of 1-10 events.

## Caveats

- The default arm is the d148cd598/d0bafcc18 default (SQUAT_SINK off), reused on Flex's statement that the package flags off are bit-identical; the package arms are new draws. Each arm one 24-game draw: shares of about .04 and about 1.5 points per throw are inside the noise.
- The A/B split is from positions at 1.5 s, not assignments; corner-depth cells at 3.0 s have n 54-342 (Cover 2 A in package+sink n 54); the "two on one flat" definition is mine (landmarks within 9 yd, within 6 yd, man on the catcher) and may not be Flex's.
