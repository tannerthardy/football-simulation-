## Supported open football problems

These are priorities supported by saved cases, not estimated population rates.

1. **Technique can disappear or become contradictory in command composition.**
   #172 repairs selected runoff losing its tangent. The drive correction below
   prevents a fixed push past a man who has moved level. Neither changes strength.
2. **Loaded contact can protect the wrong route through runner arrival.** The
   strong #172 case moves the defender more, yet exposes him to the runner sooner.
   Blocker leverage, momentum and the runner's acquired choice must be compatible;
   more lateral distance or transmitted force is insufficient.
3. **Useful first-level work does not ensure attainable second-level work.**
   Joined release needs keeper security, an available exit and collection before
   the runner arrives. Earlier release, a shorter double or a nominal backer job
   cannot prove that. Preserve #164's held earned-departure failure and #172's
   Tite limitation; do not treat them as proof that every long double is wrong.
4. **Defensive counterplay and finishing remain separate dependencies.** Renewed
   stalks, work-off, pursuit leverage and contact outcomes have their own owners
   (#169/#170 and movement research). Correct offensive access can expose those
   limits. Capping runners or undoing lawful blocking is not their correction.

The current evidence does not establish a universal OL strength deficit or a
physical cap that should be raised. Historical/research NFL comparisons remain
source/operator-qualified; no new NFL query or calibration was performed.
