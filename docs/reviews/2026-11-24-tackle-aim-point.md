# Tackle aim point: the taught rule, by approach (2026-11-24, Rules + Coaching)

For Run Game's HOLD_AIM. This doc gives the rule as coaches teach it, the cues a tackler can see to choose between its
cases, and what has been measured. Sources are paraphrased below. Exact quotes and checked status are in my message to
Run Game, and the URLs are at the end. Nothing is built here.

## The rule

The tackler chooses his aim from one observable: **where he will meet the carrier relative to the carrier's direction
of travel.** That is the bearing of the tackler from the carrier, measured in the carrier's frame (ahead / side /
behind). The tackler reads it from what he sees of the carrier's path and body, not from the carrier's intent.

| meeting the carrier from | aim (first contact) | with | then | sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| in front (head-on or a shallow angle, the carrier coming at him) | near thigh, the shoulder on the side he is meeting | head outside the body (to the side), arms squeeze the thighs or legs | leg drive through, or a hit-lift-roll on a quick, shifty runner | the Seahawks-style handout; World Rugby front-on; Connacht; Quinn |
| from the side (pursuit angle, carrier crossing in front of him) | near hip or thigh; a profile (side-on) tackle sits higher, at the near jersey number | head behind the carrier (the "head across" cue is the older, now-discouraged version) | squeeze and drive or roll; finish on top | the handout's profile tackle; World Rugby side-on; Quinn |
| from behind (chasing) | hips or thighs once within diving reach; at full stretch, a tap of the feet or ankles with an arm | head to one side | pull the arms in, slide down his body | World Rugby (tackle from behind; the tap) |
| to control the ball (carrier upright, ball exposed, close range) | the ball, between waist and chest | both arms wrap to trap his arms and the ball | drive forward | World Rugby (smother tackle) |

**Default.** In every case the default height is the thigh or the hip line: the "strike zone", from the bottom of the
thigh pads to the base of the chest. Coaches reject contact to the head and below the thigh. Low leg contact is a
last-resort reach from behind.

**What finishes it.** The sources treat the leg squeeze plus the leg drive (or the roll) as part of the tackle. A hold
at the waist with no squeeze of the legs and no drive is the case they coach against: the carrier's legs stay free.
That bears on the finish gap.

## Cues the tackler can see, to choose the case

- **The carrier's velocity and the tackler's own line to him** (bearing and closing). These decide front, side or
  behind.
- **The distance left.** Within diving reach from behind: hips or legs. Beyond it: the tap.
- **Whether the carrier is upright and the ball exposed at close range.** This is the smother option. The rugby sources
  give its purpose (stopping the offload); I found no football source for a strip or a high tackle.
- **Not a cue:** the carrier's intended cut or his assignment. The aim is executed with error, and the error comes from
  the tackler's own speed, balance and range.

## Measured

- **Hendricks et al. 2025** (51,106 rugby tackles, video-coded by first contact): for active shoulder tackles at senior
  elite level, success was 86% at the shoulder, 82% at the mid-torso and 78% at the leg. Arm tackles succeeded 75 / 69 /
  65%. **The type of tackle (an active shoulder with a wrap, against an arm tackle) moves success more than the height
  does.**
  - Caveat: rugby counts a prevented offload as success, which favours high contact more than football would.
- **Lowered tackle-height trials** (Scottish community rugby, 2025): tacklers bent at the waist more and made fewer
  contacts above the sternum. Neither study I read reports success or breaks by height.
- **Not found:** a football measurement of contact height against breaks, a football rule for a bigger vs a smaller
  back (search summaries only: a low wrap against big men, from close range), and anything on going high near the
  sideline or to strip the ball.
