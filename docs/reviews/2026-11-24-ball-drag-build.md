# BALL_DRAG (off): the spiral's drag constant from the NFL ball's measured deceleration

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-24, for Integration (go given 2026-11-24 after `2026-11-24-ball-drag-reconciliation.md`, which carries the measurement and its intervals; the QB lane revised its own number to the same value in `2026-11-23-ball-speed.md`). Flag `BALL_DRAG=1`, off by default; `src/physics/ball.ts`. Off, `DRAG_SPIRAL` is the same 0.003027 and nothing moves.

## What it is

`DRAG_SPIRAL` (`k` in a = -k |v_rel| v_rel, per yard) is 0.0050 /yd with the flag on, against 0.003027. It is a **measured physical quantity of the ball**, not a fitted outcome: BDB 2025 weeks 1-9 tracking, the horizontal speed of each 0.1 s interval of a pass, release frames and the last 0.6 s dropped, k = -d ln(speed)/d(distance) within each pass, by air-distance band (20-35 yd 0.0057 (0.0050-0.0065), 35+ yd 0.0047 (0.0043-0.0051); pooled 0.0045-0.0057). On the rulebook ball (end-on area 0.0226-0.0232 m², mass 0.397-0.425 kg, rho 1.2) k = 0.0306 C_d per yd, so 0.005 is C_d about 0.16 (0.15-0.19) against the 0.099 the engine carried. The literature does not fix it (Watts and Moore, Am. J. Phys. 71:791-793, 2003: 0.05-0.06 on a smooth foam ball, about one-third of Rae and Streit's; Price, Moss and Gay, Am. J. Phys. 88:704-710, doi 10.1119/10.0001388, on how the axis follows the path), so the in-situ deceleration is the constraint. No attitude law: one k, the average over the throw. Kicks (0.0042) and the tumbling ball (0.006) are unchanged.

Method and its limits are in the reconciliation note: a path-averaged C_d·A, wind and air density not separated, the vertical component inferred from 2-D speed.

## Checks

1. **Hang time and apex by distance are unchanged** (`solveLaunch` re-derives the release for the QB's flight time T(d); lone ball, `scripts/diag/ball/drag-bench.mts`, `data/reviews/ball-drag/bench.md`): apex height and the time to it are identical off and on at 8-50 yd (e.g. 30 yd: apex 4.25 | 4.24 yd at 0.67 s both; 40 yd 6.78 at 0.95 s both); the ball is at the catch point at T within 0.03 yd. The launch angle moves by 0.1-0.9 degrees (30 yd: 18.9 | 18.6).
2. **Release speed rises 0.2-1.2 yd/s** on the lone ball (3-D: 16 yd 21.8 -> 22.1, 25 yd 22.5 -> 23.1, 40 yd 23.8 -> 24.7, 50 yd 25.2 -> 26.4) and **arrival speed falls 0.3-0.9** (25 yd 21.3 -> 20.8, 40 yd 21.7 -> 20.9). **In actual games (6 games a side, same seeds; `release_tap.mts`) the median horizontal release moves much less:** 10-20 yd air 23.2 -> 23.3 yd/s (n 311 | 341), 20-35 yd 23.1 -> 23.1 (n 139 | 156), against the NFL's tracked 22.5 and 22.7. So the game's releases are already at or above the tracked ones in both arms (0.4-0.8 yd/s over), the drag change does not bring them closer, and the QB's own scaling (his arm, the touch option, the flight time he picks) decides the release more than k does. I did not trace why the lone-ball +0.3-0.6 shrinks to +0.0-0.1 in games (n is 140-340 per cell, the median's noise is about 0.2); it is not a result about drag.
3. **Arrival speed and the 24 yd/s step at the catch roll, from an actual run** (8 games a side, seeds 101-108, a read-only tap on the roll; `catch_tap.mts`, `drag_cards.py`, `data/reviews/ball-drag/cards.md`; target's first roll): 3-D speed p10 / p50 / p90 19.5 / 22.5 / 27.1 -> 19.2 / 21.9 / 26.6 yd/s; **the share over 24 yd/s 28.9% -> 23.7%** (not the halving the lone ball suggested: most rolls are on short throws, which lose little). By time in the air: under 0.8 s 38% -> 32%, 0.8-1.2 s 18% -> 15%, 1.2-1.8 s 7% -> 2%.
4. **Completion, drop, breakup and interception, eight games a side** (same seeds; the plays diverge once the ball arrives at a different time): completions 77.8% -> 75.6% of throws (495 | 510 of 636 | 675), incompletions without a break-up 13.4 -> 13.5%, **pass breakups 7.1 -> 9.3% (45 | 63)**, interceptions 1.7 -> 1.6% (11 | 11). Uncontested drops (first roll, nobody in the contest radius, not deflected): 61 of 504 (12.1%) -> 49 of 513 (9.6%); the roll's mean pCatch is the same, 0.899 both. What the roll can explain: the 24 yd/s term is +0.08 on the difficulty, and with 5 points fewer rolls above it the expected drop change is about 0.4 points (0.05 x 0.08 x about 1.3-hands); the observed 2.5 points (about 1.2 standard errors on 510 rolls) is mostly sampling, not drag. The breakup rise (about 2 standard errors) and the completion fall are of the same order as the noise of 8 games and I do not read them as a drag effect; they are a prompt to look at a larger sample before promotion, not a finding. Not tuned toward any outcome.
5. **Tests.** `test/ball-drag.test.ts` (off by default, 0.0050 on with C_d in 0.15-0.19 on the rulebook ball, kicks and tumbles unchanged, same arc and flight time with a faster release and slower arrival); `engine-pin` / `reference-stack` / `scorecard-provenance` registry tests pass with the flag registered off. red-diff on the files that touch the ball or the registry (`ball-drag`, `ball-estimate`, `native-pass-foul-game`, `pass-contact-rules`, `physics`, `engine-pin-manifest`, `reference-stack`, `scorecard-provenance`, `dither-budget`): 61 run, **new red 0, fixed 0**; the same ball tests with `BALL_DRAG=1` also pass (41). **The full core red-diff was not run**: with the flag off the constant is byte-identical, and the on arm changes the ball's path, which the full set would report as red only where a recorded case pins a flight; that is a promotion check.

## What the NFL ball does that this does not

- **The decay doubles 0.3-0.6 s before arrival**, for completions and incompletions alike (14.0 | 13.7 x1000 per yd against 6-7 in the 0.6-1.8 s before it); in the last 0.3 s completions double again (26.1 against 11.0, the catch). Not drag. It could be tracker smoothing near the catch (a smoothed end of a track lowers the speed there), or the ball's nose-over at the end of the arc (its attitude leaves the path and it presents more area); the data cannot tell, and **it is not modelled**: it is the reason the last 0.6 s are left out of k.
- **The catch roll's 24 yd/s step is not what the NFL ball avoids on short throws.** The NFL ball is over 24 yd/s 0.6-0.8 s before arrival on 30-41% of throws under 20 yd (p50 22.8-23.4); drag at 0.005 takes our share from 29% to 24% and leaves most of the step in place. Whether the roll should price ball speed that way, and the speed the ball has at the hands, belong to the catch rebuild, not to drag.

## Not claimed

That 0.0050 is the final constant (the interval is 0.0045-0.0057 and shorter bands are noisier), that any outcome rate improves, or that the throw's release matches the tracked releases (it does not; see check 2). The NFL's estimate of speed near the catch is untouched.
