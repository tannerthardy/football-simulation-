# BALL_DRAG (off): the spiral's drag constant from the NFL ball's measured deceleration

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-24, for Integration (go given 2026-11-24 after `2026-11-24-ball-drag-reconciliation.md`, which carries the measurement and its intervals; the QB lane revised its own number to the same value in `2026-11-23-ball-speed.md`). Flag `BALL_DRAG=1`, off by default; `src/physics/ball.ts`. Off, `DRAG_SPIRAL` is the same 0.003027 and nothing moves.

## What it is

`DRAG_SPIRAL` (`k` in a = -k |v_rel| v_rel, per yard) is 0.0050 /yd with the flag on, against 0.003027. It is a **measured physical quantity of the ball**, not a fitted outcome: BDB 2025 weeks 1-9 tracking, the horizontal speed of each 0.1 s interval of a pass, release frames and the last 0.6 s dropped, k = -d ln(speed)/d(distance) within each pass, by air-distance band (20-35 yd 0.0057 (0.0050-0.0065), 35+ yd 0.0047 (0.0043-0.0051); pooled 0.0045-0.0057). On the rulebook ball (end-on area 0.0226-0.0232 m², mass 0.397-0.425 kg, rho 1.2) k = 0.0306 C_d per yd, so 0.005 is C_d about 0.16 (0.15-0.19) against the 0.099 the engine carried. The literature does not fix it (Watts and Moore, Am. J. Phys. 71:791-793, 2003: 0.05-0.06 on a smooth foam ball, about one-third of Rae and Streit's; Price, Moss and Gay, Am. J. Phys. 88:704-710, doi 10.1119/10.0001388, on how the axis follows the path), so the in-situ deceleration is the constraint. No attitude law: one k, the average over the throw. Kicks (0.0042) and the tumbling ball (0.006) are unchanged.

Method and its limits are in the reconciliation note: a path-averaged C_d·A, wind and air density not separated, the vertical component inferred from 2-D speed.

## Checks

1. **Hang time and apex by distance are unchanged** (`solveLaunch` re-derives the release for the QB's flight time T(d); lone ball, `scripts/diag/ball/drag-bench.mts`, `data/reviews/ball-drag/bench.md`): apex height and the time to it are identical off and on at 8-50 yd (e.g. 30 yd: apex 4.25 | 4.24 yd at 0.67 s both; 40 yd 6.78 at 0.95 s both); the ball is at the catch point at T within 0.03 yd. The launch angle moves by 0.1-0.9 degrees (30 yd: 18.9 | 18.6).
2. **Release speed rises 0.2-1.2 yd/s** on the lone ball (3-D: 16 yd 21.8 -> 22.1, 25 yd 22.5 -> 23.1, 40 yd 23.8 -> 24.7, 50 yd 25.2 -> 26.4) and **arrival speed falls 0.3-0.9** (25 yd 21.3 -> 20.8, 40 yd 21.7 -> 20.9). **In actual games (6 games a side, same seeds; `release_tap.mts`) the median horizontal release moves much less:** 10-20 yd air 23.2 -> 23.3 yd/s (n 311 | 341), 20-35 yd 23.1 -> 23.1 (n 139 | 156); I did not trace why the lone-ball +0.3-0.6 shrinks to +0.0-0.1 in games (n is 140-340 per cell, the median's noise is about 0.2; the QB's own scaling, the touch option and the flight time he picks decide the release more than k does). **Against the tracked releases the comparison depends on the frames used, and the first 0.2 s is the wrong window:** the tracked ball's first 0.1 s interval is bimodal (p10 1 yd/s, p90 41 yd/s: the hand, the release frame), so the first-0.2 s mean the QB lane's operator uses is 24.2 / 22.7 / 22.8 yd/s (10-20 / 20-35 / 35+ yd) and the reconciliation note's 22.5-22.8 is on a different pass selection that I did not reconcile with it; the clean intervals, 0.2-0.4 s after the release, are 23.3 / 22.1 / 21.4 yd/s, and carrying them back to the hand with the measured decay (k 0.005 over about 4 yd) gives about **23.7 / 22.5 / 21.9 at the release**. Ours, 23.2 and 23.1 (22.3 on 3 throws at 35+), is within about 0.5 yd/s of that either side, so the earlier reading that game releases were already above the tracked ones does not stand: they are about right, and k does not change that.
3. **Arrival speed and the 24 yd/s step at the catch roll, from an actual run** (8 games a side, seeds 101-108, a read-only tap on the roll; `scripts/diag/catch/catch_tap.mts` and `catch-tap.patch` from #866 (a local patch to `catching.ts`, not in this branch), `drag_cards.py`, `data/reviews/ball-drag/cards.md`; target's first roll): 3-D speed p10 / p50 / p90 19.5 / 22.5 / 27.1 -> 19.2 / 21.9 / 26.6 yd/s; **the share over 24 yd/s 28.9% -> 23.7%** (not the halving the lone ball suggested: most rolls are on short throws, which lose little). By time in the air: under 0.8 s 38% -> 32%, 0.8-1.2 s 18% -> 15%, 1.2-1.8 s 7% -> 2%.
4. **Completion, drop, breakup and interception, eight games a side** (same seeds; the plays diverge once the ball arrives at a different time): completions 77.8% -> 75.6% of throws (495 | 510 of 636 | 675), incompletions without a break-up 13.4 -> 13.5%, **pass breakups 7.1 -> 9.3% (45 | 63)**, interceptions 1.7 -> 1.6% (11 | 11). Uncontested drops (first roll, nobody in the contest radius, not deflected): 61 of 504 (12.1%) -> 49 of 513 (9.6%); the roll's mean pCatch is the same, 0.899 both. What the roll can explain: the 24 yd/s term is +0.08 on the difficulty, and with 5 points fewer rolls above it the expected drop change is a few tenths of a point (0.05 of rolls x 0.08 x (1.3 - hands) of the roll's drop weight); the observed 2.5 points (about 1.2 standard errors on 510 rolls) is mostly sampling, not drag. The breakup rise (about 2 standard errors) and the completion fall are of the same order as the noise of 8 games and I do not read them as a drag effect; they are a prompt to look at a larger sample before promotion, not a finding. Not tuned toward any outcome.
5. **Tests.** `test/ball-drag.test.ts` (off by default, 0.0050 on with C_d in 0.15-0.19 on the rulebook ball, kicks and tumbles unchanged, same arc and flight time with a faster release and slower arrival); `engine-pin` / `reference-stack` / `scorecard-provenance` registry tests pass with the flag registered off. red-diff on the files that touch the ball or the registry (`ball-drag`, `ball-estimate`, `native-pass-foul-game`, `pass-contact-rules`, `physics`, `engine-pin-manifest`, `reference-stack`, `scorecard-provenance`, `dither-budget`): 61 run, **new red 0, fixed 0**; the same ball tests with `BALL_DRAG=1` also pass (41). **The full core red-diff was not run**: with the flag off the constant is byte-identical, and the on arm changes the ball's path, which the full set would report as red only where a recorded case pins a flight; that is a promotion check.

## Designed runs (Integration's card: run yards per carry down in six of six seeds, stuffs up in five)

`DRAG_SPIRAL` is read in three places only: the QB's throw and throwaway (`qb.ts`) and the pass flight `playsim.ts` (`solveLaunch` and the flight's `drag`). The pitch and toss use `DRAG_TUMBLE` (unchanged), the snap and handoff are not flights, and the ball estimator's only literal (`0.003`, `ball-estimate.ts`) is its fallback for a ball that is not in the air. So the flag cannot reach a designed run directly. Paired check (`run_pairs.mts`, `run_pairs.py`, `start_state_tap.mts`; 4 games a side, seeds 101-104; the snap seed of play n is the same in both arms, one draw per play): the first divergence in every game is a pass (play 1, 2, 4) or a run that follows a pass (play 3); the plays before it are identical to the yard, and of 8 runs with an identical pre-snap setup (same index, clock, down, distance, yardline, call and defence) 7 give the same result. The one that differs (seed 102, play 3: 2.36 against 2.07 yd) follows an incomplete pass whose duration is identical (3.1 s) but whose ball flew differently; a tap of the bodies at the start of each snap shows no difference at the first run (play 0) and **energy differing at the 1e-5 level on 8 bodies at the next run** (e.g. 0.92159 against 0.92160), left by the pass. That is state carried between plays, not the ball reaching the run. I cannot say from this that the six-seed yards-per-carry drop is that: a lasting -0.2 yd/carry would need a systematic composition shift (different down-and-distance and field position after different pass results), which the card can test by stratifying designed runs by distance to go (3rd and short against the rest) and by yardline; I have not run a sample large enough to do it myself.

## What the NFL ball does that this does not

- **The decay doubles 0.3-0.6 s before arrival**, for completions and incompletions alike (14.0 | 13.7 x1000 per yd against 6-7 in the 0.6-1.8 s before it); in the last 0.3 s completions double again (26.1 against 11.0, the catch). Not drag. It could be tracker smoothing near the catch (a smoothed end of a track lowers the speed there), or the ball's nose-over at the end of the arc (its attitude leaves the path and it presents more area); the data cannot tell, and **it is not modelled**: it is the reason the last 0.6 s are left out of k.
- **The catch roll's 24 yd/s step is not what the NFL ball avoids on short throws.** The NFL ball is over 24 yd/s 0.6-0.8 s before arrival on 30-41% of throws under 20 yd (p50 22.8-23.4); drag at 0.005 takes our share from 29% to 24% and leaves most of the step in place. Whether the roll should price ball speed that way, and the speed the ball has at the hands, belong to the catch rebuild, not to drag.

## Not claimed

That 0.0050 is the final constant (the interval is 0.0045-0.0057 and shorter bands are noisier), that any outcome rate improves, or that the throw's release is closer to the tracked one with the flag (it is within about 0.5 yd/s both ways; see check 2). The NFL's estimate of speed near the catch is untouched.
