# Why a hang does not topple a runner: sources, our terms, and the capture-point bench (Tools, 2026-11-17, develop 2f5315d9, no change)

Verified this session (fetched or found in search results): Hof, Gazendam & Sinke 2005, J Biomech 38:1-8 (doi 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2004.03.025): XcoM = x + v / omega0, omega0 = sqrt(g / l) (l the pendulum length, about the leg length), margin of stability b = base-of-support edge minus XcoM, a temporal margin, and a negative margin needs a step or counter-rotation (abstract only; no figures retrieved). Young & Dingwell 2012, Gait Posture 36:219 (PMC3387299): the same XcoM with l the COM-to-heel distance. Houdijk et al. 2018, J Neuroeng Rehabil 15(Suppl 1):76: unperturbed walking at 1.2 m/s, backward margin at toe-off 0.271 +- 0.022 m, intact step length 0.66-0.68 m. Vlutters, Van Asseldonk & Van der Kooij 2018, Front Bioeng Biotechnol (doi 10.3389/fbioe.2018.00048): pelvis perturbations of 8% and 16% of body weight in treadmill walking; foot-placement modulation needs more than 0.15 s (they cite 0.28 s from Hof et al.) and the second recovery step is inconsistent across and within subjects, especially for perturbations near foot contact. A forward-lean-release abstract (ISB 2005, six young adults): maximum recoverable lean about 30-33 degrees whether one step, two or free, first-step length longer when one step was imposed. Maki, McIlroy and co-workers: older adults take multiple steps and step for smaller perturbations; multiple steps predict falls (summaries only). Load carriage: a trunk load raises the combined COM and shortens steps and single support (Mummolo et al. 2016, backpack studies; abstracts only; no XcoM margin values retrieved). Tackle: Fractional Tackles (arXiv 2403.14769) reports an average 1.28 s for tracking-based contact windows on NFL run plays; nothing found that times the ground after a wrap.

Not found: a margin of stability or recovery-step study for running; a chain length or step-count limit with numbers; the effect of a sustained off-centre trunk load on the number of recovery steps; a measured time to ground after a wrap. Our own tracking, first contact to the tackle or whistle event: NFL passes 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.9 / 1.6 / 2.4 s (p10 / 25 / 50 / 75 / 90, n 4,080, 57% within 1 s); NFL runs 0.6 / 0.9 / 1.4 / 2.1 / 2.9 (n 5,827, 34%); ours passes 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.1 / 1.5 / 2.2 (n 963, 44%), runs 0.7 / 0.9 / 1.2 / 1.8 / 2.4 (n 480, 36%).

Our terms (src/physics/support.ts, world.ts `updateSupport`): base radius 0.11 + 0.05 balance + 0.04 clamp((m - 90) / 60) times (0.75 + 0.5 posture), 0.118 yd (11 cm) for the 95 kg bench body, the same at 6 yd/s; omega0 = sqrt(g / COM height) = 3.17 /s; inside the base the offset decays as exp(-omega0 t) with no limit; outside it, recovery speed `stepSpeed` (3.4 yd/s) x fatigue x (1 - feetLock) x carry, ramped over STEP_LATENCY 0.12 s, a rate and not discrete steps; critical deviation = base + recovery / omega0 = 1.20 yd at rest. Nothing in it depends on running speed, stance or flight, or a step count.
