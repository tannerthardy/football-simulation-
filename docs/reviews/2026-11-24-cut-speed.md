# Speed through a cut and the way back: NFL vs ours (measurement, then the corner law; no build)

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-24, for Integration and Rules (route chart on engine-92: receivers lose too much speed through a break and after a bend). Positions only, BDB 2025 weeks 1-9 (9,657 dropbacks, tracks to the release) against the 24 census games (default; FLAT_SHAPE alongside), both cut to the release. `scripts/diag/census/cut_speed.py` (full tables `data/reviews/census/cut-speed.md`).

Operator. A cut = a frame k, at least 0.8 s after the snap, that is the largest heading change within 0.5 s either side, the heading taken over the 0.6 s before and the 0.6 s after. Cut angle = that change (30-60 speed cuts, 60-110 breaks, 110+ reversals). **Entry speed = the stem pace**, the fastest speed from 1.0 to 0.3 s before k (a cut needs 3 yd/s of it). Minimum speed in k-0.3 .. k+0.8 s; time back to 90% of the entry speed counted from the minimum; speed at +0.6 s and +1.0 s as a share of the entry speed; lateral acceleration = mean speed in the window x the heading change / 0.6 s; braking into the cut = (entry - minimum) over the time from the entry frame to the minimum. NFL 4,802 cuts, ours 2,274 (the 7+ yd/s entry cell has 6 of ours).

## In short

1. **Receivers do lose more speed than the NFL's through a 30-60 degree bend at stem pace (5-7 yd/s), and it is the braking into it, not the lateral authority and not the way back.** Speed cuts of 30-60 degrees at 5-7 yd/s entry (NFL | ours): entry 6.0 | 5.9; minimum **4.4 | 3.6** (min / entry **0.74 | 0.61**); braking into it **1.3 | 2.5 yd/s²**; lateral acceleration used 5.4 | 5.3; time back to 90% from the minimum 0.76 | 1.07 s; speed at +0.6 s / entry 0.85 | 0.71 and at +1.0 s 0.86 | 0.77. At 3-5 yd/s entry they match (min / entry .84 | .83, back .38 | .35 s). At 7+ (ours n 37) min / entry .71 | .69, braking 1.7 | 2.5.
2. **Plants (60-110 degrees) are at the NFL's pace in the dip and slightly deeper after it.** Entry 4.8 | 4.6, min speed 2.1 | 1.9 (min / entry .46 | .45), braking 2.8 | 3.1 yd/s², lateral acceleration 5.6 | 5.8, time back .95 | 1.03 s, +0.6 s .82 | .78, +1.0 s .98 | .84. At 5-7 yd/s entry: min 2.1 | 1.7 (.35 | .31), braking 3.7 | 3.8, back 1.30 | 1.42 s, +1.0 s .71 | .54. Hitches (the majority of 60-110 at that entry) are identical (min / entry .29 | .30, back 1.42 | 1.50 s). Reversals (110+, n 339 | 147): min .15 | .16, back 1.09 | 1.21 s, +1.0 s .91 | .67. TE breaks lag most at 5-7: min / entry .34 | .26, +1.0 s .73 | .52 (n 52 | 15).
3. **Re-acceleration after the minimum is not short.** At 5-7 yd/s, 30-60 degrees: the NFL regains 1.0 yd/s (4.4 to 5.4) in 0.76 s, 1.3 yd/s²; ours regains 1.7 yd/s (3.6 to 5.3) in 1.07 s, 1.6 yd/s², faster. 60-110 at 5-7: the NFL 3.2 yd/s in 1.30 s (2.5 yd/s²), ours 3.3 in 1.42 s (2.3). So the time back is longer because the dip is deeper, not because the speed returns more slowly.
4. **The lateral authority is not short.** The lateral acceleration both sides use through the cut is the same to within 0.2 yd/s² in every cell (4.9 | 4.9, 5.6 | 5.8, 5.4 | 5.3, 5.9 | 5.9). Ours carries less speed through the same lateral acceleration, so the NFL takes the bend on a larger radius (v²/a: 4.4²/5.4 = 3.6 yd against ours 3.6²/5.3 = 2.4 yd, in speed cuts at 5-7).
5. **FLAT_SHAPE changes none of this** (it only changes the flat's effort), so the cut law is the same arm.

## From the code: what sets the through speed and the way back

`steerRoute` (`src/sim/route-motion.ts`), per corner:

- **The speed asked through a corner:** `through = tgt.s x vtop x clamp(0.82 + 0.3 x routeRunning, 0, 1)`, `vtop = vmax x effort`; `tgt.s` is the route table's declared through speed at that point (0.4-0.6 a plant, 0.85-0.9 a rolled bend) and `effort` is the family throttle (quick .78, flat .8, back .8, cross .84, intermediate .86, deep .94 + 0.04 x (running - 0.5), since `ROUTE_EFFORT` is off). For a stem pace of 6 this is already below the NFL's through speed before any corner law acts: `0.86 x 0.85 x vmax` is about 6.4 at vmax 8.7, less with a declared `s` of 0.6-0.7.
- **A corner that is not a declared cut (rolled bends, the 30-60 degree class)** is led by `ROUTE_FILLET` (on): the turn is started `lead` before the vertex at `speed = min(carry, sqrt(aLat x lead / tan(theta/2)))`, `aLat = lateralEnvelope(body, carry, cutSkill) = lateral x cutSkill x (1 - 0.2 v/vmax) x fatigue`, `lead = min(carry²/aLat x tan(theta/2), 0.6 x the shorter adjacent leg, ...)`. With a mid receiver's `lateral` = (7-16) x GAME_CUT 0.55, about 4-9 (about 5 for a mid one), a 45 degree bend at 6 yd/s needs a lead of 2.9 yd and ordinarily gets it; the cap bites on short legs. The measured braking into these bends (2.5 yd/s² against the NFL's 1.3) is the **approach braking** `decel = min(brake, lateral x cutSkill) x 0.8` (LINE_START): about 4 yd/s² with the same lateral, braking from the stem pace to `through` or `speed`, whichever the corner asks. Where the requested speed is lower than the NFL carries, the receiver brakes to it; the measured dip says it is lower.
- **A declared cut (`cut: true`: outs, digs, comebacks, hitches)** keeps the vertex law, a plant with no lead: `through = min(through, sqrt(lateral x cutSkill x 1.3 / max(0.04, 1 - cos(theta))))`, i.e. a 1.3 yd corner: about 2.8 yd/s at 90 degrees, 3.9 at 60, 5.1 at 45, for lateral 6. The plants measure at the NFL's dip, so this law is about right at plants; the NFL's own radius at a 90 degree plant is about 3 yd per Rules' route-speed note, which at its lateral acceleration is the same speed order.
- **The way back:** the next leg's `vtop = vmax x effort`, and the body's own drive `(V - v)/tau` under the friction ellipse (`locomotionAccel`: the lateral demand of the next leg takes its share of the ellipse). The measured re-acceleration equals or beats the NFL's, so no term here is short; the effort throttle only caps where it ends (the 5.3 against the NFL's 5.4 the speed returns to at 5-7 is the stem pace).

**Which term is short:** the speed requested through the 30-60 degree corner (`tgt.s x vtop x ...` and the plan's `speed`), carried against a braking approach that is stronger than the NFL's; not the lateral acceleration, not the re-acceleration, and not the plant's vertex law. That agrees with Rules' chart (outs and digs come in at the NFL's stem pace and brake from -0.6 s) and corrects its attribution of "lateral authority": the NFL receiver and ours use the same lateral acceleration through the bend; the NFL simply holds more speed through it on a wider arc. I did not isolate which of `s`, the effort constant, `routeRunning` or `FILLET_LEAD` sets ours' requested speed: that needs the per-route declared `s` table against these cells (a reading for Rules, whose table it is), and it is not a fit.

## Caveats

- Speed cuts and plants are classified by the heading change inside a 0.6 s window, so a rounded 90 degree turn can fall in 60-110 or 30-60; the receiver groups are small (TE n 15 at 60-110 / 5-7).
- The 7+ yd/s entry cell has 6 of ours: deep crossers at full speed are not measured here (the CROSS_OVER catch lag Rules sees, 4.8 vs 7.2 yd/s, is a different quantity from this).
- Entry speed is the stem pace, so it includes the receiver's fastest second-before sample; the NFL's tracking is smoothed by the provider and ours by the same centred difference.
