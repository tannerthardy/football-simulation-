# Where our uncontested drops live: the catch roll's terms and a sample (reading and measurement, no build)

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-23, for Integration (Flex's table, `claude/quirky-franklin-9lbwbp` 6ace7dc9: ours 7.0% vs NFL 4.9% uncontested, 1.5x at every speed). Develop 0226f0d3 (engine-91), `src/sim/catching.ts` `receiverContest`. A read-only tap on every receiver catch roll (`__catchTap`, one line after the roll; no RNG draw, no behavior change: `scripts/diag/catch/catch-tap.patch`, `catch_tap.mts`), the 24 games of the census set (seeds 101 x 8, 202/303/404/505 x 4), against the NFL's arrival rows rebuilt from BDB 2025 weeks 1-9 (`nfl_arrival.py`, Flex's operator and its dropped_pass tag). Tables: `data/reviews/catch/attr.md`; report script `catch_attr.py`. Nothing was changed in the engine.

## In short

1. **The drop is one Bernoulli roll whose probability is a sum of terms, so a failure cannot be assigned to "the term that failed", only shared among them in proportion to their size.** Uncontested, on-target (ball within 1.0 yd, no defender within 1.5 yd, 1,320 rolls) the expected failure is 8.4 points and the realised not-caught 6.7% (82 drops, 7 misses): floor 3.2 points (38%), stretch 2.7 (32%), hard ball 1.2 (14%), running-track 1.0 (12%), high ball 0.4 (4%), low ball 0.1, tracking penalty 0.0. On the 82 dropped rolls the shares are floor 30%, stretch 37%, hard ball 16%, running-track 13%, high 4%.
2. **The floor is the hands rating alone and is about two thirds of the NFL's whole rate.** `0.015 + 0.06 x (1 - hands)` is taken off the catch probability before any physical quantity enters; with the receivers' mean hands rating 0.72 it is 3.2 points, against the NFL's 4.9% uncontested drop. Remove it and the expected drop rate falls from 7.8% to 4.6%; remove the stretch term instead and it falls to 5.5%; the hard-ball step to 6.7%, running-track 7.0%.
3. **It is not a placement (throw) problem.** The ball is closer to the receiver at our roll than at the NFL's arrival, and its closest approach to him is small (dh p50 0.25 yd, p90 0.85; the NFL's ball is at a median 1.10 yd from the receiver tag at `pass_arrived`, p90 2.5, with the tag at his shoulder and the hands out; the two distances are not the same quantity, see below). The NFL's drop rate does not depend on that distance at all (4.3% within 0.5 yd, 5.1%, 5.6%, 4.1% at 1.5-2.5, 6.1% beyond 2.5). Ours does between 0.5 and 1.0 yd (10.4% against 5.1% inside 0.5), and that is the stretch term.
4. **What is a throw property is the ball's speed.** 25% of our uncontested rolls have the ball over 24 yd/s (p50 22.3, p75 24.0, p90 26.3) against 0.5% of the NFL's arrivals over 24 (the NFL's in-flight mode: p75 19.8, p90 21.7). The hard-ball term turns on at 24 yd/s: 1.2 points, 14% of the expected failure. The NFL's drop rate does rise with in-flight ball speed (16-20 yd/s 4.9%, 20-24 7.5%, n 831 and 1,098), and at matched in-flight speed ours is not above it (ours 16-20: 4.6%, 20-24: 5.5%, over 24: 9.3%).
5. **How big the excess is.** Our 24 games: 6.0% (82 drops of 1,365, all uncontested) and 6.2% on balls within 1.0 yd, against 4.9% (236 of 4,815): 1.2-1.3x with a 95% band of about 4.9-7.5% on ours, so it includes the NFL's. Flex's 7.0% is n 441 (band about 4.7-9.3%). Where it is largest: caught slow (0-2 yd/s) 6.3% against 3.8% (1.7x), 2-4 yd/s 5.8% against 4.7%, 4-6 5.6 against 4.7, 6-8 8.3 against 5.7. The slow receivers have no running-track term (0.0-0.1 points): their excess is floor plus stretch plus hard ball.

## The terms of the roll (uncontested, on target), classified

`pCatch = clamp(0.985 - diff x (1.3 - hands) - 0.06 x (1 - hands), 0.2, 0.99)`, `diff = 0.5 stretch + 0.4 high + 0.35 low + 0.08 [ball over 24 yd/s] + trackPen + shoulder` (`catching.ts:234-236`).

| term | what it is | kind | mean (points of failure, 1,320 on-target rolls) |
|---|---|---|---|
| floor `0.015 + 0.06 (1 - hands)` | the hands rating, nothing else | **a rating rolled directly against the outcome** | 3.19 |
| multiplier `(1.3 - hands)` on every difficulty term | the hands rating scales how much of a physical difficulty becomes a failure | rating scales physical quantities (a rating deciding how much a placement counts) | applies to all below |
| stretch `0.5 (dh / (reach + 0.3))^2` | where the ball passes against the envelope of his hands (reach 1.0 + 0.3 yd): dh is the horizontal closest approach of the ball to his centre | **physical** (placement against his envelope); the 0.5 and the square are estimated | 2.65 |
| high `0.4 (z - chest)/(maxCatchHeight - chest)`, low `0.35 (0.5 - z)/0.4` | the ball's height against his chest and maxCatchHeight | **physical**, estimated slopes | 0.37, 0.06 |
| hard ball `0.08 [ballSpeed > 24 yd/s]` | a step in the ball's flight speed at the roll | **physical quantity, a cliff**: the threshold and the 0.08 are estimated | 1.16 |
| trackPen `0.25 (1 - ballTracking)` for a flight over 1.4 s | rating times flight time | rating-driven; **zero by default** (BALL_EST bit 2: his tracking acts through his own estimate, no second charge) | 0.00 |
| shoulder, `runTrackTerm` (CATCH_SPEED) | 0.37 x speed above 3 yd/s (over 4.5) x how far the ball comes from behind his line of run (shoulders on the ball count, TRUNK_TWIST) x flight time (over 1.3 s) x `(0.7 + 0.6 (1 - ballTracking))` | physical inputs (his speed, the angle, the flight time), **magnitude fitted to the NFL's fall in completion with speed** ("calibrated to ... catch_speed.py", the term's own words) and scaled by a rating | 0.98 |
| securing window `secureT = clamp((0.22 + 0.3 diff)(1.25 - 0.5 hands), 0.12, 0.45)` | how long the ball sits in his hands before it is a catch | **no failure on an uncontested ball** (only a defender's hand or hit can dislodge it, `secureStep`); a delay, and a rating-scaled one | 0 |

What is not in the roll: his eyes and head turn (the ball-estimate gate decides who may catch at all; `ballTracking` enters only through the two terms above, one of which is off), relative velocity (the speed enters through the hard-ball step and the running-track term, not as a closing speed), and body turn beyond TRUNK_TWIST lowering `fromBehind`.

## The sample

Ours (first roll of each target's pass, no defender within 1.5 yd, no contest, not deflected, 1,399 rolls; 1,320 with the ball within 1.0 yd): caught 1,283, dropped (the roll lost with diff < 0.35) 82, missed (diff at or over 0.35) 34. Dropped rolls differ from caught ones as the model says: the ball is farther from his centre (median 0.37 yd against 0.24), stretch expected 4.0 points against 2.65, hard ball 1.8 against 1.2, running-track 1.4 against 1.0, floor 3.2 against 3.2.

By hands rating (ours, on target): expected failure 10.6, 9.6, 8.1, 6.6 points for hands 0-0.6, 0.6-0.7, 0.7-0.8, 0.8-1.0 (the floor 4.3, 3.6, 3.0, 2.4 of it); the realised drop rates 4.6%, 8.7%, 5.7%, 6.1% (n 198, 265, 544, 313) are noisy, no slope visible at this n. The mean rating of our receivers is 0.72 (p25-p75 0.65-0.80).

By speed and ball direction (ours drop / NFL tag): speed 0-2 6.3 / 3.8; 2-4 5.8 / 4.7; 4-6 5.6 / 4.7; 6-8 8.3 / 5.7; 8+ 9.1 (n 12) / 5.5; ball from behind 6.4 / 4.4; across 7.3 / 5.7; runs at it 4.3 / 7.1; standing 5.9 / 6.0. The floor is 3.1-3.3 points in every group; the running-track term is 0.0 at 0-2 and 3.0 at 6-8; the stretch is 1.3-1.5 for balls he runs at or stands for, 3.0-3.2 from behind or across.

## Placement

- **Not the same quantity.** The NFL's `d` is the ball's distance from the receiver's tracked point at the `pass_arrived` frame (a shoulder tag; the hands reach out 0.6-1.0 yd): median 1.10 yd for catches and 1.07 yd for dropped. Ours is the ball's distance from his centre at the roll, inside his reach and, with CATCH_POINT, at the closest approach: median 0.25 yd. Ours cannot be read against the NFL's as "closer"; what can be read is the drop rate against each side's own distance.
- **The NFL drops are flat in the distance** (0-0.5 yd 4.3%, 0.5-1.0 5.1%, 1.0-1.5 5.6%, 1.5-2.5 4.1%, over 2.5 6.1%); "incomplete and not tagged" (off-target and breakups with no defender within 1.5 yd) is 373 of 5,188 (7.2%) and those balls are farther (median 1.37 yd, p90 3.4). Ours is 5.1% inside 0.5 yd (n 1,036) and 10.4% from 0.5 to 1.0 yd (n 284), then misses; ours has no balls beyond 1.5 yd at a roll.
- **Throws that never reach a roll.** 518 of our 2,086 thrown passes (25%) have no first roll for the target (the defender plays it first, a batted ball, a throwaway, a ball out of reach): the tap sees none of them, so the throw's accuracy before the roll is not measured here and the NFL's off-target 7.2% has no counterpart. What the roll sees is that the balls that do arrive are placed tightly.

## Where the excess lives, from the code

Not in the hands' reach to a well-placed, slower ball: those drop 4.3-5.1% in the NFL and 5.1% in ours within 0.5 yd. It lives in (a) the rating floor, which is already most of the NFL's rate before any physics applies, (b) a stretch charge on balls that arrive 0.5-1.0 yd from his centre (about 20% of rolls) that the NFL's drops do not show, and (c) a flight that is 2-4 yd/s hotter than the NFL's in-flight arrival, which the 24 yd/s step turns into 1.2 points. The rating that decides the floor and the multiplier is the only term in the roll that is not a physical or perceptual quantity; the running-track term is a fitted stand-in the file itself says should be replaced by a model of hands and eyes at speed. The model's rules say a rating must not award the result: the floor is that case, and it is the same size as the NFL's whole drop rate, so removing it without a replacement would put ours below the NFL (4.6% expected on these rolls against the NFL's 4.9%, with the other terms as they are), and what replaces it should be a physical process (contact of the hands on a ball arriving at a given speed and height, the eyes' lead on the ball), not a number. This is a reading of where the 1.5x is, not a proposal.

## Caveats

- Our sample is 1,365 rolls: a one-point difference is about 1.5 standard errors. Flex's 7.0% (n 441) and mine (6.0-6.2%, n 1,365) are both consistent with 1.2-1.5x.
- The NFL ball speed at arrival is bimodal (p25 5 yd/s, p75 19.8): the slow mode is frames where the ball is already in the hands (the arrival flag falls after the catch on many plays), so only the fast mode is an in-flight speed, and the NFL's ball speeds are tracker-derived at 10 Hz. Ours is the 3D flight speed at the roll.
- The tap sees rolls, not throws; the NFL rows are targeted passes with an arrival event. "Uncontested" is the nearest defender over 1.5 yd on both sides; ours counts his centre, the NFL's the tracking point.
- Nothing here is a fit and no constant was changed.
