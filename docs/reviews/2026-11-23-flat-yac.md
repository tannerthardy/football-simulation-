# Flat-catch YAC, NFL vs ours (develop 0226f0d3, engine-91)

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-23, for Integration (the user's film note: "a ton of our YAC seems to just come from throws being made to flat players"). Completions only. NFL: BDB 2025 weeks 1-9, 5,189 completions through the end of the play (`nfl_catches.py`). Ours: the 24 default games of the census, 1,403 completions recorded through the end of the play (`sim_plays.mts --full`, read-only tap). One operator on both sides (`flat_yac_report.py`), with Defense's pursuit operator (`pursuit/pursuit-an.py` from defense/pursuit-operator) run on rows built from the positions on both sides (velocities by a centred 0.2 s difference, not the tracking's s / dir).

## Answer in short

- **By this definition ours has fewer flat catches than the NFL, not more.** Flat (within 4 yd of the line, 8+ yd laterally from the ball, screens apart): NFL 34.4% of completions and 44.9% of all YAC; ours 16.9% and 20.4%. By type, share of YAC: backs .216 NFL / .115 ours, TE .086 / .042, slot .106 / .037, wide .040 / .009.
- **Ours takes more of its YAC from deeper catches and screens.** Catches deeper than 4 yd: 56% of completions and 42% of YAC in the NFL, 66% and 57% in ours. Screens: ours 7.1% of completions and 11.1% of YAC (by the call's tag); the NFL proxy finds 2.6% and 4.2%, a lower bound since the proxy needs linemen downfield at the catch.
- **When ours does throw a flat, the tail is heavier and the nearest defender is behind and deeper.** Flat YAC p50 / p75 / p90: NFL 5.6 / 9.4 / 14.2, ours 4.3 / 11.2 / 18.9; share 10+ yd .221 / .283. At the throw the nearest non-lineman defender to the receiver is 5.7 yd deep in ours against 3.7 in the NFL (distance 6.6 vs 6.2, width from the sideline the same, 18.7 vs 18.1). At the catch the nearest defender is behind the receiver in .54 of ours against .35 of the NFL's, and inside in .84 vs .71. Defenders within 5 / 8 yd of the catcher: .67 / 1.32 ours, .73 / 1.55 NFL.
- **The rally is not the gap by these measures.** Nearest defender closing speed p50 2.9 (ours) vs 3.2 yd/s; the first defender within 1 yd comes in .63 of ours at 0.6 s median against .67 at 0.7 s. First-defender outcome (Defense's classes), ours vs NFL: stopped .60 / .49, trailing from the catch (never level) .21 / .12, beaten without contact .11 / .11, missed after reaching contact range .03 / .16, never reached .06 / .10. Ours misses far fewer in contact range; it is trailing more.
- **Who owned the flat in ours (by the call):** 120 of the 237 flat catches (51%) were owned by a man defender (NB man 28, SS man hips open 14, LB_W man 13, LB_M man 10); 58 (24%) by a zone seat, standing a median 7.8 yd deep with his landmark 8.5 yd deep and 12 yd from the sideline for a catch 1.3 yd deep; 25% had no seat within 9 yd.
- So for Integration's question: the flat defender's depth and position (deeper and behind at the catch) is the larger difference than the rally; but the flat itself is not where our YAC mostly comes from. Our YAC share from deeper catches and screens is higher than the NFL's, and the flat catches are few. Caveats: the NFL flat share includes screens the proxy misses (so the true NFL flat share is a little lower); our sample is 237 flat catches; the screen split depends on the proxy / tag asymmetry.

# Addendum: the outcome first, then the open flats and who owned them

Operator: `scripts/diag/census/flat_open_report.py`. Games: NFL 136 (the distinct games of weeks 1-9), ours 24. Open flat = a flat catch (screens apart) with the nearest defender over 8 yd at the catch.

## In short

- **Among big-YAC completions the flats are a smaller share in ours than in the NFL, but they are far more open.** Completions with 10+ YAC: flat share .489 NFL, .282 ours; the nearest defender at the catch on those flats: median 6.2 vs 6.9 yd, over 8 yd .26 vs .39, over 10 yd .10 vs .28; defenders within 8 yd 1.34 vs 1.01. With 15+ YAC: flat share .439 vs .254, over 8 yd .29 vs .37, over 10 yd .10 vs .31.
- **Open flats per game and what they yield:** NFL 1.50 per game (11.4% of its flat catches), mean YAC 11.6 yd, 17.4 yd per game, 9.0% of all YAC; ours 1.83 per game (18.6% of flat catches), mean YAC 13.8 yd, 25.2 yd per game, 6.4% of all YAC. So ours has 22% more open flats per game, each 19% more productive, and 45% more open-flat yards per game. The flat is not the main source of our YAC, but an open flat is more common and costs more in ours.
- **Who owned them (ours, 44 open flats, 605 yd of YAC):** (a) no seat owns that flat by the call 25 (57%, 352 yd, 72% of them 10+); (b) the owner left for something else 6 (41 yd); (c) the owner is on it but too deep to arrive 10 (153 yd); (d) the owner was blocked or picked 3 (59 yd, all 10+). So the largest class is a hole in the call: the nearest zone landmark of the call is a median 10.7 yd (p25-p75 10.0-12.9) from the catch point, and no man is assigned to him. Class (b) is zone seats now matching or covering another receiver (zone seat matching H x2, matching X x1; now man on RB, X, Z). Class (c) is zone or man owners standing 3+ yd deeper than the catch. Class (a) by call: cover4_stubbie x4 (65 yd), cover3 x4 (52), cover4 x3 (48), cover3_match x2, cover4_box x2, cover3_trips x2, split_quarters_2man x2, and one each of cover6, deuce, cover9_strap.
- **Top ten calls by yards (open-flat 10+ YAC, ours):** 26 plays, 474 yd of the 9,411 yd of all our YAC; the biggest single one is cover3_buzz / stick / gun_bunch_11 (56 yd, class (c): LB_W on it but too deep), then cover1_off / levels+pa / ace_trips_11 with motion (35 yd, class (d): LB_S blocked or picked).
- Caveats: 44 open flats in 24 games; the owner by the call uses each defender's behavior on the first tick and a 9 yd reach to the nearest zone landmark; class (b) names what the engine holds for the defender at the throw, not the rule that moved him (Coverage reads the code for that).
