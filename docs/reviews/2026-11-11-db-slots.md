# Linebackers at safety: `pickDefense` and `DB_SLOTS` (2026-11-11)

Rules + Coaching, Integration item (b).

**The question.** On called two-high coverages, is the second safety 2.4 yd
shallow (9.3 vs NFL 11.6), and why?

**The answer has three parts.**
- **Measured by slot, he isn't shallow.** The number came from an operator
  that picked deep defenders by roster position.
- **The operator was misled by a personnel bug.** A linebacker held the SS
  slot on many sub-package snaps.
- **`DB_SLOTS` (off) fixes the bug.** The operator repair landed separately in #540.

## 1. The safeties by slot

The probe is `scripts/diag/call-mix/safety-depth.ts`. It runs on the matched
panel: seeds 7 and 11 × 4 dice shifts, run-ref-17, 436 snaps whose call shows
two-high. It reads each SS/FS's installed spot, his depth when the play is
built, at the snap, and 0.5 s and 1.0 s after.

| calls | n | second-deepest DB by roster position | shallower of SS/FS by slot | deeper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| two-high shell | 372 | 9.42 | **11.40** | **13.60** |
| rotations shown two-high (one-high shell) | 64 | 8.35 | 9.65 | 13.10 |
| NFL, two-high calls | | | 11.6 | 14.6 |

- Depth when built equals depth at the snap on every call: there is no
  pre-snap movement.
- `SITU_DEPTH` takes about 0.4 yd off a half and 0.2 yd off a quarter on these snaps.
- Rotations (buzz, cloud, Cover 9) align the rotating safety at about 9.4–9.7 yd,
  which is their install.
- **No depth change is needed.**

## 2. The bug

`pickDefense` (`src/league/team.ts`) did this:
- filled NB and DB first, from CB or S on slot-corner and zone suitability, so
  a safety usually won the nickel;
- then filled FS from S/CB and SS from S/LB/CB, on free/box/robber suitability.

| package | NB a safety | SS a linebacker | FS a corner | source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nickel | 84% | 20% | 41% | 200 generated rosters, fresh legs |
| dime | — | 64% | 37% | 200 generated rosters, fresh legs |
| base 4-3 | — | 4% | 16% | 200 generated rosters, fresh legs |
| nickel, games | — | **43%** | — | 32 league-sampled games |
| dime, games | — | **93%** | — | 32 league-sampled games |
| base 3-4, games | — | **44%** | — | 32 league-sampled games |

On the pass panel an LB held SS on 78 of 197 snaps, playing a deep half or quarter.

**Operators affected.** Anything that chooses deep defenders by roster
position skipped that backer, including:
- `yards.py`'s shell and depth rows;
- the sim-shell tables in #527 and #530;
- the "second safety" row in the call-mix review.

#540 records each player's slot in `sim-export` and counts DBs by slot.

## 3. `DB_SLOTS=1`

- FS, then SS, take safeties, on the same suitability as before.
- A corner (then, for SS, a backer) is used only when no safety is available.
- Then NB and DB choose from the corners and safeties left, on the existing
  suitability, so a big nickel stays possible once the two best deep players
  are placed.
- Rosters carry 6 CB and 4 S.
- Every snap's lineup comes from `pickDefense(team, personnel, availability)`,
  in the scrimmage snap and the try, so substitutions follow the same order.

On the same 200 rosters, FS and SS are safeties 100% and the nickel is a corner
87%. In games, every safety slot is held by a DB in every package.

**What it says.**
- Every row moves within its noise. Nothing broke: shells, depths, completion
  by class and runs against nickel are all unchanged within error.
- The cushion row: deep completion 52.8% → 58.3% (n about 210 each) and the
  YAC mean +0.3 are inside about 1.5 SE.
- The safety now in the SS slot is lighter and faster than the backer he
  replaces. That plausibly helps tackling after the catch and may cost run fit
  in nickel. Neither shows here.
- **Measured by slot, the sim's shell mix and safety depths now match the NFL:**
  11/42/47 pooled vs 9/45/47, and two-high second safety 11.7–12.4 vs 12.2. That
  holds with the flag off as well: the old gaps were the position-based operator.
- **Decision rule (Integration, 2026-11-11):** a linebacker at deep safety on
  40–90% of sub-package snaps is a plain bug, so with nothing broken it goes
  on whichever way yards move.
