# The route's end: the settle and the flat's gear-down (design note, 2026-11-14)

Design only; nothing built. From the route-effort review (#751): the hitch and curl stop dead, and the flat keeps
accelerating after its landmark.

## What the NFL receiver does, measured

**Population.** BDB 2025 weeks 1-6, WRs, non-designed non-PA drop-backs, every play (no ball-held filter); "not the
target" is on all frames through the throw. Selection is ruled out: non-targets run within 0.2 yd/s of all receivers
at every time.

**Speed p50 (yd/s)** at 1.5 / 2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0 s:

| route | NFL | ours (Cover 3 bench, ball held) |
|---|---|---|
| hitch, under 8 yd | 4.5 / 3.4 / 2.0 / 1.6 | 5.4 / 2.9 / 0.9 / 0.8 |
| curl, 8+ | 6.3 / 6.5 / 5.3 / 3.0 | 6.3 / 7.3 / 5.0 / 2.3 |
| flat (WR) | 5.3 / 5.5 / 5.0 / 4.1 | 5.1 / 6.1 / 6.8 / 6.8 |

**Where an unthrown hitch and curl go.** Non-targets, from their deepest point to 3.0 s:

- They come back toward the line 0.0 yd (p50; p75 0.0-0.1).
- They are no closer to the QB.
- Hitch peak depth is 5.3 yd at 2.9 s; the curl is still at its deepest at 3.0 s.

So unthrown, the NFL settle is a gradual deceleration over about a second to a slow, live drift (1.6-2.0 yd/s at
2.5-3.0 s). It is not a stop and not a return. "Come back to the ball" is the move once the ball is in the air, which
the catch behavior owns.

**The flat** is still 9.3 yd from the sideline at 3.0 s (p50; 7.1-11.7) when it slows, so the gear-down comes near
his landmark, not at the sideline.

**As taught.**
- **Hitch and curl:** sink the hips at the break and turn back to the QB, presenting the numbers ("he does not want to
  come out of this break running back to the quarterback"); against zone, settle in the window; "come back to the
  ball" when it is thrown. Sources: USA Football, the drop-pop breakpoint; Youth Football Online's curl ("Gather cut
  on Outside Foot, Come back to the ball!"); viqtorysports.
- **Flat:** run the flat looking over the shoulder for the ball; "If a receiver is 2 yards from the sideline and the
  quarterback still hasn't thrown the ball, the receiver should stop running and get into an athletic position"
  (fourvertsfootball). Turned to look back, he cannot hold top speed (USA Football, the speed drop).

## Ours

`RouteRunner.settle` sends a `settle` or `sitVsZone` route to its last point, or to a window spot away from the
nearest underneath defender, at speed 0.6, facing the QB. The hitch's last point carries `s: 0`, so he arrives at zero
and stands. `continue` routes run off the end toward the sideline at half speed; the flat family's continuation climbs
5 yd at s 0.6.

## Proposal: `ROUTE_END` (off), two parts, attempt 1 each

1. **The settle stays live.**
   - At the break he decelerates on his own body law (no change), then holds a slow drift rather than standing. He
     keeps his last heading, eased toward the window he would sit in against zone (the existing `sitVsZone` spot),
     facing the QB, at about 1.5-2 yd/s decaying over the next second.
   - No return toward the line and no move toward the QB while the ball is not thrown (the NFL non-targets show none).
     On the throw, the existing catch behavior takes over ("come back to the ball").
   - No new speed constant is fitted: the drift is his deceleration not yet finished, his body law with a nonzero
     terminal speed, which is an execution change, not a scripted path.
2. **The flat gears down at his landmark.**
   - Past his route's last point, unthrown, he runs on turned to the QB (his own turned-running cost, Tools' facing
     kinematics), slowing.
   - He stops in an athletic position 2 yd from the sideline, as taught.
   - The continuation's speed comes from the turned body, not a new pace number.

**Witnesses** (two attempts each):
- the speed-by-time rows above, NFL against ours, on the same bench;
- hitch and curl depth from the peak to 3.0 s (NFL 0.0);
- the flat's distance to the sideline at 3.0 s (NFL 9.3);
- separation in zone, for a cost read: a live drift may lose a window.

Not in this note:
- the slant's early speed, which is the body's acceleration (Tools, with the go);
- the TE's chip-release flat, a protection call on the refit list.
