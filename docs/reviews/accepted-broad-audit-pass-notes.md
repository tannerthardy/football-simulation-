## Secondary uncertainty and measurement limits

In all eight called Quarters passes the QB posterior becomes Cover-2-dominant
before release, scramble or sack. Source `QBMind` distinguishes canonical
Cover-4 and Cover-2 families, so this is an observed family mismatch rather than
merely another name for two-high. Yet acquired pictures are limited and these
related structures can look alike. No harmful read reorder, wrong throw or
counterfactual opportunity is established. It is a consistency question with
unmeasured consequence, not an eight-play proven recognition-defect count.

A012 and A033 have short scramble-to-contact intervals, but both contain prior
climb requests and multiple free rushers. A012's request moves away from its
closest DT. These sequences can already be lost upstream; a failed escape does
not establish bad QB logic. Source/runtime compatibility is required before
borrowing a historical frozen-pocket explanation.

Native `PlayResult.frames` already round positions/facing to 0.1;
`ReplayTrace` rounds body velocities/intents to 0.01. The collector preserves
those native outputs. Initial and final bodies have greater precision. Use
stored body velocity, not finite differences of rounded positions, for speed.
Frame timestamps identify enclosing ticks after physics; event joins are tick
bins, not exact continuous collision times. Initial bodies are separate because
the first live tick replaces the original t=0 positional frame. Contacts are
sampled, and final intents cannot attribute earlier controller stages.

This review demonstrates no contact-law defect, impossible catch, grip-force
error or universal quarterback failure. Its useful current nomination is the
crossing support/coordination sequence, with the opposing successes retained.
