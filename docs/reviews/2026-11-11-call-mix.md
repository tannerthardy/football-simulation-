# Pass call mix and defensive adjustment (2026-11-11)

Report only; no engine change. Rules + Coaching for Integration (Task 1: the
passing offence throws the wrong mix at the wrong time).

**Tanner's question:** if our passing game leans on deeper breaking routes, do
the defensive callers adjust? How much of the passing excess would better
defensive adjustment fix?

**Answer: very little.**
- A realistic in-game adjustment closes **2–4%** of the gap in yards a pass play.
- About **85%** of the gap is per-throw yield, which is too high against every
  coverage call.
- The offence's mix of targets accounts for about **19%**.

## Population and method

**Sim**
- 16 games, seeds 101–116, develop 8519cc5a plus #527 (run-ref-17).
- Teams are `generateTeam(new Rng(101), …)` IRN/COM with `withIdentity(team,
  seed)` per game, i.e. league-sampled alignment habits. This is the same
  population as `sim-export.ts --seed 101 --games 16`; the game scores match
  the export exactly.
- 1,541 called passes; 1,416 attempts with a classified target.

**NFL**
- BDB 2025 weeks 1–9, `plays.csv` and `player_play.csv`.
- 9,713 dropbacks, excluding spikes and kneels.

**Commands**

```sh
node --import tsx scripts/diag/call-mix/collect.ts --seed 101 --games 16 > out/call-mix/plays.jsonl
python3 -I scripts/diag/call-mix/nfl_calls.py data/raw/bdb2025/big-data-bowl-data > out/call-mix/nfl.jsonl
python3 -I scripts/diag/call-mix/cm.py out/call-mix/plays.jsonl out/call-mix/nfl.jsonl
```

`collect.ts` calls `sim.run()` in the play hook exactly as the game does, and it
records each coordinator `observe()` call without changing it.

**Target class.** The targeted route's NFL label is combined with the intended
air yards, by the same rule on both sides. HITCH covers hitch, curl, comeback,
stick and hook; IN covers dig and short in; the back's check and checkdown
count as FLAT. The classes are:
- **quick** (under 10 yd): HITCH, OUT, IN, SLANT or CORNER;
- **flat**: FLAT, ANGLE or SCREEN;
- **cross**;
- **breaking 10–19**: HITCH, OUT, IN or CORNER;
- **deep**: everything else.

## 3. Counterfactual defensive adjustment

Calls are independent draws: the defence does not see the concept, and the
offence does not see the coverage. That makes a concept × family cell a
randomised comparison. Cells are shrunk toward the additive prediction, with
8 pseudo-plays.

| | yards a pass play (incl. sacks, scrambles) |
| --- | --- |
| today | **10.58** |
| realistic: after N = 2–3 breaking throws seen in the game, the defence moves 30–50% of its mix to the family that best holds breaking routes (Cover 6) | **10.41–10.51** |
| upper bound: the best family per concept, with an oracle of the call | 6.94 (almost all Cover 0, an all-out blitz on 73 plays); **8.29** without Cover 0 |
| NFL | 6.21 |

The upper bound needs the defence to know the play call, and the per-cell
minima are biased low.

## 4. What the coordinators learn (`src/game/playcaller.ts`)

**Offence: no ratchet.**
- `OffensiveCoordinator` learns per `pass:family:concept`.
- Concept shares barely move from the first half to the second (comebacks
  9.1% to 10.1%, levels 9.8% to 8.2%).
- Breaking targets by quarter are 23, 32, 29 and 27%.
- At game end the multipliers are ×1.01–1.25 for every concept, because nearly
  every call "succeeds". The breaking share comes from the menu, not from
  learning: `choosePass` weights the 17 pool concepts about evenly, with
  quick / dropback / play-action family multipliers only.

**Defence: the mix doesn't move, and the learner couldn't tell what beat it.**
- `DefensiveCoordinator` learns per coverage family, from offensive success.
- Its multipliers are ×0.83–0.99 on every family; each family is beaten about
  equally, so there is nothing to discriminate.
- Coverage and pressure shares are flat by quarter: Cover 3 at 46, 39, 45 and
  42%; pressure at 23, 23, 20 and 21%.
- It records nothing about the throws it has seen (depth, breaking or quick,
  side, receiver), so it cannot express "they keep hitting the dig".

**Base coverage mix, recorded unchanged.** Cover 3 is 39–46% by quarter
against the NFL's 29–35%; Cover 1 is 11–14% against 20–22%.

## What follows

**Defensive in-game adjustment from observed throws:** not worth building now,
since it is worth 2–4% of the gap. If it is built later, it needs a per-game
record of observed throws: target depth band, break direction, receiver
alignment and the coverage at the throw. All of it is observable after the
play.

**The offence's mix is the build: `CALL_MIX`, off by default, a promotion
candidate.**
- Concept weights by down and distance, toward the NFL's routes-run shares.
- Flat-first read orders where the textbook offers the choice.
- The back releasing on quick concepts instead of checking.

Expected effect is about −0.7 yd/att. It cannot reach the screen and angle
remainder.

**Per-throw yield (about 85%) stays with:**
- Coverage: catch-point timing and technique;
- YAC and pursuit;
- the pass rush and sack finish (Flex).
