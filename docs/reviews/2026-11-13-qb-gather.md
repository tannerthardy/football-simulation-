# QB_GATHER: the QB's eyes and feet before the throw (work in progress)

Flag `QB_GATHER` (off; experimental). Branch `rules/qb-gather`, from develop at engine-52. Lane: Rules + Coaching.

## Why

**Defenders don't read our QB.** Coverage (`break_release.py`) found that NFL defenders facing the QB close on the
eventual catch point before the release at +1.5 yd/s (zone) and +2.5 (man), against our +0.3 and +0.6. Our QB shows
nothing before his motion.

**Measured first, against tracking** (`scripts/diag/eyes/`). Targeted dropback passes; NFL BDB 2025 weeks 1–2, n 2017;
ours 8 games on develop, n 719.

| Measure | NFL | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| Chest facing to the target, median at −1.0 / −0.5 / −0.3 s / release | 61 / 58 / 45 / 19 deg | 48 / 35 / 19 / 8 deg |
| Speed toward the target, p75 at −0.6 / −0.5 / −0.4 s | +0.37 / +0.52 / +0.67 yd/s | 0.00 until the windup at −0.35 s |
| Eyes on the eventual target at −0.5 s | not in tracking (no head direction) | 21% |

- **The chest.** Tracking's `o` is the chest. The NFL thrower is side-on until the last half second. Ours is already
  closer at every moment, so squaring the shoulders earlier is not the cue.
- **The feet.** NFL QBs step and hitch toward the target from about 0.6 s out; ours stood still in the pocket until the
  windup.
- **The eyes.** Ours are on the eventual target at −0.5 s only 21% of the time, 85% at −0.3 s.

## Step 1 (this commit): the hitch

When his eyes reach a read in the pocket, he hitches toward it:
- the hitch is up and toward the read, never back;
- its length is 0.5–1.0 yd, longer with a slower release and less experience;
- it is capped 2.5 yd above his drop and 2.5 yd off the line;
- each later read takes a second hitch;
- no beat is added, because the hitch is taken while he reads.

There is no hitch with an edge rusher he feels; QB_FEEL and SET_DECIDE own movement under pressure.

**Result, 8 games, off → on.** Speed toward the target, yd/s, median (p75); NFL in brackets:

| Before the release | Off | On | NFL |
|---|---|---|---|
| −0.6 s | −0.36 (0.00) | −0.27 (+0.64) | −0.28 (+0.37) |
| −0.5 s | −0.13 (0.00) | −0.04 (+0.83) | −0.10 (+0.52) |
| −0.4 s | 0.00 (0.00) | +0.05 (+0.89) | +0.07 (+0.67) |

- **Time to throw:** median 2.20 → 2.11 s; it does not grow.
- **The upper quartile is a little strong.**

## Not yet: the eyes

**The eyes still reach the target only at the windup.** On, they are on the eventual target 16% of the time at −0.5 s and
87% at −0.3 s. He throws as soon as his eyes land on a read whose window already clears his bar: there is no processing
time between the look and the motion, so in effect his decision is at the windup.

**What it needs, as one piece with the deep-window belief re-read** (Integration's condition):
- **A processing gate:** his eyes on the read for his processing time before he can throw to it.
- **The decision made earlier,** at the top of the drop or the hitch, on the window as it will be at the release, so time
  to throw doesn't grow.
- **The window priced at that later release.** That is the belief tables' reliability on deep routes at 2.5–3 s.

Coverage's separation check bears on the same piece: our gos against Cover 3 and Cover 4 are 1.0–1.4 yd tighter than the
NFL's at 2.85 s, against Cover 2 looser, and man matches.

## ACC_RADIUS: the catchable radius grows with the flight (flag `ACC_RADIUS`, off)

### What the belief got wrong
**His deep window collapsed on the longer throws.** On the deep reads he moved past (13–14 yd deep at about 2.85 s,
planned flight about 2.0 s), his P was 0.09–0.18 at every separation. Our real deep throws complete about as the NFL's
or better at equal separation at the throw:

| | Under 1 yd | 1–2 yd | 2–3 yd | 3–5 yd | 5+ yd | INT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ours | 33% | 45% | 54% | 45% | 78% | 2.8% |
| NFL go+post (BDB 2025, n 345) | 29% | 32% | 39% | 56% | 52% | 5.8% |

**The cause.** pAcc used a fixed 1.35 yd catchable radius whatever the flight. Our receivers' catches by the aim miss,
relative to an on-target ball (`catch-radius.ts`, 16 games):

| Aim miss | Under 0.8 s | 0.8–1.2 s | 1.2 s and longer | Fixed radius |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| About 2 yd | 0.61 | 0.87 | 0.78 | 0.33 |
| About 3 yd | 0.21 | 0.26 | 0.55 | 0.08 |

The radius is now 1.6 + 0.9·T yd, plus 0.4·(ball skills − 0.5).

### What makes our deep ball too generous: the defenders (Coverage's half)
Our generous medium-flight completion is the defenders' play on the ball. At 1.2–1.8 s, ours against the NFL:

| | Ours | NFL |
|---|---|---|
| Completion | 68% | 46% |
| Nearest defender to the ball at arrival | 4.5 yd | 2.8 yd |
| A defender nearer the ball than the receiver | 8% | 27% |
| INT | 0.9% | 4% |
| Breakups | 9% | 20% |

Placement (ball-to-receiver 1.3 against 1.4 yd) and the receiver's adjustment range match the NFL. This flag is carded
beside Coverage's fix.

### The four-verts fixture (`test/acc-radius.test.ts`)

**The panel.** Gun doubles four verticals against two-high, 10 snaps: Cover 2 seeds 2, 2F, 6, 6F, 7F, 8F; Cover 6 seeds
2, 6, 7, 8. On every snap a seam or go is 5+ yd from his nearest defender at 2.2 s.

| | Off | On |
|---|---|---|
| Target | the back on 7 of 10 | a vertical on all 10 |
| Throw time | check-downs at 2.4–5.0 s, median about 3.7 | median 2.8 s, latest 3.5 s |

The fixture asserts at least 6 check-downs off. On, it asserts at least 8 verticals, with the median before 3.0 s and
none after 3.5 s.

**The second half fails today, so it is not asserted.** "All four capped, he checks down at about the NFL's time" does not
hold. Against Cover 4 (the survey, 16 snaps) he checks down to the back at a median 3.65 s, against the NFL's 2.20 s for
the back on four-verts shapes.
- **The cause:** the vertical peek holds him (the peek lasts until the deep defenders' squeeze shows, then stays on a
  vertical close to his bar for up to two and a half looks).
- **The fix:** the next step, the processing gate and the decision timing.

## QB_PEEK: the vertical peek taken earlier (flag `QB_PEEK`, off; partial)

**Where the time goes.** A capped Cover 4 four-verts snap, traced (ACC_RADIUS on):

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 2.05 s | He sets. |
| 2.75 s | He starts a throw to a vertical and pumps it. |
| 3.10 s | The peek declares: its rule waits until every vertical is 12 yd downfield, and the slowest (the inline seam, 10.3 yd at 2.85 s) holds it. |
| 3.30 s | He starts another throw and pumps it. |
| 3.55 s | He reaches the back. |
| 3.95 s | The ball comes out. |

**Pumps are not the main cost league-wide.** They happen on 5.8% of pass plays off and 8.6% with ACC_RADIUS on
(`pumps.ts`). Pumped plays throw at 3.0–3.25 s.

**The change.** The peek declares once he is set and the two inside verticals have shown their stems (10 yd).

**Result** (16-snap survey per coverage, ACC_RADIUS on):

| Coverage | Check-down to the back, off | On | NFL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cover 4 | 3.65 s | 3.3 s | about 2.44 s |
| Cover 6 | 3.55 s | 3.3 s | about 2.44 s |

- **Throws to verticals are unchanged,** so the seam-open fixture holds.
- **About 0.9 s remains.**

**Why routes' depth can't close the rest.** He sets at 2.05 s, and the verticals are only about 5 yd deep then. The NFL
QB's check-down by about 2.44 s means his peek reads the deep defenders, not the routes' depth:
- two-high and staying over the verticals → capped, check it down;
- a safety who widens or turns his hips leaves a seam uncapped.

**The next attempt** needs the safety's leverage as it develops in his picture. A simple "a defender deeper than the
vertical" is true of nearly every snap at the top of the drop: it would check down before the Cover 2 seam opens
(about 2.8 s) and break the fixture.

**Parked** (PARKED.md) until Coverage's match-corner and deep-third work lands. The flag keeps attempt 1. The capped

## QB_COMMIT: a started throw holds unless the picture clearly changes (flag `QB_COMMIT`, off)

**The rule.** At the delivery recheck (`pullDown`) he pulls the throw down only if his re-forecast falls well below what
he started on: the window at half the P he threw at, or its pick risk up by 0.12 or more. Off, any re-priced value below
zero pumped it.

**Pumps precede worse results** (`pumps.ts`, 8 games, seed 101):

| | Plays pumped | Pumped plays | Other plays |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 5.8% | comp 47%, sacks 6%, INT 4.3%, throwaways 4% | comp 67%, sacks 2%, INT 2.4% |
| ACC_RADIUS | 8.6% | INT 5.6% | INT 1.7% |
| ACC_RADIUS + QB_COMMIT | 7.8% | INT 2.9% | INT 2.0% (overall INT 2.1%, comp 66%, 10.0 yd/play) |

**But the margin rarely binds.** At the 33 pulls in 3 games:
- P falls from 0.69 to 0.34 within about 0.2 s (median ratio 0.51);
- pick risk rises from 0.03 to 0.09;
- the median depth is 5.1 yd: short throws, not contested verticals;
- the commit rule keeps 11 of them.

A window halving on a 5-yd throw in 0.2 s is the receiver forecast's swing at the break, not a real change. That is
FORECAST_HOLD's ground (#630, "half the pumps"). QB_COMMIT is the right rule, but the larger lever is the forecast, so
the two belong together.

## QB_EYES: the processing gate (flag `QB_EYES`, off)

**The rule.**
- The ball goes only to the man his eyes have been on for his processing time, 0.15 + 0.25·(1 − decisionMaking) s.
- A throw he wants to a man his eyes are not on moves them there first.
- The window is priced at the release after the rest of his processing on that man (added to the windup in `evaluate`'s
  lead).
- `sim.qbEyes`, and his pocket facing (which defenders read through SEEN_EYES), follow the man he is gating to.
- The skilled exception is the existing look-off (`lookOff`, a decoy).

**Read** (8 games, seed 101, ACC_RADIUS on in both arms):

| | Base | Gate from when he sets | Gate, first read counted from halfway through the drop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eyes on the eventual target at −0.5 s | 21% | 80% | 64% |
| Time to throw, median | 2.06 s | 2.26 s | 2.11 s |
| Read 0 | 2.25 s | 2.35 s | 2.25 s |
| Read 1 | 2.70 s | 2.90 s | 2.85 s |
| Read 2 | 3.05 s | 3.20 s | 3.30 s |
| Read 3+ | 3.10 s | 3.50 s | 3.60 s |
| Completion | 62.9% | 62.1% | 63.9% |
| INT | 2.1% | 1.9% | 2.1% |

**Why the first read counts from the drop.** Counted from when he sets, the gate added a full processing time to his
first throw. An NFL QB reads his first look during the drop and decides at or before the back foot.

**Open.** Later reads still take 0.15–0.5 s longer, because each needs its processing time once his eyes arrive. That is
the price of a read he reaches late. Condition 1 holds for the median and for the first read, but not for later reads;
tracking has no read index to say what the NFL's later reads cost.

**Pocket facing within 20° of the target at −0.5 s:** 34% → 48%. Our facing is also what defenders read as his eyes;
tracking's chest stays side-on.

**Next:** Coverage re-runs `break_release.py` on QB_EYES to see whether defenders facing him now close on the catch point
before the release.

## BLITZ_READ: the post-snap blitz read (flag `BLITZ_READ`, off; not working yet)

### The opportunity (`free-rusher.ts`, 16 games)
- **How often a rusher is free:** on 34% of our blitzes a rusher is free by the protection's accounting (no protector is
  assigned to him) at 0.4–1.0 s.
- **What happens then:** on those plays he threw quick on 5% and was sacked on 12.7% (4% when all are accounted for).
- **A built-in hot exists:** there is a quick route on the free rusher's side 65% of the time.
- **The NFL** throws quick on 18% of blitzes (9% otherwise), 69% to wideouts.

### The build
In the first second, a free rusher (unaccounted, unblocked, coming at him in his picture) held for his processing time
sends his eyes to the hot: the quickest-breaking route on that side, else the back. The ball goes at the hot bar,
through HOT_LOOK and QB_EYES when they are on.

### Two attempts, not working
- **Attempt 1:** it fired on 1 of 36 free-rusher blitzes.
- **Attempt 2** (blitzers read from 3 yd off the line and within 12 yd): it fired 5 times, none on a free-rusher play,
  at a median 1.55 s.

### Diagnosis
For truly free rushers:

| | 0.4 s | 0.6 s | 0.8 s | 1.0 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| True closing speed | 1.8 | 2.7 | 3.6 | 4.3 yd/s |
| Closing speed in his picture | 0.0 | 0.9 | 1.8 | 2.8 yd/s |
| His position error | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.2 | 0.2 yd |

- **His position is right, but his velocity estimate lags about 0.4 s.** A speed cue arrives at about 1 s, which is too
  late.
- **The cue a QB reads is the blitzer's first movement** toward the line (the trigger): the direction of his motion in
  the QB's picture, not his speed.
- **Our free blitzers are also slow out of the gate** (1.8 yd/s at 0.4 s). That is a question for Defense against NFL
  blitzer acceleration.

### Still to build
- **The receiver's half:** a sight adjust with its own read, delay and error, by his awareness.
- **The caveat:** no-blitz sacks at 1.6% against the NFL's 5.5% flatter every read here until the rush is fixed.

### BLITZ_READ, continued: the get-off, and the trigger cue

**The blitzer's get-off is NFL-like** (`blitzer-getoff.ts`, `nfl_blitzer_getoff.py`). Non-DL rushers on 5+ man pressures,
ours n 689 against NFL n 1510:

| | 0.2 s | 0.4 s | 0.6 s | 0.8 s | 1.0 s | 1.2 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speed, ours (yd/s) | 0.6 | 1.8 | 2.7 | 3.5 | 4.0 | 4.1 |
| Speed, NFL (yd/s) | 0.9 | 1.8 | 2.8 | 3.5 | 4.2 | 4.5 |
| Moved toward the line, ours (yd) | 0.0 | 0.2 | 0.6 | 1.2 | 1.9 | 2.6 |
| Moved toward the line, NFL (yd) | 0.1 | 0.4 | 0.8 | 1.4 | 2.1 | 2.9 |

So the NFL QB is not reading speed either: the cue is the first movement.

**The trigger cue.**
- **What he watches:** from the snap, a second-level defender (aligned 1 yd or more off the ball, not a lineman) whose
  position in his picture has moved toward the line from his snap spot, while the protection does not account for him
  and he is unblocked. His position is accurate to 0.2–0.3 yd.
- **What it does:** held for his processing time, it ends the drop and sends him to the hot.

| Arm (4 games) | Hots on free-rusher blitzes | Quick throws there | Sacks there | Hots on non-blitz plays |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger at 0.6 yd | 40 of 45 | 9% → 36% | 9% → 4% | 206 of 278 |
| Trigger at 1.0 yd | 29 of 41 | 9% → 24% | 9% → 0% | 174 of 282 |

**The false hots are coverage players coming up on short routes**, not rushers. On non-blitz plays the triggering
defender was a man corner 40 times, a zone linebacker 40, a man linebacker 22, a zone safety 20 and a zone corner 19.
"A step toward the line" does not tell a defender closing on a hitch from a blitzer.

**What the cue still needs** (the coached read): the blitzer comes at the quarterback through the box, or off the edge,
not toward a receiver. That is his direction in the QB's picture (bearing at the QB) and his alignment (in the box
between the tackles plus about 2 yd, or a wide edge blitzer). Not yet built; two attempts on this cue.

**Caveat:** no-blitz sacks of about 1.6% against the NFL's 5.5% flatter every read here until the rush is fixed.

### BLITZ_READ reshaped: a rush belief per defender (the user's direction, through Integration)

**Not a trigger.** A continuous rush belief on each defender off the line (`updateRush`).
- **Prior from his alignment at the snap:** on the line off the ball 0.45, in the box 0.25, on its edge 0.3, elsewhere
  0.03.
- **Updated every tick from the snap** from the QB's felt picture: each yard the defender moves toward him adds 2.5 in
  log-odds, each yard he drops or widens takes it away, so a man who shows and bails loses it.
- **The belief follows the evidence at a rate set by his awareness** (τ = 0.1 + 0.4·(1 − awareness) s), so a poor reader
  acts on a stale belief.
- **Believed excess** = linemen + Σ belief − his protection's blockers.

**Its consequences:**
- **Urgency:** max(urgency, 0.4·excess), capped at 0.7.
- **The coverage picture:** in his window valuation a believed rusher's margin to the catch point grows by
  3 s × his belief, so he is discounted as a coverage defender, and the throw to the vacated space comes from the
  ordinary valuation.
- **A look prior:** an excess over 0.5, recognized, ends his drop and puts his eyes first on the quickest route on the
  side of the man he most believes is coming.
- **No forced throw.**

**Read** (seeds 101/201 × 8 games, off → on):

|---|---|---|---|
| Quick throws, blitz | 5% | 13% | 18% |
| Quick throws, plain no-blitz | 4% | 10% | 9% |

- **The trap's direction is there.** Throws into a refilled seat complete 19 points less often for half the yards. The INT counts are too small to split (3 and 2).
- **No interception came from the refiller.** The 8 look-prior interceptions were:
  - 3 by a rushing lineman (ball at the line);
  - 4 by a man-coverage corner or safety on the hot receiver, 8-20 yd from the seat (three of them in Cover 0);
  - 1 by a zone corner.
- **The replacement-defender trap does not happen yet.** Re-deals refill the seat, but nobody undercuts the throw from it.
- **The league teams have no QB below 0.7** awareness or decision-making, so recognition is only a narrow range.

**Most beliefs are false.** Of 432 hot calls, the believed man did not rush on about 60%:
- box backers 141;
- corners 76;
- safeties 29.

The look prior fires on them, but the throw still comes from the window valuation, so it costs little (64% / 2% INT).

**Urgency on plain plays** (no called pressure, 1,061 throws). Urgency was raised (excess > 0.5 in the first 2.5 s) on 346 plays (33%), against 715 where it was not:
- time to throw p50 2.25 against 2.50 s (within concept −0.33 s);
- air yards p50 5.1 against 5.1;
- to the back 29% against 27%;
- completion 65% against 67%;
- yards 8.4 against 7.7.

It costs him time on the clock, not shorter looks or check-downs.

56% of the raised plays are play action, against 12% of the rest. On play action, box backers step up on the fake, and the belief rises on any displacement toward him. That is the missing half of the design: the belief should keep rising only while he comes past the depth where a run fitter or a coverage player stops (the line, the back's depth). False urgency (33% of plain plays) is the number to beat. The aim is low single digits.
