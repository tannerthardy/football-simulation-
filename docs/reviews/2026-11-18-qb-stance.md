**What the NFL shows.**
- **It is a throwing stance, not a lag.** A right-handed passer's chest is turned to his throwing-arm side of the
  target, front shoulder at it, on 91% of throws.
- **It is about 50° until the delivery rotates him.** Wider on throws to his left, narrower to his right.
- **Ours squared his chest to the read and the target**, symmetric about 0°. QB_ALIGN and QB_BODY_FOLLOW change when
  the trunk gets there, not where it points, so neither moves these rows: carding those two is not the answer.

**Taught.** As he reads he is in his throwing stance to the man he is on: weight back, front shoulder to the target,
chest turned away. The throw is the hips and shoulders rotating through, the shoulders closing on the target through
the delivery to the release. His feet reset only as he commits to a throw (the bit-2 reset steps are kept).

**Built (QB_STANCE=1).**
- His set facing is the stance to the man he is set to: chest 51.5° clockwise of the bearing, the NFL median.
- Through the load the chest holds the stance to the target.
- It rotates square over the delivery's last 51.5° / TURN_RATE, about 0.27 s, arriving square at the release.
- With QB_ALIGN the throwing turn's rate limit applies to that path, so a late reset is still charged.
- Everyone throws right-handed: the roster has no handedness. A left-handed passer would be the mirror.

**Result.**
- The angle comes from 21° to 41° and the throw-to-base-side share from 74% to 64%, against the NFL's 53° and 61%.
- The signed offset reaches −38° against −51.5°.
- Completion is unchanged (0.702 against 0.693).
- The rest of the gap is under-turning, not the stance's size: on throws to his left −56° against −66°. Most likely
  his set facing still reaching the stance from a reset at a free trunk rate. It is not tuned.
