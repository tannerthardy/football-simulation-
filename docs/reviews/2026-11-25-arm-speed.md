# ARM_SPEED: where 3 yd/s comes from, what reads it, and what the literature gives (note; no engine change)

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-25, for Integration and Defense (the batted-ball work on `defense/rush-release-bat`: hands travel from the shoulders to overhead at `ARM_SPEED`, about 0.5 s, 0.75 s for an engaged man). Develop `de9692248`. I read the code on develop; I did not read Defense's branch or the tackle package's `REACH_TIME`. Sources were read through search results and abstracts only; every number below says which.

## Where 3 yd/s comes from

`src/physics/actuation.ts` (commit `dfcd68606`, 2026-10-09): "Arm speed reuses the solver's former 3 yd/s correction ceiling as an explicit, unvalidated shortening-speed bound, not NFL data." It is a stated placeholder carried over from a solver clamp, 2.74 m/s, not a measured or sourced hand speed.

## What reads it on develop

One constant serves two different physical roles:

1. **A loaded arm's power and shortening ceiling (contact solver).** `world.ts:1057`: the arm work limit per tick is `armCap x ARM_SPEED x dt`, so ARM_SPEED fixes the arm's mechanical power at `armForce x 3 yd/s`; `world.ts:1767-1768` and `3002`: a grasp or hands joint pulls in at no more than `min(ARM_SPEED, pull)` (the original correction ceiling); `world.ts:2894-2896` (HAND_MOTOR, off): drive and shove clamped to +-ARM_SPEED.
2. **An unloaded hand's travel speed (kinematics of where the hands are).** `engagement.ts:646` (the hands' length follows the gap, `ARM_SPEED x dt` a tick: the fit and the press), `691` (HAND_SITE's working length), `840` (the hand offset), `876` (the re-fit time = reaction + gap / ARM_SPEED, 0.25 s + 0.6 / 3 in `test/rush-hands.test.ts:92`), `1038`, `1072`, `1120` (the club and counter durations: forearm thickness / (ARM_SPEED x 0.75-1.25), about 0.03 s; RUSH_HANDS, off).

Tests that compute from it: `rush-hands.test.ts:90-92`, `hand-site.test.ts:37-43`. Not read by catching, by the quarterback (`qb.ts`'s `armSpeed` is the throw speed, a different quantity in different units) or by the tackle reach on develop.

## What the literature gives

Unloaded and fast (a punch):
- Walilko, Viano and Bir 2005, Br J Sports Med 39:710-719 (Olympic boxers' straight punches to the jaw; abstract, via PMC1725037): hand velocity **9.14 (SD 2.06) m/s**, punch force 3,427 (811) N, effective mass 2.9 (2.0) kg. The abstract does not say where in the movement the velocity is taken (it reads as the impact velocity), the camera rate, or the duration; the full text was not retrievable.
- Stanley, Thomson, Smith and Lamb 2018, Int J Performance Analysis in Sport 18:835-854 (15 amateur male boxers, 3-D motion capture; abstract only): lead hook peak fist velocity **11.95 +- 1.84 m/s**; "the jab the shortest delivery time (405 +- 0.15 ms)" as printed (the spread is implausible next to the mean, and the start and end of "delivery" are not stated); lead uppercut elbow angular velocity 651 +- 358 deg/s; rear uppercut shoulder 1,070 +- 105 deg/s.
- A karate reverse-punch study in the ISBS proceedings (2008; three karateka; a search snippet, not the paper): total punch duration under 400 ms and peak fist speed about 8 m/s.
- 3 yd/s is 2.74 m/s: 30% of the boxers' mean hand velocity at impact, 23% of the lead hook's peak. Rules' 0.12 s from torso to extension over an arm's length (0.7-0.8 yd) is a mean of 6-7 yd/s (5.5-6 m/s); for a smooth reach the peak is about 1.9 times the mean (the minimum-jerk shape), so 10-12 m/s, which is at the top of the boxing range. 0.12 s is quick but not outside it; I could not find a source that gives the time to full extension from the chest directly.

Loaded (a hand carrying a force):
- González-Badillo and Sánchez-Medina 2010, Int J Sports Med 31:347-352 (bench press; abstract via search): the mean velocity at the one-repetition maximum is **0.16 +- 0.04 m/s**, and mean propulsive velocity tracks percent of the maximum with R-squared of about 0.98. A loaded arm's speed is set by the load (the force-velocity relation); 2.74 m/s is 17 times the speed at maximum load. As a speed cap on a loaded arm it does not bind except at light loads; as the factor in a power budget it means 3 yd/s at full arm force, which is not a physiological quantity.

Not found: a sourced time or hand speed for an unloaded overhead raise (shoulders to overhead) or for a fast reach from the chest with no hit. The searches returned the reaching-movement literature (Gordon, Ghilardi and Ghez 1994: peak velocity scales with target distance; values not retrieved), a volleyball block comparison (Ficklin et al. 2014, J Sports Sci Med 13:78-83: hand penetration and jump measures, no hand speeds in what I could read) and jump arm-swing studies (shoulder angular velocity changes of +67 deg/s, no absolute hand speeds). For a hand with nothing to hit and no accuracy demand the punch values are the nearest sourced bracket, not a measurement of the raise. Hands in NFL tracking are not available (the player's centre only), so film or a motion-capture study is the only direct source; none is in the repository.

## What is wrong, and what would move

- As a **hand travel speed** (role 2) 3 yd/s is slow against every sourced fast arm movement (punch 8-12 m/s peak, 9 m/s mean impact velocity); a stated value from the bracket would be a mean of about 4.5-6 yd/s (a peak of 8-10 m/s at the end of an extension, a mean over the movement of about half of that), which gives a raise from the shoulders to overhead (the hand now travels 1.5 yd in 0.5 s) in 0.25-0.35 s and a re-fit over a 0.6 yd gap in 0.1-0.13 s instead of 0.2 s. That is a stated choice bracketed by sources, not a sourced raise time.
- As the **arm power factor** (role 1) it is load-bearing for the contact solver: ARM_SPEED x armForce is the work an arm can do per second in every hand fight, grasp pull and push through the hands. Raising the constant to make hands travel faster would double the arms' power budget too. The two roles should not share a constant: give the hand's unloaded travel its own named constant (a stated choice, sourced to the punch bracket above), and leave ARM_SPEED as the solver's loaded ceiling until the arm power budget is itself sourced (the arm force-velocity relation, `press`, enters there).
- What a change to the hand-travel constant alone would move (role 2 readers, all hand-length and timing): the press fit rate (`engagement.ts:646`, `691`, `840`: how fast hands reach and give with a man; the PUNCH and HAND_SITE paths), the re-fit delay after a hand comes off (`876`), and the batted-ball raise time Defense is building. Nothing in role 1, the solver's work budget or the grasp pull, moves.
