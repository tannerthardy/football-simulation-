# C9, the break on the ball: NFL picture and diagnosis (Defense, 2026-11-11)

Report only, no engine change. This answers Integration's brief for C9 (`docs/GAPS.md`) and Tanner's questions:
why our zone defenders don't break in time, what NFL defenders do, and how to get there without cheating.

**Population.**
- **NFL:** BDB 2025 common-format plays (the catchwho file), PFF coverage labels.
- **Sim:** develop fc5a5e48 (engine-28), leagueSample, seeds 101–116, 16 games (`sim-export.ts`); the controller trace is 8 games (101–108).
- **Operator:** the same on both sides, in `scripts/diag/pass-matrix/flightwin.py` (it extends `flight.py` from one 0.4 s mean to a timeline).
- **Defenders measured:** other than linemen, 3–8 yd from the eventual catch point (the target at pass_arrived) at the release.

**What NFL defenders do:**
- Deep zone defenders are already slowing their retreat before the throw. At −0.4 s they move away from the eventual catch point at 0.1–0.8 yd/s, and they cross zero just before or at the release.
- Their sustained break (2+ yd/s toward the catch point) starts at a median of −0.1 to +0.2 s around the release.
- Underneath defenders are already moving toward it before the release.
- In the 0.3 s before the break they move at 1.4–2.1 yd/s with little downfield drift (vx +0.1 to +0.5): settled, not running.
- Long flights break later (median onset: under 0.6 s flight −0.2 s; 0.6–1.0 s flight 0.0; over 1.0 s flight +0.2).

The NFL break is gradual and anticipatory, not a snap reaction to the ball.

## 3. Diagnosis

1. **The first wrong stage is the state at the release, not the reaction.**
   - Our deep defender over the catch point is still retreating about 1.4 yd/s from it 0.4 s before the throw. He's carrying a receiver at speed (man-match jobs inside zone calls) or backpedalling.
   - The NFL defender is already slowing (0.1–0.6 yd/s away) and crossing zero at the release.
   - Turning a 3.6 yd/s retreat round costs about 0.4 s at the braking rates we see. That is the 0.24–0.37 vs 1.25–1.36 gap in C9's first 0.4 s.
2. **The turn is physical but not at full effort.** The request ramps from +4.3 to +8.1 over 0.4 s instead of asking for the full stop at once. A smaller second factor, worth one look at the World's deceleration under a request reversal.
3. **About a fifth are carrying a receiver turned away and can't see the throw.** That's the corner-over / QB-timing picture: the ball leaves early in the stem while the carry is still on (Coverage's QB_TIMING).
4. **Anticipation, as the NFL shows it, is a settle before the throw.** The defender stops gaining depth as the QB sets and turns his shoulders, so the break costs less. It isn't knowledge of the target.

## 4. Design candidates (to judge, not assumed)

All from observable evidence only:
- **Settle on the QB's set.** The QB's set and his shoulders, through the defender's own delayed picture (SEEN_EYES as the basis, Flex's SHOULDERS_SET for honest shoulders), take the deep defender from retreating to settled, holding his roof. My earlier SET_SQUEEZE spent cushion on a set cue and found none to spend on the corner over a still-vertical receiver. This targets the 1.4 yd/s retreat over a stopping route, and is judged by the −0.4 to 0 s rows against the NFL.
- **A full request reversal on seeing the throw** (if the World check shows the ramp costs time).
- **A pump or late shoulder turn moves him the wrong way sometimes**, by his recognition rating. That price has to be measured on the NFL side first (breaks toward a throw that goes elsewhere), and it isn't measured yet.

**Still to measure on the NFL side:**
- **facing** (needs raw BDB orientation);
- **false breaks** (pump fakes, look-offs);
- **interceptions by facing.**
