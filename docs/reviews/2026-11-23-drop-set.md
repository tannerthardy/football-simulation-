# The drop and the set, NFL against ours (2026-11-23)

Flex's underneath-depth work, through Integration: "our QB is still drifting back at 2.0–2.5 s (−8.0 yd at 2.0 s,
−8.4 at 2.5 s; a 3.9 yd drop from the gun)". The NFL's median dropback is 3.05 yd and its time to throw 2.47 s; ours
is about 2.75 s. Zone droppers key their settle on a set QB, and 60 of 63 had not settled by 2.0 s. Measurement only.

## Operators

- `scripts/diag/escape/drop_set.py`: one operator, same definitions as `drop-steps.py`, on NFL tracking (BDB 2025,
  `out/mechanics/nfl-plays.jsonl.gz` with `plays.csv`; 8,112 thrown non-designed dropbacks) and on a fresh engine-92
  export (`sim-export.ts`, EXPORT_FACING=1, seeds 11 and 23 × 4 games; 649).
  - Depth is yd behind his snap spot.
  - The first set is the first frame 1 yd or more from the snap spot moving slower than 1 yd/s.
  - The drift is depth at the release minus depth at the set.
  - A hitch is a forward movement of 0.3+ yd after the set.
  - NFL tracking has no drop call, so gun non-PA dropbacks are banded by depth at the set (under 2 yd, 2–3.5, 3.5+), the
    same on both sides.
- `scripts/diag/eyes/drop-drift.ts`: sim only, by called drop type. It records each tick's phase and, in the set phase,
  the pocket spring as `pocketMove` computes it from his felt pictures. On the reference engine QB_POCKET, QB_FEEL,
  POCKET_CLIMB and QB_GATHER are off, so the spring and the edge step-up are all that moves him after the set.

## What it says

1. **He does not drift after the set.** His depth after the set changes by −0.04 yd (p50), as the NFL's does (−0.09 to
   +0.11). The pocket spring is not it.
   - The "−8.4 at 2.5 s" is depth at the set, measured from the line: alignment of about 4.5 yd plus a 3–4 yd drop.
   - In each depth band he sets at the NFL's time (2.00 against 2.00 s; 2.40 against 2.30) and depth (3.03 against
     2.78; 4.15 against 4.01).
2. **The difference is the mix and the throw off the drop.**
   - He almost never throws before he sets: 26 of 431 gun dropbacks (6%), against 35% in the NFL. Rhythm throws off
     the drop's last step, the timing game, is the largest single gap.
   - He almost never sets short (3 sets under 2 yd, against 12% of NFL gun dropbacks).
   - His deep drops are over-represented: 3.5+ yd is 41% of ours against 18% (or 27% with the NFL's never-set plays,
     which go 3.05 yd at the median).
   - The called quick drop sets at 2.5 yd at 1.75 s and throws at 2.45 s. The NFL's short set is 1.55 yd and its
     throw 2.00 s.
3. **Under centre, non-PA, his drop is about 2.5 yd too deep** (6.3 against 3.7 yd at the set). It is a small share
   (34 dropbacks), but it is the one place the drop itself is wrong. `dropSpec` sets the under-centre depths from the
   line (gun3 6.2, gun5 8.0, five per FIVE_UNDER), but NFL under-centre dropbacks set 3.7 yd behind the snap spot.
4. **Hitches:** the NFL takes one on 24–42% of 2–3.5 and 3.5+ sets; ours on 7–9%. In the code the hitch is a read
   beat (`stride()`) on the clock from `setAt`, not a movement, unless QB_GATHER is on (off).
5. **His read clock waits on the set.** Reads 2+ open only in the set phase. `setAt` is stamped when the drop ends (within
   0.4 yd of home, or the drop's time + 0.4 s), and every beat counts from it: READ_WINDOW, QB_PEEK_CLOCK, the grass
   reads, the stride beats. On the drop only read 1 can be thrown, on rhythm, and only if `ready()` and its price clear
   the rhythm bar. So the clock does not start at the snap as an NFL drop's timing does; it starts when he arrives.
   With a later arrival on deeper calls, every read after the first moves later. This is the same root as the
   progression's reach (65% of open men he never reached) and Rules' release-at-the-plant.
6. **For Flex's droppers:** the NFL passer is also not set at 2.0 s on 3.5+ drops (1.7 yd/s at 2.0 s), and 35% of NFL
   gun dropbacks are never set before the throw. A settle keyed on "QB set" would not trigger on those in the NFL
   either. NFL droppers settle on their landmark and the routes, so the settle trigger is worth checking against NFL
   droppers before blaming the QB.
