quality.** Retain the moving-catch candidate for integration review and keep
its adverse witnesses as controls. The next useful coverage work is whether a

## Where the pass game stands

| Area | Useful existing foundation | Most valuable remaining question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage installation | 25 coverage calls, numbered receiver keys, match rules, trips checks, split-field roles, Palms/Poach/Stubbie and man brackets | Does the executed route distribution match the taught call when threats cross, stop or release late? Static assignment existence is not achieved coverage. |
| Coverage technique | Physical cushion/hip transitions, leverage/trail/over-top choices, delayed route anticipation and movement limits | Is leverage surrendered by the chosen request, by achievable turning/braking, or by a legitimate offensive advantage? |
| Coverage help | Observed teammate pose/motion, primary-vertical protection and projected short-term roof margin | Can a teammate actually sustain the relevant lane? There is no general communication/acknowledgment contract, and help still has a six-yard depth cap. |
| Defensive information | ReleaseReader uses delayed motion and persistent declarations; QB release visibility already matters | Several ZoneCoverage branches still use private `sim.qbEyes` identity and current target position. Body orientation alone is not a validated substitute for a read. |
| Routes/releases | Shared RouteRunner, real bounded press contacts, physical breaks, option routes, TE/RB check releases | Options classify the true installed man/zone flag; settling/scramble space uses live defender state. Do branches follow observable leverage and installed coaching rules? |
| QB decisions | Gaze-limited defensive memory, fallible coverage beliefs, finite progressions, pressure/error, arm-limited flight and route timing | The QB reads the live receiver's precise private RouteRunner path/index at evaluation and release. Does that hide option disagreement or misforecast a contacted release? |
| Pocket/protection | Launch-relative sets, finite movement/contact, actual protection ownership and check releases | Separate sound pocket decisions from physical execution and logging. QB pressure valuation still reads private engagement/down state; this is not a pressure-rate calibration. Shared contact/protection work stays with its owner. |
| Ball contests/catches | Three-dimensional ball flight, finite reach, actual recipient IDs, reachable contests and physically costly movement | Catch height/reach can change success without a fully achieved jump/landing model. Preserve ball contests before richer catch motion; the in-stride experiment remains off. |
| Pursuit/tackles | Several corrected approach/finish mechanisms, native loads and earned assists; moving-catch candidate now has fresh transfer | Remaining coverage cushion, legitimate weak holds and missing secondary containment should not be conflated or masked with grip strength. |
| Call pool | 17 installed pass concepts, asymmetric Flood/Levels/Dagger and other side combinations; diagnostic Mills/Yankee/Mesh-rail/Texas-seam | More independent side packages and true screens/motion/RPOs can eventually improve variety. Catalog breadth has lower value than trustworthy decisions in existing calls. |

These are different maturity levels. The basic football vocabulary is already
substantial, but its decisions and information contracts need refinement.
A new coverage coefficient, random bust roll or mandatory loss against a
named concept would bypass those questions.

Some former defects are already repaired: fixed catch standoff, press hips
opening at the stationary snap, unused deep landmark floors, early primary
abandonment, treating a visibly grounded teammate as a roof, and permanent
protector progression reads. Do not reimplement those historical repairs.
Projected cap logic already protects the next read against disappearing
cushion/lateral help; its remaining six-yard maximum is not justification to
remove that maximum alone. Read the retained counterexample in the
[roof audit](../archive/reviews/coverage-roof-priority-audit.md).

a coverage-distribution failure. Levels/Robber is a useful pre-throw opposing
control because actual catch depth and recipient selection change. Mesh
provides ordinary effective defense even against a purported man-beater.
Yankee reverses the presumed control advantage through **both** earlier catch
exposure/selection and later YAC. That earns film inspection, not a declaration
that Poach is inherently wrong or every post-over must be stopped. The selected
recorded Yankee pair both end incomplete, so they cannot represent that cell's
explosive catches.

The Mills offensive win has a legitimate-looking responsibility picture:
at its 2.30-s throw, actual **O8081-WR2 / Z, index 9** is owned by

Earlier NFL evidence supports looking at flight closure, late approach and
ordinary pocket response. It also found unusually tight early separation and
no globally excessive time to throw. Those are historical hypotheses, not
current league calibration and not a mandate to tighten every defender.

## Ranked next work

1. **Coverage threat selection and pre-throw leverage.** First separate
   removal of the private QB target cue from any new directional replacement.
   Use one two-threat zone encounter, a late-changing read, and an owned
   vertical/ordinary short-throw control. Hold rules, leverage policy, bodies,
   physics and randomness fixed. Record consumed receiver/QB pictures, chosen
   route/landmark, request, achieved turn/cushion, plausible ball window and
   complementary ownership. The question is whether observed routes and
   taught ownership can sustain a sensible choice without private target
   knowledge. Only then address one demonstrated exchange/help error. More
   believable offense can legitimately gain more yards against this change.
2. **One receiver/QB option agreement contract.** Stick or Hook should read
   its installed leverage key through that receiver's observations. The QB
   should anticipate his taught branch from his own picture and update from
   subsequent visible receiver movement. Compare sound agreement, a disguise
   with different observation times, a wrong interpretation, and agreement
   where press/contact prevents the cut. No random disagreement penalty or
   additional throw-error multiplier. Keep this separate from coverage changes.
3. **Pocket/pressure decision versus execution.** Preserve the protection
   owner's work and quantify whether a lost set, late QB adjustment, true
   pressure or a logging blind spot causes a poor pass. The historical native
   pressure logger omits part of windup; do not tune accuracy to a label whose
   meaning differs from geometric pressure. Shared hands, force and recovery
   laws stay with the physics owner.
4. **Offensive composition and additional route techniques.** Once current
   ownership/reads are credible, add a small asymmetric side package or one
   genuinely supported screen/motion interaction. Current static call labels
   and `beats` tags are no proof of installed superiority. Grade actual routes,
   timing, protection, receiver choices and feasible defensive responses.

For the first task, reuse the previously failed QB-cue work rather than
restarting it. The [withheld-policy review](../archive/reviews/position-technique-repair-verification.md#qb-cue-information-repair-demonstrated-both-policies-withheld)
shows why a generic body-direction replacement was unsound: it first moved
defenders toward empty grass before a route released; its narrowed version
still gave uncertain body orientation confidence one. Its adverse completed
and live-eight-second cases remain historical evidence. They cannot be pooled
with current outcomes or automatically counted as illegitimate regressions.
Do not restore either whole archived patch just because it removes private data.

## What pausing YAC means

The [moving-catch improvement](moving-catch-improvement.md) remains ready for
integration review, with the integration owner running the preserved full suite
on the exact combined candidate. Keep P58, P4's two retained execution draws,
P16 interception, ordinary successful rear holds, fresh same-execution C1,
fresh safety C3, sideline C4 and unchanged P173 as reusable controls.

Do not launch another broad arrival/grip/capability sweep. Reopen targeted YAC
work if a coverage change produces a new wrong approach, destroys earned
support, or exposes a still-implausible loaded response. A completion allowed
with good leverage, an ordinary broken tackle, or a touchdown under weaker
execution is not itself an error. Global catcher awareness, helper coordination,
roster corrections and prolonged/ground holds remain separate or parked.

There is no correctness blocker to this review or to a controller-only coverage
investigation. There is a modeling limitation: gaze/communication and catch
landing are coarse, and shared loaded recovery is not universally validated.
Keep those limitations explicit while testing decisions inside existing limits.
The next task can make substantial progress without new anatomy or strength.
