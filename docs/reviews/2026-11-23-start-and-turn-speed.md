# Speed from the snap and through a turn, by alignment and release direction: NFL vs ours (measurement and reading, no build)

Tools/Physics, 2026-11-23, for Integration (Rules' FLAT_SHAPE, PR #870). Positions only, BDB 2025 weeks 1-9 (9,657 dropbacks, tracks to the release) against the 24-game census set on develop engine-91 (0226f0d3), four arms of the same 24 games: **default**; **FLAT_SHAPE=1** (rules/flat-shape d91df4f1); **LINE_START=0** (a diagnostic arm, see the cause); and the NFL. One operator on both sides: `scripts/diag/census/start_speed.py`. Tables: `data/reviews/census/start-final.md` (turn window 0.4 s) and `start-final-win6.md` (0.6 s). Nothing was changed in the engine.

Operator. Receivers = offensive players with a route, not QB or OL, stationary at the snap and not in motion (NFL: PFF motion flag; ours: no motion on the play). Alignment is read from the snap positions by the same code: a TE within 2.5 yd of a tackle and on the line is in-line, any other TE is flexed, backs are RB/FB/HB, and of the other receivers the outermost on each side of the center is wide and those inside him are slot (a spot check on plays shows trips and bunch sets resolve to one wide and the others slot). Speed at t = the centred 0.2 s difference of position at 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 s after the snap. Release direction = the angle of the snap-to-1.0 s displacement against straight upfield (0-20 / 20-45 / 45-90 / over 90 degrees); it needs 0.7 yd of displacement. (A higher bar, 1.5 yd, made ours look much faster: 68% of our receivers were under it against 40% of the NFL's, so the cut kept only our fast tail. The alignment tables have no cut; only the direction tables lose the receivers who have not moved: NFL 2,921, ours 36.) Rows: NFL 31,879 receivers, default 5,330, FLAT_SHAPE 5,576, LINE_START=0 5,591.

3. **The cause of the first second is in the get-off, `LINE_START`** (on by default). ## What limits it, from the code

- **Get-off (LINE_START, `route-motion.ts` `getOffDrive`; `receiver.ts:476`).** Until he has "declared his stem" (0.5 yd out of the stance and his own observation delay since the start) the receiver's command carries `drive = clamp(0.4 + 0.03 x min(break depth, 10) + 0.05 x defender term + 0.15 x (route running - 0.5), 0.3, 1)`: a share of the body's start force. `locomotionAccel` multiplies the near-standstill (`speed < 0.35`) and sprint-curve accelerations by `drive` (`kinematics.ts:306, 326, 341`), so a hitch or slant (first break at 3-6 yd, drive about 0.5-0.6) and a flat (a short first break) leave the line more slowly than a go (break depth 10, about 0.7-0.8). The data say the same direction: the deficit at 1.0 s, wide, NFL | default, is hitch 4.2 | 3.4, slant 3.4 | 3.0, go 4.2 | 4.0. The NFL's slow routes are slow too (their flats 3.2 at 1.0 s) but not that slow. Its stated reason is the friction ellipse: a full forward push leaves no lateral authority, and nothing else in the receiver's controller values keeping it.
- **No angle dependence in the start.** `locomotionAccel`'s start force does not depend on facing (`kinematics.ts:296-301`: "The start force does not depend on his facing"). The drive share is a function of the route (break depth) and a defender, not of the release angle, and the first 0.35 yd/s is isotropic. That is why ours has the NFL's relative penalty for an angled release or less (item 2); it comes from the path and the geometry, not from the body.
- **Stance and first-step facing.** The body has no stance: a receiver is at rest and the first tick pushes toward the first waypoint at the drive share; hips are `commandFacing`'s, below `PLANT_SPEED` 2.5 yd/s he can square to any heading at once (`kinematics.ts:221-231`) and the turn-rate limit (`turnRateOf`, the profile's `turnRate` 4.5-12, 0.75 of it above 6 yd/s) only bounds the hips' yaw; it does not bound the velocity. So no turn-rate limit slows a start at low speed.
- **Turning speed.** Velocity turns through the friction ellipse: `lateralEnvelope = lateral x cutSkill x (1 - 0.2 v/vmax) x fatigue` (`kinematics.ts:117`, `lateral` 7-16 yd/s² x `GAME_CUT`), and `locomotionAccel` at speed splits the demand into along (`At`, the sprint curve) and across (`An`). The route follower slows into a corner on `decel = min(brake, lateral x cutSkill) x 0.8` (LINE_START), through-speed `tgt.s x vtop x clamp(0.82 + 0.3 rr, 0, 1)` and the fillet plan (`ROUTE_FILLET`, on): the corner is led and carried at `min(carry, sqrt(aLat x lead / tan(theta/2)))`. A 55-degree rounded turn at 4 yd/s costs ours about what it costs the NFL (item 6). What follows the corner is `vtop = vmax x effort` on the next leg, the family throttle of item 4: that is where ours is slow to recover, and why FLAT_SHAPE (effort 1 for the flat) closes the recovery gap.
- **Where the angle is missing.** The NFL's start has a direction dependence (DIR_START's measurement, below), and the offence's is weaker than the defence's. The body does not carry it, and at the receiver's first step the route shape sets the angle, not the legs.

## DIR_START (#850, parked) and these results: they do not conflict

#850 measured defenders' reaction starts from rest: the sim's lateral start was 0.95 of its forward start (NFL 0.66-0.70 at 0.2 s) and too fast by 0.6 yd/s at 0.2 s, because the body is isotropic. It also measured the offence's (WR/TE .83 at 0.2 s, .84 at 0.4 s; QB .83 / .90; RB .89 / .98; OL 1.06 / 1.05) and said a planned first step pays no penalty (the loaded-start rule). Here: ours' angled releases are not penalised more than the NFL's (item 2: slot 0.91 vs 0.86 at 1.0 s, 0.83 vs 0.91 at 0.5 s; ours slightly less penalised, consistent with an isotropic body), and ours are late off the line by the same amount at every angle (a magnitude, from the get-off drive share). The two are different quantities: direction dependence (missing in the body, measured on the defence, weaker for the offence) and the magnitude of the receiver's early push (a deliberate share in the controller, too low for wide and slot, too high without it for TE and back). Fixing the second cannot be done by raising the share alone: the NFL's slot is at 1.7 / 4.2 with the share off, but TE and back would then overshoot as the `LINE_START=0` arm shows; and adding DIR_START's angle penalty to a receiver would lower the angled starts further below the NFL's, since ours are already 0.5 yd/s under it at 0.5 s on 45-90 degree releases. They would conflict only if both changes landed in the same body without separating the offence's preload (which #850 already did with the loaded rule).

## Caveats

- Positions only at 10 Hz; the NFL's tracks end at the release (median 2.7 s) and ours are cut to it, so the 2.0 s speed uses plays with at least 2.0 s (NFL 1,545 of 9,657 plays are shorter; ours 136 of 2,242).
- NFL receivers anticipate the snap; their "speed at 0.5 s" includes some pre-snap movement not removed by the motion flag (the PFF flag covers motion, not a jump). Ours start on the snap. That would make the NFL's early speed higher, so 0.1-0.2 s of the onset gap may be anticipation and not the body; the 2.0 s deficits are not affected by it.
- Alignment is read from positions; PFF's `receiverAlignment` is play-level. Wide receivers in ours are rarely 45-90 degree releasers (0 of 2,235), so the wide-by-angle cell cannot be compared.
- Turn rows are small (ours 33-253 by window); the loss is window-dependent. No fit of any constant was done; the `LINE_START=0` arm is a diagnostic, not a proposal.
- Ours route flag is the controller's route; NFL's `wasRunningRoute` includes backs who check-release; the NFL back's early speed (1.1 at 0.5 s) is that.
