# What the QB chooses at the throw: read order, timing and checkdowns (2026-11-11)

Report only; no engine change. Rules + Coaching, for Integration. The read side
of "the offence throws the wrong mix"; Coverage holds the `window()`
calibration. With `CALL_MIX` on (run-ref-20), targets are still 24% breaking
10–19 yd routes against the NFL's 11.5%, and screens and angles do not exist.

**Answer.**
- **Read order and timing are not the lever.** The QB throws on NFL timing:
  release p50 2.65 s vs 2.60, and on 10–19 yd throws the ball leaves a median
  0.85 s before the receiver's deepest point (NFL 1.00 s).
- **The difference is the short game.**
  - The NFL's early throws (before 2.0 s) are half flats and screens; ours are
    almost all quick routes.
  - Flats are as open at the throw as the NFL's, but we throw them a third as
    often when open.
  - Backs are open at the throw a third as often.
  - Flats and backs come late, as a last resort, instead of on rhythm.

## Population and operator

**Sim.** `scripts/diag/call-mix/qb-reads.ts` is a passive probe, on 32 league-sampled games:
`leagueSample` (habits and coordinators, #546), develop facb5e0f (run-ref-23),
seeds 101–116 and 201–216, 2,875 dropbacks. It wraps `QBPass` and records:
- the read order;
- when each read became current;
- at the throw, the read he was on, the target's place and the path;
- at the throw or the sack, every skill player's route, depth, width and
  nearest defender.

**NFL.** `scripts/diag/call-mix/nfl_throw_picture.py`: BDB 2025 weeks 1–3, 3,372
dropbacks. Every route runner at `pass_forward` (or the sack), with the same
nearest-defender operator.

**Tables.** `scripts/diag/call-mix/qb_reads.py`.
- Time to throw is the release on both sides: `stats.timeToThrow` in the sim,
  `pass_forward` in tracking.
- Target class is the route label plus the throw's air yards on both sides.
- "Open" means the nearest defender is 5+ yd away. The NFL median on flat
  targets at the throw is 5.3.

**Two operator traps avoided.** Both would have produced a false "the sim
throws 0.7 s early".
- Classing NFL routes by their depth measured *up to the throw* puts every
  early-thrown 12-yd route in "quick".
- The QB's motion start is not the release; the release is one windup later.

Routes run on NFL time. A targeted receiver first reaches 5 / 10 / 15 yd at
1.70 / 2.40 / 3.10 s in the sim and 1.80 / 2.50 / 3.10 s in the NFL (32 games
of exports vs weeks 1–3).

## 2. Reads (sim)

- **Path of the throw:**
  - current read 61.3%;
  - rhythm throw on the last step of the drop 18.1%;
  - rhythm outlet as the eyes leave a read 12.4%;
  - hot 5.4%;
  - out of reads 2.8%.
- **Target's place in the read order:** #1 50.3%, #2 18.3%, #3 10.8%, #4 6.9%, #5+ 13.7%.

## 3. Checkdowns and the short game at the throw

| | sim | NFL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| back targeted, all throws | 19.0% | 18.7% |
| ... released under 2.0 s / 3.5 s+ | 3.0% / 30.3% | 18.9% / 15.6% |
| throws to a breaking route: a back released / open | 82.8% / **9.2%** | 74.5% / **33.0%** |
| throws to a breaking route: a flat or released back open | 20.8% | 38.9% |
| sacks: a flat or released back open | 26.1% | 34.7% |
| every flat runner (FLAT/ANGLE) at the throw: open | 35.8% | 40.9% |
| ... targeted when open | **9.5%** | **32.6%** |
| every released back at the throw: open | **11.9%** | **36.4%** |
| ... targeted when open | 29.4% | 28.5% |

**Reading.**
1. **The flat is open about as often as the NFL's, and thrown a third as often
   when open.** That is the QB's estimate of a short, wide throw (`window()`:
   P 0.36 and INT 0.13 at 3–4.5 yd vs NFL 87% complete and 0 INT, #538). It is
   Coverage's calibration. When it is fixed, flats should move toward the
   early window, where half the NFL's early throws go.
2. **Backs are released as often as the NFL's but open a third as often.** Once
   open, they are thrown at the NFL rate. The installed back route on most
   concepts is the check-release (`check`: block, release at 1.0–1.3 s, settle
   at 4 yd over the middle among the backers). NFL backs run flats, angles and
   swings from the snap. That is route construction, and Run Game owns the
   back's quick releases.
3. **The checkdown share already matches (19.0 vs 18.7%), but on the wrong
   clock.** The sim's back is a late last resort (30% of throws after 3.5 s).
   The NFL's back is thrown at an even rate from the first second.
4. **Screens and angles** remain the named remainder, also Run Game's.

## What follows

- **No read-order change is proposed.** The order and its timing are NFL-like.
  `FLAT_FIRST` (branch `rules/flat-first`) showed that reading the flat first
  cannot help while the flat's open throw is judged closed.
- **The fix the numbers point to is the short game's construction and
  judgement:**
  - Coverage: the `window()` estimate on short, wide throws (flat targeted when
    open 9.5% vs 32.6%);
  - Run Game: the back's release on quick and flat concepts (open at the throw
    11.9% vs 36.4%), and screens.
- **What the concept teaches, for when those land.** In curl-flat, flood, stick
  and slant-flat, the flat is a rhythm throw taken when the flat defender
  declares. It needs no new read order, only a flat that is seen open when it
  is open.
