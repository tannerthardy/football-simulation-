## What good football requires, and what is actually missing

These are installation choices, not universal rules for every NFL defense.
The existing [teaching synthesis](../football-handbook/defense-and-perception.md)
and its inspected coaching sources support the principles; supplied manuscripts
are teaching aids, not measurements or project instructions. Historical native
results retain their old pins and are not new baseline measurements.

## New useful witness: leverage is spent before the handoff

R2-C02 is Four Verticals/Cover3 Match. Actual eventual catcher H10 is
`PO2-WR3`; nickel19 is `PD2-S2`. Positive x is upfield for the offense.

| Decision time | Installed/current technique | Acquired H declaration | Nickel achieved vx before request | Requested vx |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1.00 s | Curl-flat, match not active | Vertical, depth 1.22 yd | -2.61 yd/s | -4.07 yd/s |
| 1.40 s | Man H | Vertical, depth 3.43 yd | -0.94 yd/s | +8.06 yd/s |
| 2.50 s | Man H | Vertical, depth 11.32 yd | +5.19 yd/s | +10.09 yd/s |

By 2.50 H is about 6.1 yards farther upfield than the nickel. Generated top
speeds are H 9.93 and nickel 10.09 yd/s. This does not establish identical
acceleration, turn ability, loads or execution. It does show why giving the
defender a larger speed rating would be an unsupported remedy: his earlier
requested direction and later momentum already explain a substantial problem.

The same early policy is visible in another Cover3 roster: R1-C02's nickel
has an acquired vertical at depth 1.61 at 1.00, yet requests vx -3.22 while
achieving -2.73. That play ends with an ordinary successful tackle of **Y**,
not H. It is an opposing football ending, not evidence that H's technique was
successful. In Quarters Drive R2-C06 the nickel requests vx -3.62 at 1.20
with an acquired H vertical at depth 1.43, then transitions to wall H. H later
declares under; we must preserve that legitimate offensive change of direction.

Source mechanism: `seamCurlFlat`/`wallTwo` defer their direct relationship to
installed depth thresholds. The underneath `ZoneCoverage` fallback favors a
QB-to-receiver lane, with its own later vertical-carry threshold. The branch
boundaries leave a period where the defender already recognizes climbing
motion but still asks to close downhill. Not every downhill read step is
wrong. The discriminating question is whether that request unnecessarily
consumes the ability to protect the acquired stem and respond to its break.

The smallest next behavior experiment should preserve useful release cushion
through this boundary using existing observed motion and movement machinery.
It must compare achieved position **and velocity**, not merely earlier man
labels. No private route, assumed future vertical or fixed persistence timer.

## Exchanges cannot erase earlier positioning

At 2.60 R2-C02's curl-flat carry ends after the acquired H depth passes twelve
yards. The nickel consumes no controller-phase helper picture at this exit.
The diagnostic post-step FS20 body is only 1.8 yards farther upfield than H
but **8.66 yards laterally away**. That actual position is diagnostic truth,
not information the nickel is allowed to acquire by reading this receipt.
Any improved handoff would need a legitimate local picture or an explicitly
uncertain installed expectation.

However, the nickel is already six yards behind. Extending the carry cannot
be presumed to correct the initiating problem. The catch at 3.75 has no
physical contact until 8.00; this interval cannot establish grip failure.
The unchanged recorded primary approach through that eventual grasp also
separates #97's later execution change from a coverage improvement.

Drive presents a different distribution problem. R1-C06's nickel confirms
under at 1.65 with an interpreted H picture 2.69 yards away; the hook takes H
at 1.70 and retains him until catch. R2-C06's nickel confirms under at 1.90
with a picture only 0.73 yards away. The hook takes H, then takes the back at
2.15 on an acquired vertical declaration at depth -2.52. The back is genuinely
releasing. Ignoring him permanently would exchange one coverage problem for
another. Fresh F2 repeats this sequence and ends incomplete.

The correctly occupied apex is not a free shallow defender: Y's dig begins
with a credible vertical stem. #97's Drive YAC costs, 3.15->6.92 and
1.57->10.03, remain adverse evidence. Both finish in tackles. A rational
coverage may concede a shallow completion rather than surrender the dig;
the question is whether the defense preserves reasonable leverage and rally.

## Leverage, anticipation and rally must agree

Useful shading denies an access or channels it toward an expected defender.
That expectation should come from the call plus fallible observations. A
post safety moving away is different from one maintaining depth over the
route. A deep safety may cap a vertical without being able to tackle the
shallow. A defender should not treat proximity as ownership, or assume his
own inside/outside shoulder remains optimal after a teammate is beaten.

Current `ManCoverage` derives a roughly fixed shoulder offset from the
installed option and the receiver's side of the ball. `hasProjectedCap` uses
a short age/read projection with depth/lateral bounds. These are useful
simplifications, but neither is a full help-relative leverage policy. Do not
remove the cap's distance bound or continuously flip man leverage without a
bounded cue: earlier archived roof audits retain useful-help and departing-help
counterexamples, and are historical evidence only.

After catch, `chase`/`pursuitPoint` preserve some installed force leverage and
use attainable interception/arrival machinery. They do not explicitly decide
which observed helper is closing which escape lane. A future primary tackler
and cap/containment policy belongs in a separate experiment coordinated with
Flex/Run, not folded into this release or exchange correction.

R1-C08's exact unchanged 63.34-YAC touchdown includes a broken SS grasp; other
defenders are nearby but not all are ahead. That does not prove their support
choice was the first error. R2-C11's exact unchanged 21.93-YAC Smash has no
broken hold: FS gets within 0.79 yards at 5.25 but physical contact begins only
at 6.50. Distance alone cannot decide reach legality, perception or gather
quality. Those require their own contact/request evidence, not stronger grips.
R2-C03's exact 0.39-YAC wrap and the interception in R1-C04 remain controls;
fresh F4's exact 51.85-YAC play-action gain remains an earned/adverse outcome.

## Information boundaries before stronger anticipation

`ZoneCoverage` uses `sim.qbEyes` both to select threat weights and to move
toward the named receiver's live position. QB code assigns that field from
its current read/look-off; it is not a defender-owned acquired gaze cue.
`PlaySim.see` returns teammates from a 0.05-second history sample on demand.
Opponent keys receive longer delays, but querying a non-key still returns a
picture rather than unavailable information. These are not repaired by #97.

Preserve the useful bounded route-motion interpretation, confirmation and
recognition/support corrections. For future anticipation, prefer a small
role-specific key set, last-seen evidence/age and uncertain installed help.
Repeated requests or recording must not create new sightings. Unknown help
must remain unknown. The earlier QB torso-proxy trials were rejected; replacing
the shortcut with an unvalidated body-facing signal is not an established fix.

No online learning, detailed head simulation or central all-seeing assignment
ledger is needed. A small local contract can express primary duty, current
key, permitted exchange, expected help, observation age and fallback. Add only
the field needed by a tested consumer. Genuine short-route exchanges may be
retained while provisional block evidence expires or is contradicted; avoid
globally clearing every locked branch and creating assignment churn.
